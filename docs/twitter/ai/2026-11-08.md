# Twitter AI - 2026-11-08

## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 Benchmarks are testing complete work and their own scoring (🡕)

Several high-signal posts focused on whether benchmarks measure a finished task correctly, rather than only partial rubric scores or familiar tests. Alibaba’s [Co-Design Bench paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35138) draws 273 coding tasks from 26,314 real user sessions and uses 2,331 hidden acceptance criteria. Under its shared harness, the best of ten model endpoints passed 69.9% of criteria on average but fully satisfied only 26.4% of tasks. [@Tesla_Optimus_K](https://x.com/Tesla_Optimus_K) summarized the result in a [post](https://x.com/Tesla_Optimus_K/status/2119468133677510808) (1 like, 3 replies, 53 views).

The [MaxxBench authors](https://maxxbench.unlap.ai/research/why-maxxbench/) changed instructions or starting code in 89 DeepSWE tasks. They report that Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 3.8 Flash, each near 74% on the cited DeepSWE baseline, scored 76.4% and 53.4% on the mutated tasks. [@Austen](https://x.com/Austen) included task mutation in a [builder roundup](https://x.com/Austen/status/2119403781725024675) (27 likes, 2 reposts, 36 replies, 3 quotes, 3,205 views).

An [Amazon and Iowa State audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07042) examined nine policy-decision benchmarks. Correcting a scorer raised acceptance by more than 80 percentage points for each of three models on 96 LegalBench SARA numeric cases; correcting a required zero floor changed 27 of 300 RuleArena Tax references. [@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai) highlighted the issue in a [post](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2119541630361554965) (12 likes, 10 replies, 1,534 views).

![Paper figure showing a RuleArena Tax answer changing from rejected to accepted after correcting the zero-floor rule](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HWoftaFawAAYGTD.jpg)

TCSExam applies Lean-kernel or executable checks to 100 expert-reviewed tasks across theorem proving, solver engineering, constructions, and open conjectures. [The paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07962) reports that GPT-6 Astra led its full benchmark index, while [@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai) noted that no open conjecture was fully resolved in the 30-task track in a [post](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2119501674465439786) (49 likes, 7 reposts, 16 replies, 3,347 views). The paper also reports independently verified special cases and bounds, so zero complete resolutions does not mean zero progress.

![TCSExam paper cover showing its four tracks and benchmark index](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HWn6ttkaAAAPHim.jpg)

**Discussion insight:** These results use different task definitions. Co-Design Bench measures completion of compound application edits, while TCSExam distinguishes full proof or refutation from partial verified results; their percentages are not directly comparable.

**Comparison to prior day:** Nov. 7’s discussion included a coding-agent harness leaderboard ([post](https://x.com/fofanah_denis/status/2118844576026800389); 4 replies, 66 views). Nov. 8 added evidence about mutated tasks and benchmark answer-key or scorer errors.

### 1.2 Agent improvement is being framed as an iterative training problem (🡕)

Sentient Labs’ [EvoSkill v2 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25931) describes a benchmark adaptor, solver, and stateful evolver that updates an agent’s skills from training attempts while leaving its model and harness unchanged. It reports held-out gains of 7.7 percentage points on SpreadsheetBench v2 and 5.4 points on τ3-bench banking over the stronger comparison baseline. [@slatro_eth](https://x.com/slatro_eth) summarized the method in a [post](https://x.com/slatro_eth/status/2119402062202945797) (36 likes, 7 replies, 574 views).

Terminal-RSI instead combines verified-trajectory rewriting with policy refinement during multi-stage training. Its [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22192) reports Qwen3.8-27B results of 19.84% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, 86.52% on 2.1, 16.95% on 3.0, and 79.00% on Terminal-Bench Hard. The paper describes a training pipeline, not an agent autonomously rewriting itself at runtime; [@Dr_Singularity](https://x.com/Dr_Singularity) introduced it in a [post](https://x.com/Dr_Singularity/status/2119466921582100586) (119 likes, 18 reposts, 15 replies, 3,578 views).

![Terminal-RSI paper figure showing its staged training approach and benchmark results](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HWnbpQuWkAAXe-3.jpg)

### 1.3 Local-first tools pair model choice with hardware-specific tradeoffs (🡒)

An update to llama.cpp, [PR #30966](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/30966), adds CPU execution for missed Mixture-of-Experts cache entries and a tuning script. Its contributor reports 24.2 to 47.6 tokens per second at a 6,464 MiB cache when CPU share rises from 0 to 0.95 in one test setup; the result is configuration-specific. [@TeksEdge](https://x.com/TeksEdge) covered the change in a [post](https://x.com/TeksEdge/status/2119219599799669073) (54 likes, 5 reposts, 6 replies, 2,939 views, 59 bookmarks).

![Merged llama.cpp pull request showing CPU fallback for MoE cache misses and example throughput measurements](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HWjwNHJbAAAR3yz.jpg)

One daily Hermes Agent user says the model-neutral setup preserves memory and skills while switching among GLM 5.3, GPT Sol 6.1, and Fable. The reviewer also reports that 94.4% of token usage across 1,013 sessions was cached; this is a personal account, not an independent benchmark. [@Da7_Tech](https://x.com/Da7_Tech) shared the [review](https://x.com/Da7_Tech/status/2119460293398937811) (159 likes, 15 reposts, 28 replies, 7,226 views, 102 bookmarks). The [Nous Research repository](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent) documents its model providers, memory and skills, and local or remote execution backends.

Two other posts described local products: [Vane](https://github.com/BunsDev/perplexica-search-engine-ai), which the poster called a Perplexity clone using Ollama and sourced web answers, and [TandemLLM](https://tandemllm.0xbakeer.com/), a local-model app whose site identifies DGX Spark as its validated hardware target. ## 2. What Frustrates People

### Partial completion is easy to mistake for success

**Severity: High.** Co-Design Bench reports that the strongest of ten endpoints met 69.9% of task criteria on average but fully completed only 26.4% of tasks. MaxxBench also shows that models with similar scores on a familiar benchmark can diverge after small task changes. These are benchmark-specific findings, not a population-wide failure rate, but they point to a clear risk when partial checks stand in for usable deliverables. **Worth building for: High**, especially regression tests that verify the complete requested outcome. ([Co-Design Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35138); [MaxxBench](https://maxxbench.unlap.ai/research/why-maxxbench/))

### Benchmark references and scorers can reject correct decisions

**Severity: High for benchmark validity.** The nine-benchmark policy audit found unsupported targets, incorrect references, and evaluators that reject acceptable answers or accept incomplete ones. Correcting one scorer changed acceptance by more than 80 percentage points on 96 LegalBench SARA numeric cases for each of three models. The audit is substantial evidence of specific defects, but it does not establish how common such defects are across all AI benchmarks. **Worth building for: High** in high-stakes or policy-based evaluation. ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07042); [@rohanpaul_ai’s post](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2119541630361554965) (12 likes, 10 replies, 1,534 views))

### Agent convenience can expose sensitive data

**Severity: High.** Apiiro’s investigation, reported by The Hacker News, identified secrets in 1,122 of 104,185 public GitHub copies of coding-agent settings files. The researchers did not establish that every exposed credential remained active. [@Austen](https://x.com/Austen) included the finding in a [builder roundup](https://x.com/Austen/status/2119403781725024675) (27 likes, 2 reposts, 36 replies, 3 quotes, 3,205 views); the report describes approved commands being saved into project settings that later became public. **Worth building for: High**, with defensive secret scanning, least-privilege approvals, and clearer user warnings. ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/claude-codes-dont-ask-again-file.html))

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### Evaluation for non-coding business workflows

A practitioner says their company writes its own evaluations because public benchmarks for operations, research, and administration are thin compared with coding-agent tests. The practical need is a way to test complete business workflows with configurable tools and deliverable-level checks. This is a direct first-person signal, though the post has limited reach. **Opportunity: Direct.** [@ntkris](https://x.com/ntkris) described the gap in a [post](https://x.com/ntkris/status/2119325339650253175) (1 like, 8 replies, 103 views).

### Safer agent permissions and extension trust

One builder explicitly invited others to create an AI security watchdog for leaked secrets and risky dependencies, or a trust layer showing who audited an extension and what it can access. The security finding in section 2 provides a concrete problem context, but this post is a list of startup ideas rather than evidence of customer demand. **Opportunity: Competitive.** [@BoboLinNotes](https://x.com/BoboLinNotes) listed the ideas in a [post](https://x.com/BoboLinNotes/status/2119506741033570678) (3 likes, 7 replies, 213 views).

### Consumer discovery of useful agent tasks

One user asks for an in-app feed of user-generated prompts or requests ranked by how often others use them, arguing that consumers often do not know what agents can do. The post points to existing “Ideas” features as partial precedents, but does not show adoption data. **Opportunity: Aspirational.** [@thebryanjun](https://x.com/thebryanjun) made the suggestion in a [post](https://x.com/thebryanjun/status/2119407548243509424) (5 likes, 4 replies, 340 views).

## 7. Where the Opportunities Are

**[+++] End-to-end agent evaluation and benchmark auditing** — Co-Design Bench shows a gap between criteria passed and tasks completed; MaxxBench measures adaptation after task changes; and the policy-benchmark audit shows scorer corrections can radically change acceptance. A practitioner also reports writing custom evaluations because public tests are thin for business workflows. ([Co-Design Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35138), [MaxxBench](https://maxxbench.unlap.ai/research/why-maxxbench/), [benchmark audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07042), [@ntkris](https://x.com/ntkris/status/2119325339650253175) (1 like, 8 replies, 103 views))

The measured llama.cpp gain is setup-specific, and TandemLLM identifies a primary hardware target, so reliable configuration and transparent performance limits remain important. ([llama.cpp PR](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/30966), [Vane](https://github.com/BunsDev/perplexica-search-engine-ai), [TandemLLM](https://tandemllm.0xbakeer.com/))

## 8. Takeaways

1. **Partial rubric scores can obscure task failure.** Co-Design Bench reports 69.9% of criteria passed on average but only 26.4% of tasks fully satisfied. ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35138))
2. **Benchmark validity can materially change model scores.** The policy-benchmark audit reports scorer corrections of more than 80 percentage points on a 96-case set and 27 corrected references in RuleArena Tax. ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07042))
3. **Agent improvement methods are becoming more explicit and testable.** EvoSkill v2 edits skills against held-out tasks; Terminal-RSI reports staged training results on terminal benchmarks. Both are author-reported research results, not evidence of general-purpose runtime self-improvement. ([EvoSkill v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25931), [Terminal-RSI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22192))
4. **Local-inference performance depends on the hardware and configuration.** The llama.cpp contributor’s 24.2-to-47.6 tokens/second result is tied to one cache size and CPU-share setting. ([PR #30966](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/30966))
5. **Persistent memory and model choice are valued by users, but anecdotes are not benchmarks.** One Hermes reviewer reports high cache use across 1,013 sessions and describes switching models while retaining skills and memory. ([review](https://x.com/Da7_Tech/status/2119460293398937811); 159 likes, 15 reposts, 28 replies, 7,226 views)
