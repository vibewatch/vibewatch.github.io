## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 A federal alert flags misuse of an agentic cyber platform 🡕

The FBI's Cyber Division said malicious actors had used ARTEX, an LLM-driven agentic platform, in campaigns against organizations across multiple sectors. The agency's post points organizations to a FLASH with mitigation guidance. This is one official warning in the day's feed, not a prevalence estimate; this report does not reproduce operational tradecraft or indicators.

[@FBICyberBranch](https://x.com/FBICyberBranch) [announced](https://x.com/FBICyberBranch/status/2121317398305186287) (421 likes, 15 replies, 27,230 views, 176 bookmarks) the advisory and characterized the system as reducing the time needed for multi-step intrusions. The attached image is only the advisory cover and adds no evidence beyond the post.

**Comparison to prior day:** Nov. 12's AI report centered on workplace evaluation and model claims; today's clearest new signal is an official warning about agent-enabled abuse.

### 1.2 Agent infrastructure costs are being compared at workload scale 🡕

Rivet's OpenOrange team compared S3 request costs for its storage engine and celld under a simulated agent workload. The authors' chart reports about $9,007 versus $259 per month at 10,000 concurrent agents, and $22,654 versus $457 at 25,000. The chart says each simulated agent lives for 60 seconds and writes to SQLite every 10 seconds; it measures S3 request cost, not hardware or total operating cost.

[@NathanFlurry](https://x.com/NathanFlurry) [shared](https://x.com/NathanFlurry/status/2121320866646782293) (134 likes, 17 replies, 4,177 views, 66 bookmarks) the 34.8x comparison and linked the [OpenOrange repository](https://github.com/rivet-dev/open-orange), which describes a runtime for running Cloudflare Workers and Durable Objects on other infrastructure.

![OpenOrange and celld S3 request-cost comparison at simulated agent concurrency](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HXBxltPbUAAVNVR.jpg)

**Comparison to prior day:** The Nov. 12 benchmark measured task success across models; this post instead puts a specific infrastructure cost component under a workload-shaped comparison.

### 1.3 Evaluation discussion favors failure analysis and relevant data over one score →

One practitioner recommends labeling production failures before choosing metrics, maintaining versioned golden datasets, and using deterministic checks where possible. Separately, a research summary says a large single-cell foundation-model study found no clear language-model-like scaling law from simply adding more of the same data.

[@divaagurlxw](https://x.com/divaagurlxw) [outlined](https://x.com/divaagurlxw/status/2121210987130790275) (67 likes, 23 replies, 1,773 views, 45 bookmarks) an evaluation workflow beginning with error categories and distinguishing retrieval and deterministic assertions from model-based grading. A reply notes that rare failures can disappear inside averages.

[@curiouswavefn](https://x.com/curiouswavefn) [summarized](https://x.com/curiouswavefn/status/2121325246536183933) (40 likes, 2 replies, 1,935 views, 21 bookmarks) a paper that, according to the post, pretrained 400 models on 22.2 million cells and ran 6,400 evaluations. The post's takeaway is that biological coverage and relevant endpoints matter, not only adding more similar records.

**Comparison to prior day:** Nov. 12 highlighted an end-to-end file-and-web benchmark; today's posts add practical guidance for diagnosing failures and a domain-specific caution about data scaling.

### 1.4 Local inference claims pair model size with hardware-specific results →

A model release claims that a 145B-parameter model can run locally on an RTX 5090 D with 24 GB of VRAM. The author and model card report 98.3 tokens per second for agent decoding, 79.6 for chat decoding, and 3,165.7 for 16K-token prefill with NVFP4; the model card also reports 23.25 GiB of VRAM and 38.27 GiB of host RAM. These are publisher-reported results, not an independent comparison.

[@TeksEdge](https://x.com/TeksEdge) [summarized](https://x.com/TeksEdge/status/2121324972266406008) (41 likes, 7 replies, 2,625 views, 50 bookmarks) Edge0-145B and noted that the weights use an Edge0 Community Model License, distinct from the inference code's Apache 2.0 license. The [model card](https://huggingface.co/Edge0/Edge0-145B-NVFP4) provides hardware and build details.

![Edge0 model card throughput tables for 145B and 120B builds across consumer hardware](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HXBoj4hasAAEiBS.jpg)

**Comparison to prior day:** Nov. 12 included an unnamed 1.7B-model workflow claim; today's post adds a hardware-specific 145B local-inference claim, with both remaining publisher-reported.

The evaluation post recommends labeling failures from production traces, versioning a representative dataset, and using deterministic checks before model-based grading. A reply specifically warns that rare cases can disappear in averages.
