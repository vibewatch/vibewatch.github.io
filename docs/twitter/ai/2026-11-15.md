## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 Benchmark saturation is becoming a measurement problem 🡕

SatBench counts a benchmark as saturated when the best recorded score reaches 85%. Its author says 109 of 173 widely cited benchmarks met that threshold by November 2026 and shows saturation arriving faster for newer benchmark cohorts. The 2028/2030 curve is explicitly an extrapolation, not a forecast.

[@emollick](https://x.com/emollick) [introduced](https://x.com/emollick/status/2121751565845360769) (253 likes, 83 replies, 17,028 views, 72 bookmarks) the analysis and reported that private benchmarks also start harder but can fall quickly. The four charts show the threshold, time-to-saturation by release year, a scenario projection, and a count by lab; these are the author's analysis of cited benchmark results.

### 1.2 Practitioners question benchmark trust and recommend private evaluations →

In an informal poll of 56 AI practitioners, Terminal-Bench 4 received the most votes as a frontier signal, but the author says the most common response was that people do not really trust benchmarks. The sample is small and not representative.

[@chrisbarber](https://x.com/chrisbarber) [summarized](https://x.com/chrisbarber/status/2122020768523714827) (34 likes, 6 replies, 1,848 views, 18 bookmarks) the poll. Separately, [@pggalaviz](https://x.com/pggalaviz) [recommended](https://x.com/pggalaviz/status/2122052365599592582) (13 likes, 7 replies, 1,029 views, 13 bookmarks) testing new models on recent tasks from one's own work, using the same harness and effort, and tracking time and cost with pass rate.

**Discussion insight:** The poll discloses investments in some listed companies; the private-eval post frames its steps as practical advice, not a measured result.

**Comparison to prior day:** The earlier Fable comparison surfaced the ranking question; these posts shift toward evaluating models against private, task-specific work.

### 1.3 Local-model packaging advances faster than comparative evidence →

A community Qwen derivative is described as expanding a 27B model to about 40B parameters, with a 4-bit build listed at 21.8 GB. The post itself says it has not seen independent evidence that the larger derivative outperforms the original.

[@TeksEdge](https://x.com/TeksEdge) [described](https://x.com/TeksEdge/status/2122057050431824292) (51 likes, 8 replies, 3,829 views, 58 bookmarks) the model variants and memory sizes. The feed provides packaging details, not a controlled quality comparison.

**Comparison to prior day:** Nov. 14's strongest model signal was a benchmark score; today's local-model post puts quantization and memory requirements beside an explicit evaluation gap.

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Benchmark results lose value when tests saturate

**Severity: Medium for model selection and tracking progress.** SatBench counts 109 of 173 cited benchmarks as reaching its 85% threshold, and the informal poll reports that distrust was the most common response among 56 practitioners. These are one author's benchmark analysis and a small poll, not proof that every benchmark is unusable. ([@emollick](https://x.com/emollick/status/2121751565845360769) (253 likes, 83 replies, 17,028 views, 72 bookmarks); [@chrisbarber](https://x.com/chrisbarber/status/2122020768523714827) (34 likes, 6 replies, 1,848 views, 18 bookmarks))

### Comparing models on public scores may not answer a user's question

**Severity: Medium for teams with domain-specific tasks.** A practitioner recommends using recent tasks from one's own work, the same harness, and cost and time tracking because public benchmarks do not show performance on that workload. This is practical advice, not a measured estimate of how many users face the problem. ([@pggalaviz](https://x.com/pggalaviz/status/2122052365599592582) (13 likes, 7 replies, 1,029 views, 13 bookmarks))

**Workaround observed:** Build a small private evaluation with explicit expected outcomes and rerun it when model behavior or pricing changes.

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### Benchmarks that stay informative as models improve

SatBench's 85% saturation threshold and the poll's mixed trust signal point to a need for tests that are refreshed, difficult to game, and explicit about their scope. The poll is only 56 practitioners, and the SatBench analysis is one author's method. **Urgency: Medium. Opportunity: Direct.** ([@emollick](https://x.com/emollick/status/2121751565845360769) (253 likes, 83 replies, 17,028 views, 72 bookmarks); [@chrisbarber](https://x.com/chrisbarber/status/2122020768523714827) (34 likes, 6 replies, 1,848 views, 18 bookmarks))

### Private evaluations that compare models under equal conditions

The suggested workflow uses recent work tasks, defined expected outcomes, the same harness and effort, and cost and time tracking. It is an accessible starting point, but still requires users to curate representative tasks. **Urgency: Medium. Opportunity: Competitive.** ([@pggalaviz](https://x.com/pggalaviz/status/2122052365599592582) (13 likes, 7 replies, 1,029 views, 13 bookmarks))

### Independent evidence for local-model variants

The community Qwen derivative post lists parameter and quantized-size changes but explicitly lacks independent comparisons against the original model. Reproducible quality, speed, and memory results would make local deployment choices clearer. **Urgency: Low. Opportunity: Emerging.** ([@TeksEdge](https://x.com/TeksEdge/status/2122057050431824292) (51 likes, 8 replies, 3,829 views, 58 bookmarks))

## 7. Where the Opportunities Are

**[+++] Private, task-relevant evaluations** — SatBench's author reports that many tracked benchmarks have crossed a stated saturation threshold, while an informal poll records widespread skepticism and a practitioner proposes comparing models on the same recent tasks, harness, and effort. Together these point to demand for transparent, regularly refreshed evaluations tied to users' own work. ([SatBench](https://x.com/emollick/status/2121751565845360769), [practitioner workflow](https://x.com/pggalaviz/status/2122052365599592582))

**[++] Comparable cost and time reporting** — The proposed private evaluation explicitly tracks cost and time beside pass rate; the Qwen derivative post lists local memory requirements but provides no independent performance comparison. Reporting the conditions and cost per task would make these different options easier to evaluate. ([evaluation advice](https://x.com/pggalaviz/status/2122052365599592582), [Qwen derivative](https://x.com/TeksEdge/status/2122057050431824292))

**[+] Independent local-model comparisons** — A community derivative is presented with quantization and memory details, while its author says independent evidence of outperformance was not available. The evidence supports an evaluation gap, not a claim that the derivative is better. ([post](https://x.com/TeksEdge/status/2122057050431824292))

## 8. Takeaways

1. **Benchmark freshness is a live concern.** SatBench reports 109 of 173 tracked benchmarks at its stated 85% threshold and labels its future curve an extrapolation. ([SatBench post](https://x.com/emollick/status/2121751565845360769))
2. **Practitioners recommend evaluating their own tasks.** One workflow calls for a small recent task set, a shared harness, and tracking cost and time alongside pass rate. ([evaluation advice](https://x.com/pggalaviz/status/2122052365599592582))
3. **Local-model size is not evidence of quality.** The Qwen derivative post lists a 4-bit build size but says independent evidence of better performance was unavailable. ([post](https://x.com/TeksEdge/status/2122057050431824292))
