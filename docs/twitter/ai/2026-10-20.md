# Twitter AI - 2026-10-20

## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 Calibrating model evaluations 🡕

Two posts focus on how model evaluations are designed. [@HamelHusain](https://x.com/HamelHusain) [shared](https://x.com/HamelHusain/status/2112588257104216294) (267 likes, 24 replies, 10,293 views) a guide to choosing a decision model and tuning its threshold rather than assuming 0.5 is appropriate. A reply from [@petrkubelkaa](https://x.com/petrkubelkaa) [suggested](https://x.com/petrkubelkaa/status/2112601613844074927) (1 reply, 40 views) a small classifier for narrow decisions; Hamel agreed that this is reasonable if drift is measured.

### 1.2 Health model scores depend on the rubric 🡒

[@ArtificialAnlys](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys) [introduced](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2112318755321868493) (111 likes, 19 replies, 11,454 views) leaderboards for HealthBench and HealthBench Professional. The post describes 5,000 everyday health conversations and 525 clinician conversations; length adjustment materially changes rankings. For example, Claude Opus 5.5 falls from a 71.4% raw score to 59.9% after adjustment on HealthBench Professional, while the chart shows GPT-6 Astra at 66.0% adjusted.

![Artificial Analysis charts comparing raw health benchmark rankings after response-length adjustment](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HVB2dc-a0AAKEK4.jpg)

### 1.3 Cyber-risk evaluation is also being discussed 🡒

[@ValsAI](https://x.com/ValsAI) [described](https://x.com/ValsAI/status/2112471082397778083) (148 likes, 14 replies, 10,404 views) a simulated corporate-network evaluation intended to measure cybersecurity risk from AI models. The post says the full evaluation would be published later; a follow-up explains that the highlighted systems were run multiple times while other comparisons used single runs. No detailed task results are needed to see the day’s broader focus on evaluation design.

**Comparison to prior day:** Yesterday’s concern was whether agent skills had meaningful pass/fail tests; today’s posts move to task-specific decision thresholds, benchmark scoring rules, and evaluation methodology. [@sermakarevich](https://x.com/sermakarevich) [raised](https://x.com/sermakarevich/status/2112198653469241856) (46 likes, 11 replies, 2,653 views) the skill-testing question.

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Benchmark scores can obscure task fit

The HealthBench post shows that answer-length adjustments change model rankings, and the decision-model guide warns against using a default threshold without tuning. These are measurable evaluation issues, not evidence that every benchmark is unreliable. The cybersecurity evaluation post also notes that some comparisons use more repeated runs than others.

---

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### Better-calibrated, task-specific evaluation

The evaluation posts point to practical needs: thresholds selected for the task, transparent scoring rules, and enough repeat runs to interpret results. The reply on small classifiers adds a concrete alternative for narrow decisions, provided drift is measured. [@HamelHusain](https://x.com/HamelHusain) [shared](https://x.com/HamelHusain/status/2112588257104216294) (267 likes, 24 replies, 10,293 views) the threshold-tuning guide, and [@petrkubelkaa](https://x.com/petrkubelkaa) [suggested](https://x.com/petrkubelkaa/status/2112601613844074927) (1 reply, 40 views) a small classifier for narrow decisions. Opportunity: direct, though the evidence is limited to evaluation-focused posts.
