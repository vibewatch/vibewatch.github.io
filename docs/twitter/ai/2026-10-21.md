### 1.2 Multi-agent systems are being tested in health and science 🡕

[@EricTopol](https://x.com/EricTopol) [highlighted](https://x.com/EricTopol/status/2112935031329161534) (147 likes, 13 replies, 15,911 views) a Nature paper on a personal health agent that outperformed a chatbot in the reported evaluation. The paper’s abstract describes three specialist agents and an open-source framework, tested across 11 benchmark tasks with more than 7,000 annotations and 1,350 hours of expert and end-user effort. Topol’s reply says an outcome-focused randomized trial is the next major step.

[@ModelScope2022](https://x.com/ModelScope2022) [released](https://x.com/ModelScope2022/status/2112816255375745132) (72 likes, 5 replies, 3,542 views) Sage-A1.5 for long-horizon scientific workflows, citing a 256K context window, parallel research, evidence checks, and science benchmark scores. Its chart compares results across science and coding tasks.

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Health-agent outcomes still need validation

The Nature paper reports benchmark evaluation, but not improved patient outcomes. Topol’s reply explicitly identifies a randomized controlled trial as the next step. This is a validation gap in the reported research, not a claim that the system failed.
