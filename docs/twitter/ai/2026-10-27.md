### 1.1 Agents move from chat into design work 🡕

### 1.2 Long-term memory is being judged by control, not just recall 🡕

[@sermakarevich](https://x.com/sermakarevich) [summarized](https://x.com/sermakarevich/status/2115087554877776113) (70 likes, 32 replies, 3,423 views, 79 bookmarks) a review of 30 agent-memory products covering what they store, retrieval, changed facts, and extraction-token costs. Replies focus on whether a user can easily delete or correct a wrong memory.

**Discussion insight:** The usefulness of persistent memory depends on maintenance and correction paths, not only on retrieving old context.

### 1.3 Specialist models target document parsing and on-device vision 🡕

[@jerryjliu0](https://x.com/jerryjliu0) [reported](https://x.com/jerryjliu0/status/2114856267118494178) (64 likes, 14 replies, 2,908 views, 46 bookmarks) OCRia-1 results on dense PDF tables, including 92.9% on the table subset of olmOCR-bench and 84.1% on PulseBench-Tab. These are publisher-reported benchmark results; the post directs users to LlamaParse. Separately, [@kimiatehrani](https://x.com/kimiatehrani) [highlighted](https://x.com/kimiatehrani/status/2115185131241517383) (64 likes, 21 replies, 3,678 views) Liquid AI's LFM3-VL-2B claims: video input, on-device use, and an average 71.4 across 62 vision benchmarks.

**Comparison to prior day:** October 26 emphasized open translation and enterprise knowledge layers. Today's posts move toward focused workflows: design agents, editable memory, and specialist vision/document models.

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Keeping agent memory accurate

The review of 30 memory products explicitly considers changed facts and extraction-token costs. Replies identify the difficulty of deleting a wrong fact or correcting a claim without rebuilding embeddings. This is a concrete maintenance concern, but the thread does not establish how common it is.
