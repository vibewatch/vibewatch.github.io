# Twitter AI Agent - 2026-11-19

## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 The harness, not only the model, is becoming the optimization target ↑

Several research and product posts put the runtime around a model in focus: tools, context, permissions, verification, memory, and feedback. A widely shared clip attributed to Karpathy argues that a smaller model plus tools and feedback can outperform a larger model; a reply asks for benchmark evidence, so the clip's model-size comparison remains a claim rather than a demonstrated result. [@Yarilo7brigada](https://x.com/Yarilo7brigada) [summarized](https://x.com/Yarilo7brigada/status/2123129806015652182) (737 likes, 74 retweets, 27 replies, 71,648 views, 1,506 bookmarks).

[@0xSynapz](https://x.com/0xSynapz) [published](https://x.com/0xSynapz/status/2123151831899517178) (10 likes, 13 replies, 101 views, 10 bookmarks) an independent graphic that synthesizes Anthropic engineering posts and Claude Code docs into six harness layers: tools, context, permissions, hooks, verification, and evaluations. The post explicitly says this is an independent summary, not an Anthropic publication.

![Independent six-layer harness map covering tools, context, permissions, hooks, verification, and evaluations](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HXbzT2cWUAAHL0R.jpg)

Inferact's Simon Mo said the company will build an autonomous inference system around vLLM that improves the engine and itself. A reply asked how upstream changes would avoid slowing some workloads; Mo answered that changes would stay opt-in to prevent regressions. [@simon_mo_](https://x.com/simon_mo_) [described](https://x.com/simon_mo_/status/2123133742063706343) (483 likes, 61 retweets, 24 replies, 39,052 views, 421 bookmarks) the plan.

The research evidence is more specific about bounded self-updates. Safe Personal RSI keeps model weights and runtime code fixed, tests instruction edits against 29 executable cases, and rejects 20 of 21 risky updates; the paper reports one admitted repair lowering attack violations from 10.7% to 0% while raising safe task completion from 89.7% to 100%. [@dair_ai](https://x.com/dair_ai) [summarized](https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2123511637584036078) (28 likes, 4 retweets, 8 replies, 2,496 views, 34 bookmarks) the [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23651).

![Safe Personal RSI report page describing its 29-case update gate and 20-of-21 risky edits rejected](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HXg6lJmaIAAHM8R.jpg)

Google's GUARD preprint uses self-checks and regret guidance to accept harness edits only when gains outweigh regressions. The post reports 80.80% accuracy on 43 hosted SDK tasks, 8.89 points above the starting harness, and 54.5% fewer tokens per task. [@omarsar0](https://x.com/omarsar0) [summarized](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2123420538030957015) (8 likes, 2 retweets, 1 reply, 880 views, 8 bookmarks) the [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23476).

![GUARD preprint figure comparing harness performance across continual task blocks](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HXfnubNaUAAUbAH.jpg)

The Self-Improvement Memory paper addresses a related portability problem: an optimized harness may transfer poorly when the underlying model changes. Its proposed memory records why each edit was made and whether it worked; the study reports an average 2.36-point gain over harness-only continuation across ten coding runs, while noting that cross-model transfer does not always beat starting fresh. [@Chinazhidx](https://x.com/Chinazhidx) [described](https://x.com/Chinazhidx/status/2123309823060308148) (3 likes, 1 retweet, 289 views, 2 bookmarks) the [study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18856).

![Self-Improvement Memory study figure comparing direct transfer with memory-assisted cross-model continuation](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HXeC7JKbgAAGcAE.jpg)

At the more ambitious end, an arXiv preprint reports that an open 27B model rewrote its own weights, gained 6.6 accuracy points on unseen problems, and restored more than 80% of a damaged copy's accuracy on image tasks. The result is a research claim from one model family, with the authors reporting that smaller models in the family did not improve; it is not evidence that general-purpose agents can safely self-modify. [@FanJiajun67938](https://x.com/FanJiajun67938) [presented](https://x.com/FanJiajun67938/status/2123183386801889776) (4 likes, 3 retweets, 1 quote, 2,270 views, 1 bookmark) the [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21885).

![Self-rewriting paper chart reporting accuracy improvement over eight sessions on 490 unseen problems](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HXcNqzhWMAAC9cz.png)

**Comparison to prior day:** The Karpathy clip and Inferact announcement also led the prior day's feed, so they are continuing discussion threads rather than new releases. The distinct additions here are research on safe harness edits, cross-model memory, and production evaluation.

### 1.2 Evaluations are moving closer to real work ↑

Meta's MLE Agents paper describes agents proposing, implementing, training, and evaluating model changes for production search rankers while engineers retain control of the objective, search direction, and final review. The paper reports 1,097 experiments, 325 accepted changes, and a 0.23% search-volume increase in an A/B test; it also notes that experiments take hours, offline metrics can diverge from online impact, and gains can flatten. [@dair_ai](https://x.com/dair_ai) [summarized](https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2123423809839698400) (29 likes, 6 retweets, 8 replies, 3,015 views, 26 bookmarks) the [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23910).

![Meta MLE Agents paper abstract describing human-on-the-loop search-ranker experiments](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HXfqs48bUAAJEO2.png)

Google Cloud describes AlphaEvolve proposing a coding skill, Harbor running the agent on tasks, comparing the result with a baseline, and checking it on held-out tasks. Google reports a 5% improvement on unseen tasks after a few iterations; the published example is a specific coding-skill experiment, not a general result across agent work. [@GoogleCloudTech](https://x.com/GoogleCloudTech) [reported](https://x.com/GoogleCloudTech/status/2123234809354846702) (5 likes, 1 reply, 1,485 views, 2 bookmarks) the result, with a [methodology post](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/how-to-build-an-agent-skill-optimizer-with-alphaevolve-and-harbor).

![AlphaEvolve and Harbor loop that compares candidate and baseline skills, then checks held-out tasks](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HXbHX0mWMAAPx6_.jpg)

Stripe's public coding-agent evaluation compares 14 model and harness configurations on payments, subscriptions, and Connect integrations. Its post reports a 3-to-8-point gain from Stripe's plugin across tested pairs, but only 29% of runs met every criterion; the benchmark page publishes the [scores and methodology](https://stripe.dev/evals). [@hazelcough](https://x.com/hazelcough) [summarized](https://x.com/hazelcough/status/2123481209963737473) (3 likes, 2 retweets, 1 quote, 86 views) the release.

![Stripe integration benchmark comparing plugin and no-plugin scores across task types](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HXgd4EWbgAAMXpx.jpg)

Bespoke Labs released MAC Bench, a public benchmark of 12 long-horizon tasks. Each compares a single agent, teams of two, four, or eight sharing a message board, and teams without coordination, separating the effect of coordination from simply adding more agents. The tasks and Harbor-based harness are in the [repository](https://github.com/bespokelabsai/multi-agent-coordination-bench). [@bespokelabsai](https://x.com/bespokelabsai) [released](https://x.com/bespokelabsai/status/2123533467644518859) (17 likes, 5 retweets, 2 replies, 580 views, 11 bookmarks) the benchmark.

Tapwright's 1.9.0 release reports a 21-task physical-iPhone benchmark with 31% fewer tokens, 30% fewer model turns, and 26% lower API cost. Its post attributes the improvement to reducing tool-discovery and navigation turns, including loading core tools up front and reusing previously observed routes; these are vendor-reported results from one phone and a defined task set. [@BO5AMIS](https://x.com/BO5AMIS) [reported](https://x.com/BO5AMIS/status/2123529252112924915) (2 replies, 105 views) the update.

![Tapwright 1.9.0 benchmark summary showing fewer tokens, model turns, and cost across 21 tasks](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HXhIZAVawAAah6s.png)

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Real-world latency and safety

The clearest operational failure report came from a CoRL robotics workshop: coding agents spent only 20–36% of task time moving the robot, and one agent waited 48 seconds before requesting a retract after detecting high force. The same account says another agent continued pushing despite a risk cue, and argues for stopping mechanisms that do not wait on a model. Its reported task results are based on three trials per task, so the author cautions against treating the observed success rates as stable model rankings. Worth building for: local, low-latency safety controls and faster simulation-to-real iteration. [@junfanzhu98](https://x.com/junfanzhu98) [reported](https://x.com/junfanzhu98/status/2123234896730792301) (5 likes, 2 retweets, 2 replies, 228 views).

### Brittle workflows and supervision overhead

A practitioner described daily agent work as a grind of prompt rituals, babysitting brittle multi-agent loops, correcting over-engineered answers, and handling intent misses. Replies added a human merge-review bottleneck and hallucinations. This is one detailed first-person account with confirming but limited replies, not a prevalence estimate; it points to demand for systems that reduce supervision and recover cleanly from mistakes. [@tokenbender](https://x.com/tokenbender) [described](https://x.com/tokenbender/status/2123421151666000046) (69 likes, 2 retweets, 10 replies, 2,426 views, 16 bookmarks).

Setup friction also appeared in a reply to Hermes Enterprise: one user said configuring and fixing Hermes took more time than using it and asked for better setup guidance. That reply is a specific usability signal, not broad product feedback. [@NousResearch](https://x.com/NousResearch) [announced](https://x.com/NousResearch/status/2123535954946257096) (440 likes, 55 retweets, 67 replies, 11,132 views, 77 bookmarks) the enterprise deployment.

### Last-mile task completion remains uneven

Stripe's coding-agent evaluation reports that only 29% of its runs met every criterion across the tested integration tasks, despite plugin gains on measured scores. This is a benchmark result, not a user complaint; it shows that a higher average score can coexist with an incomplete end-to-end pass rate. The remaining gap is worth addressing with task-specific checks and explicit completion criteria. [@hazelcough](https://x.com/hazelcough) [summarized](https://x.com/hazelcough/status/2123481209963737473) (3 likes, 2 retweets, 1 quote, 86 views).

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### Approval screens that preserve the work context

The TermiX walkthrough describes a concrete need: before signing an agent-prepared transaction, show the provider, price, and agreed deliverables together, so the human can approve without reconstructing the context from a long thread. This is a practical, direct opportunity around understandable, scoped approvals; the post's replies agree with the trust point but do not independently establish its prevalence. [@0xDvox](https://x.com/0xDvox) [walked through](https://x.com/0xDvox/status/2123483211699564721) (224 likes, 5 retweets, 143 replies, 10,068 views).

### Agents that need less babysitting

One practitioner explicitly wants agent use to feel less like a grind: fewer prompt rituals, brittle loops, and cycles spent correcting intent misses. The post praises a model experience that tolerates incomplete thoughts and mid-conversation changes, but this is an individual preference rather than a broad comparison. Opportunity: make ordinary interaction more forgiving and reduce supervision work. [@tokenbender](https://x.com/tokenbender) [described](https://x.com/tokenbender/status/2123421151666000046) (69 likes, 2 retweets, 10 replies, 2,426 views, 16 bookmarks).

### Harness improvements that survive a model change

A practitioner asks what actually transfers when switching models and argues for recording why each harness change was made and whether it worked, rather than carrying only a prompt or tool configuration. The linked study reports that a memory-assisted harness outperformed a harness-only continuation in coding on 9 of 10 paired runs; its workspace comparison used one run per condition, so the result is promising but limited. Opportunity: direct, for teams maintaining agents across model updates. [@Howard_Pengg](https://x.com/Howard_Pengg) [compared](https://x.com/Howard_Pengg/status/2123219313142485474) (1 like, 3 replies, 193 views).
