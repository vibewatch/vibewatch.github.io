# Twitter AI Agent - 2026-11-17

## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 Long-horizon agent work is being tested with explicit task suites 🡕

Kaggle announced a competition for open-source autonomous harnesses to solve 85 expert-reviewed, long-horizon engineering tasks across structural, civil, electrical, and mechanical work. Its scale and task definition make this a more concrete evaluation target than a short conversational demo, although the competition's results are not yet available.

[@kaggle](https://x.com/kaggle) [announced the Physical-Engineering's Last Exam challenge](https://x.com/kaggle/status/2122725546459070920) (374 likes, 10 replies, 16,395 views, 344 bookmarks). The [competition page](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/physical-engineerings-last-exam) describes autonomous harnesses for real-world engineering tasks; the post lists eight Google-sponsored models, a $400,000 prize pool, and a January 5, 2027 entry deadline.

**Discussion insight:** Replies highlight structural and civil tasks and ask for teammates, but do not report agent performance.

### 1.2 Enterprise "software factories" emphasize isolation, deterministic checks, and human gates 🡕

Islo's launch post describes persistent environments, separate implementation and verification steps, policy checks, and approval pauses for risky actions. The company's site shows its own dashboard reporting 1,053 runs and a 72% success rate over the last seven days; these are vendor-reported figures, not independently audited outcomes.

[@islolabs](https://x.com/islolabs) [announced Islo Enterprise Software Factory](https://x.com/islolabs/status/2122721428856328598) (159 likes, 85 replies, 51,924 views, 46 bookmarks). The post says teams can evaluate model, harness, and prompt changes against historical engineering work before rollout. The [product page](https://islo.dev/) describes persistent machines, scripts, reviewer agents, logs, and human-controlled merging. The launch post also announces $15 million in funding and a Databricks infrastructure partnership.

**Comparison to prior day:** Nov. 16's AI Agent feed focused on self-hosted delivery gates and reusable skills. Today's posts add an enterprise product launch and a public long-horizon evaluation competition.

### 1.3 Agent managers are framed around outcomes, but verification remains open 🡒

An account describing a week-long trial of Devin Bots says each persistent bot can own outcomes, coordinate subagents, and report through interactive artifacts. A reply to the post questions agents evaluating their own work when acceptance criteria are unclear.

[@swyx](https://x.com/swyx) [described the Devin Bots workflow](https://x.com/swyx/status/2122746401482592549) (211 likes, 88 replies, 12,389 views, 122 bookmarks). The post lists AEO, QA, app porting, personal administration, and video editing as trial tasks; it is an individual early-use account, not a measured productivity study.

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### Public, long-horizon evaluations for agent systems

The Physical-Engineering's Last Exam challenge defines expert-reviewed engineering tasks and invites open-source harnesses, but its results are still pending. Comparable task suites could help teams evaluate agents beyond short coding or chat interactions. **Urgency: Medium. Opportunity: Direct.** ([@kaggle](https://x.com/kaggle/status/2122725546459070920) (374 likes, 10 replies, 16,395 views, 344 bookmarks))

### Clear acceptance criteria with independent verification

Islo's product description uses scripts, reviewer agents, and approval gates; a reply to Devin Bots questions whether an agent can reliably grade its own output when the target is unclear. The posts point to a need for explicit success criteria and an independent path to verify results. **Urgency: Medium. Opportunity: Direct.** ([@islolabs](https://x.com/islolabs/status/2122721428856328598) (159 likes, 85 replies, 51,924 views); [@swyx](https://x.com/swyx/status/2122746401482592549) (211 likes, 88 replies, 12,389 views))

### Persistent agents that own outcomes without opaque supervision

Devin Bots is presented as maintaining identity and context across tasks and coordinating subagents. The post suggests an appetite for moving beyond single-task prompts, while leaving open how outcomes are measured and who approves consequential actions. **Urgency: Low to medium. Opportunity: Emerging.** ([@swyx](https://x.com/swyx/status/2122746401482592549) (211 likes, 88 replies, 12,389 views))
