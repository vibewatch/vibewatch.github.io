# Twitter AI Agent - 2026-11-04

## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 Agent harnesses become explicit engineering workflows (🡕)

Posts move beyond prompt chaining toward defined environments, integrations, operations, memory, and coordinated specialist agents. The evidence includes a workshop curriculum, practitioner critique, and a team-reported bug-fixing workflow.

[@GrahamHelton3](https://x.com/GrahamHelton3) [released](https://x.com/GrahamHelton3/status/2118030259329949803) (19 likes, 3 retweets, 2 replies, 1,499 views, 2 bookmarks) materials for an introductory harness-engineering workshop for vulnerability research. A reply groups its lessons as local validation environments, external integrations, operations interfaces, and memory; the linked [workshop repository](https://github.com/GrahamHelton3/harness-engineering-workshop) contains the book source. The materials are a curriculum, not measured security outcomes.

[@zachlloydtweets](https://x.com/zachlloydtweets) [argued](https://x.com/zachlloydtweets/status/2117944698647494678) (258 likes, 28 retweets, 70 replies, 35,906 views, 154 bookmarks) that teams who expect prompt chains to substitute for harnesses will regret it. The highly discussed post frames the gap as a workflow-design problem; it does not offer a benchmark or prescribe a specific architecture.

[@patrickjcampbell](https://x.com/patrickjcampbell) [reported](https://x.com/patrickjcampbell/status/2117928338422786536) (122 likes, 9 retweets, 16 replies, 7,868 views, 37 bookmarks) that an XLA harness-and-swarm setup had found and fixed hundreds of bugs. The author says a lead agent handles deduplication while specialists investigate reports, but the post does not give a denominator or independent validation.

**Comparison to prior day:** Nov. 3 already featured harness evaluation, skills, and security controls. Today's examples add a practical workshop curriculum and another team-reported workflow, alongside products aimed at managing or improving agent runs.

### 1.2 Agent capability catalogs gain product and developer-program surfaces (🡕)

Distribution of reusable capabilities is becoming a product surface, while a separate launch targets learning from repeated agent failures.

[@NousResearch](https://x.com/NousResearch) [launched](https://x.com/NousResearch/status/2118006378690754694) (651 likes, 55 retweets, 70 replies, 60,050 views, 529 bookmarks) Hermes Marketplace for plugins, connectors, and skills. The [live catalog](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/marketplace/) makes discovery and installation concrete; the announcement does not establish extension quality or adoption.

[@NousResearch](https://x.com/NousResearch) [announced](https://x.com/NousResearch/status/2117988208528449824) (307 likes, 32 retweets, 42 replies, 33,768 views, 151 bookmarks) a Nov. 4-11 Hermes Agent hackathon, with six winners sharing $10,000 and judging criteria that include usefulness, reliability, polish, and creativity. It is an organizer's stated program, not evidence of completed projects.

[@oumi_ai](https://x.com/oumi_ai) [introduced](https://x.com/oumi_ai/status/2117992968347181298) (35 likes, 5 retweets, 3 replies, 3,254 views, 7 bookmarks) FAiE, a private-preview system that uses recurring failure patterns from an existing harness to create evals, training data, and fine-tuned models. Its [product page](https://faie.oumi.ai/) describes a build-and-evaluate loop; the training claims have no independent quality results in the cited material.

### 1.3 Agent state and approval waits are part of the user experience (🡒)

A builder's account highlights the time an agent can spend blocked on human approval, even when its activity is visible.

[@0xMfox](https://x.com/0xMfox) [shared](https://x.com/0xMfox/status/2117975491963945269) (14 likes, 2 retweets, 4 replies, 1,490 views, 5 bookmarks) a Claude Code side panel that shows what an agent is doing and said it had spent two hours waiting for approvals. This is a single workflow report, not a measure of how often approval waits occur.

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Approval waits can strand long-running work

**Severity: Medium; prevalence: low-confidence.** One builder said a Claude Code task spent two hours waiting for approval ([post](https://x.com/0xMfox/status/2117975491963945269), 14 likes, 2 retweets, 4 replies, 1,490 views, 5 bookmarks). The side panel makes the wait visible, but the post does not describe automated escalation or resumable approvals. It is worth building for workflows that run asynchronously, though this single account does not establish broad demand.

### Prompt chains are viewed as an inadequate substitute for operational design

**Severity: Low; prevalence: unmeasured.** [@zachlloydtweets](https://x.com/zachlloydtweets) [warned](https://x.com/zachlloydtweets/status/2117944698647494678) (258 likes, 28 retweets, 70 replies, 35,906 views, 154 bookmarks) that teams may regret treating prompt chains as harnesses. The thread signals practitioner concern, but supplies no named failure case or adoption data. The critique supports better workflow design as a concern, not a quantified pain point.

## 3. What People Wish Existed

No broad direct wishlist emerged. The clearest practical need is inferred from one report of a blocked task; other evidence is product supply rather than user demand.

### Approval handoffs that preserve task state

The Pixel Agents creator reports a two-hour approval wait ([post](https://x.com/0xMfox/status/2117975491963945269), 14 likes, 2 retweets, 4 replies, 1,490 views, 5 bookmarks). A visible state, asynchronous approval, and reliable resume point could address that case, but no respondent explicitly asks for a particular feature. **Need type:** Practical. **Opportunity: Direct**, based on one concrete workflow example only.

### Capability catalogs with clear reliability signals

Hermes Marketplace provides a place to find plugins, connectors, and skills, while its hackathon names reliability among its judging criteria ([marketplace announcement](https://x.com/NousResearch/status/2118006378690754694), 651 likes, 55 retweets, 70 replies, 60,050 views, 529 bookmarks; [hackathon announcement](https://x.com/NousResearch/status/2117988208528449824), 307 likes, 32 retweets, 42 replies, 33,768 views, 151 bookmarks). The posts do not show user requests for security or quality ratings, so a catalog with verified compatibility and provenance is an inference, not an expressed wish. **Need type:** Practical but unvalidated. **Opportunity: Aspirational** on this evidence.

## 6. New and Notable

FAiE's private-preview pitch links an existing harness to a recurring-failure pipeline: extract failure patterns, create evaluations and training data, then fine-tune models while retaining the harness. The announcement does not provide independent results showing that the training loop improves task success.

## 7. Where the Opportunities Are

**[+++] Dependable agent-run operations** - The workshop curriculum covers validation, integrations, operations, and memory; the XLA example adds lead-agent deduplication; Pixel Agents exposes waits for human approval. Together they point to the need for observable, resumable workflows with reviewable outcomes ([workshop](https://github.com/GrahamHelton3/harness-engineering-workshop); [XLA post](https://x.com/patrickjcampbell/status/2117928338422786536), 122 likes, 9 retweets, 16 replies, 7,868 views, 37 bookmarks; [Pixel Agents post](https://x.com/0xMfox/status/2117975491963945269), 14 likes, 2 retweets, 4 replies, 1,490 views, 5 bookmarks). The evidence is a mix of curriculum and individual/team reports, not a market-size estimate.

**[++] Trusted extension discovery** - Hermes Marketplace is live and its hackathon explicitly scores reliability as well as usefulness, polish, and creativity ([launch](https://x.com/NousResearch/status/2118006378690754694), 651 likes, 55 retweets, 70 replies, 60,050 views, 529 bookmarks; [hackathon](https://x.com/NousResearch/status/2117988208528449824), 307 likes, 32 retweets, 42 replies, 33,768 views, 151 bookmarks). Compatibility, provenance, and quality signals could help users choose extensions, but neither announcement demonstrates demand for a verification layer.

**[+] Failure-to-evaluation pipelines** - FAiE proposes converting recurring run failures into evaluations and training data ([announcement](https://x.com/oumi_ai/status/2117992968347181298), 35 likes, 5 retweets, 3 replies, 3,254 views, 7 bookmarks). This is an emerging product direction; the preview has no independent improvement results.

## 8. Takeaways

1. **Agent engineering is being framed as workflow design, not prompt sequencing.** A workshop names validation, integrations, operations, and memory, while the XLA example describes lead-agent deduplication and specialist investigation ([workshop](https://github.com/GrahamHelton3/harness-engineering-workshop); [@patrickjcampbell](https://x.com/patrickjcampbell) [reported](https://x.com/patrickjcampbell/status/2117928338422786536) (122 likes, 9 retweets, 16 replies, 7,868 views, 37 bookmarks) the workflow).
2. **Reusable agent capabilities now have a live catalog and a builder program.** Hermes Marketplace launched with a high-engagement announcement, and the associated hackathon makes reliability one of its judging criteria ([marketplace](https://x.com/NousResearch/status/2118006378690754694), 651 likes, 55 retweets, 70 replies, 60,050 views, 529 bookmarks; [hackathon](https://x.com/NousResearch/status/2117988208528449824), 307 likes, 32 retweets, 42 replies, 33,768 views, 151 bookmarks).
3. **Human control remains visible in the run loop.** A creator reported a two-hour approval wait, while FAiE proposes reusing failures for evaluation and training; both are early signals rather than independently measured outcomes ([Pixel Agents](https://x.com/0xMfox/status/2117975491963945269), 14 likes, 2 retweets, 4 replies, 1,490 views, 5 bookmarks; [FAiE](https://x.com/oumi_ai/status/2117992968347181298), 35 likes, 5 retweets, 3 replies, 3,254 views, 7 bookmarks).
