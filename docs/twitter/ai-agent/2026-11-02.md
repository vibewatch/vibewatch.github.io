# Twitter AI Agent - 2026-11-02

## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 Agents move into supervised product workflows (🡕)

Two product patterns moved agents out of a chat window and into workflows with a review boundary. [@mathewpregasen](https://x.com/mathewpregasen) [announced](https://x.com/mathewpregasen/status/2117300715739857151) (120 likes, 30 retweets, 38 replies, 4,677 views, 22 bookmarks) Letterpress-1, a beta growth-engineering agent, harness, and IDE that its founder says reads external website and market signals and turns fixes or experiments into pull requests. In a launch-thread reply, the founder [claimed](https://x.com/mathewpregasen/status/2117300717304324120) (5 likes, 1 reply, 177 views) an A/B test could create variants and conversion tracking in 60 seconds; these are product claims, not measured conversion results. One reader [asked](https://x.com/oliviersm199/status/2117304961008890038) (1 like, 1 reply, 67 views) whether to view it mainly as an agent harness or as an SEO/AEO review tool.

[@Saltan_PK](https://x.com/Saltan_PK) [described](https://x.com/Saltan_PK/status/2117327604336464244) (17 likes, 6 replies, 1,416 views) Firstmate as one operator agent coordinating parallel sessions, retaining project rules and in-flight work, and bringing completed changes back for approval. The author reports 560+ tasks and 365 merged pull requests across nine weeks; that is one user's account, not an independent benchmark. The public [MIT-licensed repository](https://github.com/kunchenguid/firstmate) describes a Shell-based agent distribution for coordinating coding work. Its presentation photo displays the project page with about 8.5k stars and 173 contributors, a distribution signal rather than evidence of task quality.

![Firstmate presentation slide showing its GitHub project page, MIT label, and displayed community metrics](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HWJBpjhWIAA2b--.jpg)

**Comparison to prior day:** The Nov. 1 report emphasized harness design and cross-session memory; today's posts make those ideas more product-specific, with a growth-engineering IDE and a human-reviewed parallel-task workflow.

### 1.2 Persistent skills meet changing tool ecosystems (🡕)

Two small tools target different continuity gaps. [@DanKornas](https://x.com/DanKornas) [introduced](https://x.com/DanKornas/status/2117314826049306660) (2 likes, 4 replies, 496 views) Skills Manager, a shared skill library for Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, and other agents. Its [MIT-licensed TypeScript project](https://github.com/shenysun/skills-manager) describes an npm CLI with skill-specific update checks and an approximate context-cost ledger. One reply [requested](https://x.com/doktordos/status/2117319277430906984) (4 views) per-skill permissions, a reminder that sharing capabilities also raises scoping questions. [@ShubhamInTech](https://x.com/ShubhamInTech) [released](https://x.com/ShubhamInTech/status/2117315809500680468) (9 likes, 2 replies, 217 views) ImproveMyAgent, an open-source local tool intended to turn repeated corrections in coding-agent histories into `AGENTS.md` rules; its eight-second runtime is the author's claim.

The research extends the problem from preserving context to keeping agent knowledge current. [@zxlzr](https://x.com/zxlzr) [introduced](https://x.com/zxlzr/status/2117258520307585041) (8 likes, 115 views) Δτ-Bench, an executable benchmark with 1,588 tasks across 42 domains and six changing tool-ecosystem states. The [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34238) reports that the strongest evaluated model or harness achieved 42.3% task success and that skill-learning gains did not consistently survive ecosystem changes. Its proposed STEWARD system maintains skills and provisions tools as tasks change; [code](https://github.com/zjunlp/delta-tau-bench) and a [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/zjunlp/delta-tau-bench) are public.

![Δτ-Bench illustration of an agent facing retired, overlapping, and newly available tools, where task success can hide missed opportunities](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HWIBpFEbQAEoHiD.png)

![Δτ-Bench construction diagram showing validated tasks and successive capability-preserving tool changes](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HWIBr5FaUAATITH.jpg)

![STEWARD diagram showing joint maintenance of skills and provisioning of tools as task requirements change](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HWIBwrWbgAA6IlH.jpg)

**Comparison to prior day:** Nov. 1 posts framed memory as a way to resume work across sessions. Today adds shared skill libraries and correction capture, while Δτ-Bench tests whether those stored procedures remain useful when tools change.

### 1.3 Agent transaction trust moves from proposals to measured failures (🡕)

[@BlockRunAI](https://x.com/BlockRunAI) [reported](https://x.com/BlockRunAI/status/2117280846168117613) (7 likes, 6 retweets, 2 replies, 472 views) results from its Agent Money Benchmark: 7 of 10 models paid a cheating seller at least once, 6 paid twice for the same data, and only 4 made no money errors. In a separate financial-research track, the top model answered 50% of questions correctly at a reported $0.468 per question. The [public results](https://agentmoneybench.com/) cover 80 adversarial-marketplace tasks with simulated sellers and 76 paid-data questions; payments used real USDC on Solana. Each model ran each task once, the full task set and run records are not public, and research answers receive human review with AI assistance. These are benchmark results, not estimates of real-world scam prevalence.

![Agent Money Benchmark summary showing 10 models, 80 adversarial tasks, 76 research questions, and real USDC settlement](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HWIXtS3W0AI6L7V.png)

The liability question also surfaced directly. [@RickLFG](https://x.com/RickLFG) [asked](https://x.com/RickLFG/status/2117338082127069194) (40 likes, 21 replies, 2,053 views) who should be blamed or refund a customer when one agent delegates poor work. The post says TermiX offers agent identities, job records, escrow until approval, and arbitration, while explicitly noting that adoption and outcomes remain uncertain. One reply [compared](https://x.com/0xsmalldas/status/2117338392791052431) (17 views) the problem to a contractor subcontracting work to someone less capable.

**Comparison to prior day:** Nov. 1 discussion centered on proposed trust infrastructure for agent marketplaces. Today adds a public benchmark of payment failures and a prominent question about accountability, while the benchmark's simulated sellers and one-run design limit what can be inferred about live usage.

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Context switching and repeated corrections

**Severity: Medium.** Three builder posts describe related friction, not a population-wide measure: Firstmate's user [said](https://x.com/Saltan_PK/status/2117327604336464244) (17 likes, 6 replies, 1,416 views) parallel sessions made it hard to remember what each agent was doing; Skills Manager's author [pointed to](https://x.com/DanKornas/status/2117314826049306660) (2 likes, 4 replies, 496 views) duplicated skill copies across coding agents; ImproveMyAgent's launch [targeted](https://x.com/ShubhamInTech/status/2117315809500680468) (9 likes, 2 replies, 217 views) repeated corrections. The proposed workarounds are a persistent project brief and human approval in Firstmate, a shared skill hub, and local extraction of recurring corrections into `AGENTS.md`. A reply to Skills Manager [asks](https://x.com/doktordos/status/2117319277430906984) (4 views) for per-skill permissions, showing that portability alone does not settle scope control. These are concrete build signals, but the posts do not independently establish that the products eliminate the friction.

### Tool knowledge goes stale

**Severity: Medium, benchmark evidence.** [@zxlzr](https://x.com/zxlzr) [introduced](https://x.com/zxlzr/status/2117258520307585041) (8 likes, 115 views) Δτ-Bench, which tests tasks across six evolving tool-ecosystem states. Its [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34238) reports only 42.3% success for its strongest evaluated model or harness; accumulated skills did not consistently preserve their value after changes. This is a designed research benchmark, not a production failure rate, but it isolates a failure mode that static instructions and one-time tool setup may miss. The released STEWARD prototype is a partial response, not evidence that the problem is solved.

### Financial mistakes and unclear recourse

**Severity: High when agents control funds; prevalence is unknown.** [@BlockRunAI](https://x.com/BlockRunAI) [reported](https://x.com/BlockRunAI/status/2117280846168117613) (7 likes, 6 retweets, 2 replies, 472 views) that 7 of 10 evaluated models paid a cheating seller at least once, and 6 paid twice for the same data. The separate user discussion is about the remedy after poor delegated work: [@RickLFG](https://x.com/RickLFG) [asked](https://x.com/RickLFG/status/2117338082127069194) (40 likes, 21 replies, 2,053 views) who should be blamed or refund a customer when one agent delegates to another. Escrow and arbitration are proposed coping mechanisms in the post, but it explicitly says real usage and outcomes are not yet clear. The benchmark's simulated sellers and single run per task limit external validity; payment errors make controls and dispute handling worth building.

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### Portable project memory and permissioned skills

This is a practical, direct need: builders describe wanting corrections, project state, and skills to persist across sessions and coding agents. [@Saltan_PK](https://x.com/Saltan_PK) [described](https://x.com/Saltan_PK/status/2117327604336464244) (17 likes, 6 replies, 1,416 views) Firstmate's saved project rules and in-flight work; [@ShubhamInTech](https://x.com/ShubhamInTech) [proposed](https://x.com/ShubhamInTech/status/2117315809500680468) (9 likes, 2 replies, 217 views) converting repeated corrections into `AGENTS.md`; [@DanKornas](https://x.com/DanKornas) [outlined](https://x.com/DanKornas/status/2117314826049306660) (2 likes, 4 replies, 496 views) a shared skills hub. A reply [asks](https://x.com/doktordos/status/2117319277430906984) (4 views) for per-skill permissions, so portability without explicit scoping is not the whole request. No post states a deadline or willingness to pay. Firstmate, Skills Manager, and ImproveMyAgent partially address the need at different layers. **Opportunity: Direct.**

### An operating system for agents

[@_banzaan](https://x.com/_banzaan) [asked](https://x.com/_banzaan/status/2117326625645691012) (14 likes, 3 replies, 147 views) for an agent system that schedules work, isolates tasks, preserves memory, and enforces permissions. This is a practical product request, but the evidence is one post and does not state urgency or budget. [Firstmate](https://github.com/kunchenguid/firstmate) demonstrates part of the workflow through parallel sessions, project state, and human approval; no cited project claims to satisfy the full operating-system spec. **Opportunity: Direct, with low confidence.**

### Clear responsibility when agents delegate

This is a practical, high-stakes need with competitive products already claiming partial solutions. [@RickLFG](https://x.com/RickLFG) [asked](https://x.com/RickLFG/status/2117338082127069194) (40 likes, 21 replies, 2,053 views) who bears the loss when delegated work is poor; one reply [compared](https://x.com/0xsmalldas/status/2117338392791052431) (17 views) it to a contractor subcontracting work to someone less capable. TermiX's identity records, escrow, and arbitration are a claimed partial answer, but the same post says adoption and outcomes remain uncertain. **Opportunity: Competitive.**

## 6. New and Notable

### Human direction remains material in autonomous research

[@armand_ruiz](https://x.com/armand_ruiz) [summarized](https://x.com/armand_ruiz/status/2117316110244774109) (1 reply, 65 views) the Meta FAIR paper [“Aligning Autoresearch: Co-Improvement between Agents and Humans”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38882). The paper studies agents that create research benchmarks and finds that human input remains important, especially during ideation; it also introduces HackBench to evaluate weaknesses in locating reward-hacked behavior and explores training agents for better human interaction. The paper is a preprint, and its reported results are not independent replications.

![First page of the Meta FAIR paper on co-improvement between human researchers and autoresearch agents](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HWI3qRJbUAAm5Hn.jpg)

### Epic outlines external connectors for Agent Factory

[@healthapiguy](https://x.com/healthapiguy) [reported](https://x.com/healthapiguy/status/2117287723488997792) (27 likes, 4 replies, 2,351 views, 25 bookmarks) that Epic Agent Factory agents will be able to invoke external MCP servers. Epic's [Open@Epic page](https://www.oec.epic.com/) schedules connectors for 2027 and says preconfigured connections can be offered through Epic Showroom beginning in March; the post says pricing and review details remain unknown. ### FireFlash reports a new cost-performance point

[@kaiwei_chang](https://x.com/kaiwei_chang) [reported](https://x.com/kaiwei_chang/status/2117378474654781780) (16 likes, 2 replies, 374 views) 46.2% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 for FireFlash, with over 30× lower cost per task than Fable 5 Max. The linked [technical report](https://agenteconomics.org/posts/agentic-rl-recipe) says FireFlash was trained from GLM-5.3-Flash with full-parameter reinforcement learning on 64 NVIDIA B300 GPUs in under five days; it reports a five-trial average and gains on two additional coding benchmarks. The comparison is author-reported and uses the authors' harness, so it needs independent replication.

## 7. Where the Opportunities Are

**[+++] Portable, permissioned state across agents** — Firstmate, Skills Manager, and ImproveMyAgent target project handoffs, shared capabilities, and correction memory, while a reply asks for per-skill permissions. [@Saltan_PK](https://x.com/Saltan_PK) [reported](https://x.com/Saltan_PK/status/2117327604336464244) (17 likes, 6 replies, 1,416 views) the parallel-session problem; [@DanKornas](https://x.com/DanKornas) [outlined](https://x.com/DanKornas/status/2117314826049306660) (2 likes, 4 replies, 496 views) the skills hub; and [@ShubhamInTech](https://x.com/ShubhamInTech) [launched](https://x.com/ShubhamInTech/status/2117315809500680468) (9 likes, 2 replies, 217 views) correction capture. The repeated build pattern supports the opportunity, but no cited product combines all three layers or proves comparative results.

**[+++] Financial controls and remedies for delegated work** — The benchmark's 7-of-10 scam result and the 40-like question about who refunds poor delegated work point to both prevention and recourse. [@BlockRunAI](https://x.com/BlockRunAI) [reported](https://x.com/BlockRunAI/status/2117280846168117613) (7 likes, 6 retweets, 2 replies, 472 views) the benchmark result; [@RickLFG](https://x.com/RickLFG) [asked](https://x.com/RickLFG/status/2117338082127069194) (40 likes, 21 replies, 2,053 views) who bears responsibility. The benchmark is limited to a company-designed task set, and marketplace mechanisms are not accompanied by adoption or outcome data.

**[++] Maintaining tool and skill knowledge as ecosystems change** — Δτ-Bench supplies an executable benchmark and dataset for this problem, while its paper reports that the strongest evaluated model or harness reached 42.3% success. The research [release](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34238) and [code](https://github.com/zjunlp/delta-tau-bench) make the gap testable; commercial impact is not established by this sample.

**[+] Domain-specific agents with human checkpoints** — Letterpress-1 applies agent workflows to growth engineering, and Epic has announced future MCP connectors for healthcare systems. The [Letterpress beta](https://letter.dev/) and [Epic's 2027 plan](https://www.oec.epic.com/) are early signals; neither source establishes outcomes, pricing, or broad adoption.

## 8. Takeaways

1. **The clearest product launches keep a human approval boundary.** Letterpress routes changes through pull requests, while Firstmate's user says completed work returns for approval. ([Letterpress launch](https://x.com/mathewpregasen/status/2117300715739857151) (120 likes, 30 retweets, 38 replies, 4,677 views); [Firstmate report](https://x.com/Saltan_PK/status/2117327604336464244) (17 likes, 6 replies, 1,416 views))
2. **Agent memory is splitting into project state, shared skills, and captured corrections.** These are separate product approaches, and the Δτ-Bench results show stored procedures can lose value as tools change. ([Skills Manager](https://github.com/shenysun/skills-manager); [ImproveMyAgent](https://github.com/AgnostAI/improvemyagent); [Δτ-Bench paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34238))
3. **Financial execution remains error-prone in this benchmark.** BlockRun reports that 7 of 10 models paid a cheating seller, but one run per task and the unpublished full task set limit generalization. ([Benchmark post](https://x.com/BlockRunAI/status/2117280846168117613) (7 likes, 6 retweets, 2 replies, 472 views); [results](https://agentmoneybench.com/))
4. **Human direction still matters in open-ended research.** The Meta FAIR paper reports that human input remains important in benchmark ideation and evaluates gaps in hack localization. ([Paper summary](https://x.com/armand_ruiz/status/2117316110244774109) (1 reply, 65 views); [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38882))
5. **Responsibility for delegated work remains unsettled.** TermiX is presented with identity, logs, escrow, and arbitration, but the post asking who refunds poor work explicitly says adoption and outcomes are unknown. ([Discussion](https://x.com/RickLFG/status/2117338082127069194) (40 likes, 21 replies, 2,053 views))
