# Twitter AI Agent - 2026-11-13

## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 Agent "factories" are being described as controllable, measurable stacks 🡕

A software-factory proposal breaks the environment into versioned workflows, owned data and context, isolated compute, model choice, measurement, orchestration, and access. The author says teams should track cost per pull request, cycle time, and latency, while keeping data and traces on infrastructure they control.

[@zachlloydtweets](https://x.com/zachlloydtweets) [presented](https://x.com/zachlloydtweets/status/2121318753711583254) (95 likes, 17 replies, 6,522 views, 115 bookmarks) the stack at a developer conference. The photo of the slide makes the six layers visible; it is a proposed architecture, not evidence that all teams have adopted it.

![Conference slide laying out a software-factory stack from factories-as-code and data to compute, inference, improvement, orchestration, and access](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HXBwKdvaMAAJL6a.jpg)

A separate post summarizes Anthropic's 1,000-agent Fermat's Last Theorem experiment. The post describes a shared task file, but Anthropic's [primary account](https://claude.dev/blog/safely-scaling-multi-agent-teams-with-fable-5-2/) says the agents coordinated through a shared Postgres database, each in its own sandbox and Git branch, with a merge-and-grade gate accepting only verified work. Anthropic calls the run a toy experiment and attributes the result to a carefully designed harness, not a general recipe for scaling agents.

[@sammy_capital](https://x.com/sammy_capital) [summarized](https://x.com/sammy_capital/status/2120949005211607171) (65 likes, 4 replies, 2,627 views, 78 bookmarks) the run as a lead agent assigning tasks to isolated helpers. Its image diagrams the lead/helper structure, shared database, independent sandboxes, and verified merge gate.

![Anthropic diagram of a 1,000-agent harness with isolated sandboxes, shared task database, and a merge-and-grader gate](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HW8f39XWIAAhOi-.jpg)

**Comparison to prior day:** Nov. 12 emphasized recovering long-running agent jobs. Today's factory framing adds team-level ownership, permissions, metrics, and model choice around those jobs; Anthropic's experiment adds a concrete shared-state and verification design.

### 1.2 Skills and plugins are being packaged for reuse across agent environments 🡕

Posts describe portable task instructions as a way to make repeatable team workflows reviewable instead of leaving them in individual chat histories. Product builder Buzz also framed plugins as a way for separate teams to connect tools and workflows without forcing every team into one setup.

[@dashboardlim](https://x.com/dashboardlim) [summarized](https://x.com/dashboardlim/status/2121324889877475651) (17 likes, 12 replies, 930 views, 14 bookmarks) a GitHub guide to reusable skills across Claude Code, Cursor, and Copilot. The example is a production-bug investigation checklist; the author notes that a skill describes a workflow but does not itself grant tools or permissions.

[@Hello_World](https://x.com/Hello_World) [described](https://x.com/Hello_World/status/2121298698265297003) (128 likes, 17 replies, 74,427 views, 200 bookmarks) Buzz plugins as a network harness for teams with different tools and rules. The post says a plugin marketplace is planned, so the marketplace itself is not yet a shipped feature.

**Comparison to prior day:** The Nov. 12 report covered a .NET skills catalog and model-specific helpers. Today's posts broaden the discussion to team portability and plugin-mediated collaboration.

### 1.3 Fundamentals and commerce remain human-scale gaps around capable agents →

One highly engaged post argues that software fundamentals become more important when agents can write code, because people still need to choose architectures and recognize insecure or unscalable results. Another argues that an agent's ability to perform a task does not by itself provide customers, pricing, or payment settlement.

[@johncrickett](https://x.com/johncrickett) [argued](https://x.com/johncrickett/status/2121279673451577766) (673 likes, 73 replies, 36,499 views, 332 bookmarks) for renewed emphasis on algorithms, networking, databases, architecture, and security as ways to evaluate generated software.

[@Nybuzi](https://x.com/Nybuzi) [described](https://x.com/Nybuzi/status/2121269392206184600) (201 likes, 123 replies, 2,575 views) the commerce layer as a separate problem for agents that need to find customers, agree on a price, and settle payment. The post points to TermiX SDKs and AACP as one proposed approach; it does not establish adoption beyond the author's account.

**Comparison to prior day:** Nov. 12 focused on long-run execution and security engineering. Today's posts turn toward the human judgment and business infrastructure needed around agent execution.

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Agent loops often lack checks, state, or an explicit stop condition

**Severity: Medium; the underlying scan is summarized in a promotional post.** One roundup says a scan of 36,487 repositories found 217 agent loops, with only 76 having a verifier, 35 preserving state between runs, and 10 recording cost. These counts are the author's summary, not independently established by the post. The listed workarounds include a verifier, spending limit, persisted state, and a stop rule. ([@fluixoo](https://x.com/fluixoo/status/2121300210349674787) (13 likes, 4 replies, 184 views))

### People still need enough engineering judgment to catch wrong output

**Severity: Medium, based on practitioner opinion.** A widely discussed post argues that generated code does not remove the need to assess architecture, networking, data storage, and security trade-offs. It is an argument about skill requirements, not evidence that agents routinely fail those checks. ([@johncrickett](https://x.com/johncrickett/status/2121279673451577766) (673 likes, 73 replies, 36,499 views, 332 bookmarks, 9 quotes, 66 retweets))

### Business execution is separate from task execution

**Severity: Low-confidence, early market signal.** One post says an agent that can perform a service still needs customer discovery, pricing, and payment settlement. The author points to a commerce SDK as a partial solution, but provides no adoption or transaction data. ([@Nybuzi](https://x.com/Nybuzi/status/2121269392206184600) (201 likes, 123 replies, 2,575 views, 45 retweets))

### Parallel-agent runs need shared state and verified integration

**Severity: Medium in Anthropic's experiment; prevalence is not established.** Anthropic reports that a single shared machine hit resource limits at around 100 agents, and describes using isolated sandboxes, shared task state, and a grader for the 1,000-agent run. The result is a company-reported toy experiment, not a general scaling threshold. ([Anthropic's account](https://claude.dev/blog/safely-scaling-multi-agent-teams-with-fable-5-2/))

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### A measurable and controllable agent factory

The proposed stack calls for versioned workflows, owned data and traces, configurable sandboxes, model choice, and metrics such as cost per pull request and cycle time. This is a practical systems need, though the post presents a design rather than a deployment case study. **Urgency: Medium. Opportunity: Direct.** ([source](https://x.com/zachlloydtweets/status/2121318753711583254) (95 likes, 17 replies, 6,522 views, 115 bookmarks, 12 retweets))

### Repeatable skills that travel across tools

The skills post gives a production-bug investigation as an example of a workflow that could be reused across Claude Code, Cursor, and Copilot. Existing `SKILL.md` conventions partially address it; the author notes that skills do not provide the tools or permissions themselves. **Urgency: Medium. Opportunity: Competitive.** ([source](https://x.com/dashboardlim/status/2121324889877475651) (17 likes, 12 replies, 930 views, 14 bookmarks))

### Agent service discovery, pricing, and settlement

The commerce post describes customer acquisition, pricing, and payment as work left over after an agent can perform a task. It points to an SDK and protocol as partial infrastructure, but provides no transaction evidence. **Urgency: Aspirational. Opportunity: Aspirational.** ([source](https://x.com/Nybuzi/status/2121269392206184600) (201 likes, 123 replies, 2,575 views, 45 retweets))

### Shared task state and verified handoffs for agent teams

Anthropic's experiment uses a shared database for task assignment and progress, isolated agent sandboxes and branches, and a grader before work is accepted. A reply to the social summary also asks what task metadata is needed for handoffs. These are design evidence from one experiment, not proof of broad demand. **Urgency: Medium. Opportunity: Direct.** ([Anthropic's account](https://claude.dev/blog/safely-scaling-multi-agent-teams-with-fable-5-2/), [discussion](https://x.com/sammy_capital/status/2120949005211607171) (65 likes, 4 replies, 2,627 views, 78 bookmarks))

### Research collections that stay current and work with agents

The paper-collection launch offers daily updates, paper chat, and MCP connections; replies ask for agent-side retrieval and a way to distinguish revisions from new papers. The collection is a partial answer, but update tracking and retrieval quality are not evaluated. **Urgency: Medium. Opportunity: Competitive.** ([Harness Engineering collection](https://academy.dair.ai/papers/collections/harness-engineering?view=latest), [source post](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2121277499673895121) (32 likes, 11 replies, 4,555 views, 46 bookmarks))

## 8. Takeaways

1. **The agent factory is becoming a team-owned system.** One proposed stack includes versioned workflows, owned data, controlled compute, model choice, and cost and cycle-time measurement. ([@zachlloydtweets](https://x.com/zachlloydtweets/status/2121318753711583254) (95 likes, 17 replies, 6,522 views, 115 bookmarks))
2. **Fundamentals remain important for evaluating generated work.** A high-engagement post argues that engineering knowledge helps people recognize unsuitable architectures and insecure or unscalable code. ([@johncrickett](https://x.com/johncrickett/status/2121279673451577766) (673 likes, 73 replies, 36,499 views, 332 bookmarks))
3. **Skills and loops need explicit controls.** Posts discuss portable workflows, but one roundup says its repository scan found relatively few loops with verifiers, state, or cost records; those counts are the author's summary. ([@dashboardlim](https://x.com/dashboardlim/status/2121324889877475651) (17 likes, 12 replies, 930 views, 14 bookmarks); [@fluixoo](https://x.com/fluixoo/status/2121300210349674787) (13 likes, 4 replies, 184 views, 8 bookmarks))
4. **Large agent teams need explicit coordination and verification.** Anthropic's toy proof run used isolated workspaces, shared task state, and grader-checked merges; the result does not establish a general scaling recipe. ([account](https://claude.dev/blog/safely-scaling-multi-agent-teams-with-fable-5-2/))
5. **Research curation is being connected to agent workflows.** DAIR.AI's collections pair daily-updated reading lists with paper chat and MCP access, while users ask for retrieval and revision tracking. ([@omarsar0](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2121277499673895121) (32 likes, 11 replies, 4,555 views, 46 bookmarks))
