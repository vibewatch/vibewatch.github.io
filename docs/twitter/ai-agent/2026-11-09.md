# Twitter AI Agent - 2026-11-09

## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 Harnesses as the system around the model (→)

Posts focused on the surrounding system that makes an agent useful over long tasks: task decomposition, persistent state and context handoffs, independent evaluation, and human checkpoints. The recurring distinction was between a model that can generate work and a harness that can keep it coherent, test it, and decide what happens next.

[@Mr_Buzzoni](https://x.com/Mr_Buzzoni) shared a paper on harness patterns across coding agents ([post](https://x.com/Mr_Buzzoni/status/2119730968437432423), 70 likes, 8 replies, 79 bookmarks, 3,591 views). The linked [Anthropic research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/harness-design-long-running-apps) describes a planner, generator, and evaluator; context resets with structured handoffs for long tasks; and a separate evaluator to counter agents' tendency to praise their own work. It also notes the added orchestration, token, and latency costs.

[@kmeanskaran](https://x.com/kmeanskaran) presented an ML workflow built with Google ADK and Gemini ([post](https://x.com/kmeanskaran/status/2119817320810057827), 94 likes, 21 replies, 95 bookmarks, 2,550 views). Its analyst, engineer, and skeptic roles profile data, build and evaluate models, and review for leakage or unfair features; a person approves features and the final model. The [public repository](https://github.com/kmeanskaran/auto-ml-adk) adds independent scoring on held-out rows and a fairness gate.

**Discussion insight:** Replies to the harness thread asked who audits a loop that edits itself; one practitioner described relying on a checklist and diff review. In the ML-team thread, the author said feature selection was the hardest step and described keeping a human decision gate for fairness-sensitive choices.

**Comparison to prior day:** This continued a topic already visible on Nov. 8, when [@omarsar0](https://x.com/omarsar0) discussed reusing agent trajectories as procedural experience for evolving harnesses ([post](https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2119473781689421855), 108 likes, 64 replies, 88 bookmarks, 7,832 views). On Nov. 9, examples and discussion put more emphasis on independent evaluation, role separation, and approval.

### 1.2 Enterprise autonomy is a control and adoption problem (→)

Enterprise deployment was framed as a gap between granting an agent a task and giving it only the authority needed to perform that task. The posts and linked reports point to access scoping, accountability, and organizational readiness as distinct from the model's ability to act.

[@berman66](https://x.com/berman66) described an internal 200-click task that agents could take over, but said engineers had to scope each agent's access and that this would not scale to hundreds of employees ([post](https://x.com/berman66/status/2119876427944251874), 26 likes, 8 replies, 8 bookmarks, 1,218 views). The recommendation was to enforce limited roles outside the prompt. Replies added the need for a definition of done and an accountable owner, and warned that excess access may only be noticed after an unexpected action.

[@dhinchcliffe](https://x.com/dhinchcliffe) linked a Nov. 9 [Frontier governance report](https://www.thisisfrontier.ai/research/your-ai-policy-cant-stop-an-agent-ai-governance-platforms) ([post](https://x.com/dhinchcliffe/status/2119848562863587605), 1 like, 1 reply, 1 quote, 116 views). It compares 23 governance offers and maps 46 vendor families, arguing that an inventory or written policy does not itself establish an enforceable control path. [@normativeai](https://x.com/normativeai) linked a separate [accountability analysis](https://www.norm.ai/resources/intent-of-ai-agents-operating-inside-companies) ([post](https://x.com/normativeai/status/2119882705257328734), 4 likes, 2 replies, 2 bookmarks, 261 views) about explaining, reproducing, and stopping actions in multi-agent systems.

[@businessbarista](https://x.com/businessbarista) argued that enterprise AI diffusion could take more than ten years, citing organizational readiness and a multi-step corporate development process ([post](https://x.com/businessbarista/status/2119863491141996946), 128 likes, 54 replies, 120 bookmarks, 12,728 views). Replies compared the rollout to cloud adoption and cited procurement delays, while other commenters disagreed with the forecast.

### 1.3 Agent work raises a unit-economics question (→)

The discussion of paid agent tasks moved beyond whether an agent can finish a job to whether it can stop before costs exceed the reward. [@2mrpc](https://x.com/2mrpc) used a $10 bounty and $4-per-attempt example: two attempts leave $2, while a third puts the task $2 in the red ([post](https://x.com/2mrpc/status/2119892436042523018), 21 likes, 13 replies, 6 bookmarks, 281 views).

![Diagram showing how $4 attempts can consume a $10 agent bounty](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HWte4viWwAAredz.jpg)

Replies proposed checking the remaining budget before each tool call, reserving costs per job to prevent concurrent tasks from spending the same balance, and counting retries and unpaid attempts. The linked [Agent.family marketplace](https://agent.family/) describes escrow, settlement on delivery, and a challenge process; the thread author said they had not found public documentation for per-attempt cost accounting.

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Reliability can regress mid-task

[@JoeyGilkey](https://x.com/JoeyGilkey) reported that an enterprise coding-agent experience deteriorated after Opus 5.5 was removed and improved after it was restored ([update](https://x.com/JoeyGilkey/status/2119906934799126889), 38 likes, 4 replies, 6 bookmarks, 1,249 views). He suspected that changing models mid-task and context compaction contributed, while explicitly labeling that explanation as a surmise. This is one user's account, not a controlled comparison, but it illustrates why teams care about stable model routing, recoverable context, and regression checks.

### Access scoping can become a rollout bottleneck

The access problem in [@berman66's account](https://x.com/berman66/status/2119876427944251874) was not only permission risk: engineers also had to configure each employee's access before the agent could be reused across a larger organization (26 likes, 8 replies, 8 bookmarks, 1,218 views). Replies asked for explicit completion criteria and ownership, and described discovering excess access only after an unexpected action. The post therefore connects governance risk with implementation labor; it does not quantify how common that bottleneck is.

### Unbounded retries can consume the reward

The $10-bounty example from [@2mrpc](https://x.com/2mrpc/status/2119892436042523018) assumes $4 of model and test-environment cost per attempt (21 likes, 13 replies, 6 bookmarks, 281 views). In the example, a third attempt makes the task unprofitable. Replies questioned whether failed attempts and unpaid work are counted, and proposed a budget check before each tool call. This is a hypothetical scenario, but it points to a measurable product gap: job-level cost and stop-condition accounting.

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### Reusable policy-level access for delegated agents

In [@berman66's words](https://x.com/berman66/status/2119876427944251874), “Access has to be scoped at the policy level” so engineering does not have to configure every agent-user pairing by hand (26 likes, 8 replies, 8 bookmarks, 1,218 views). The linked [Frontier report](https://www.thisisfrontier.ai/research/your-ai-policy-cant-stop-an-agent-ai-governance-platforms) similarly argues for controls that can constrain action, not just record policy or inventory systems. This is a practical need with direct enterprise evidence; opportunity: **Direct**.

### Budget checks and cost ledgers for each job

Replies to [@2mrpc's cost example](https://x.com/2mrpc/status/2119892436042523018) propose a budget check before each tool call and a per-job ledger that reserves estimated spend before parallel work begins (21 likes, 13 replies, 6 bookmarks, 281 views). The same discussion calls out retries and tasks that never pay as missing inputs to profitability. These are concrete controls that could be added to an agent runtime or job marketplace; opportunity: **Direct**.

### Skills learned from demonstrations without losing hidden constraints

[@Yuchenj_UW](https://x.com/Yuchenj_UW/status/2119852921244524895) questioned whether agents should need long written skill files if they could observe a task once and improve the method (33 likes, 12 replies, 5 bookmarks, 2,166 views). Replies noted that observation may miss rules such as what not to touch in production, and may confuse the intended method with a person's mistakes. The need is for learning from demonstrations that preserves constraints and can be reviewed, rather than merely generating more documentation; opportunity: **Aspirational**.

## 6. New and Notable

### Act-onomy links behavior labels to trace evidence

[@jiegaosophia](https://x.com/jiegaosophia) introduced Act-onomy as a taxonomy for describing agent behavior at runtime ([post](https://x.com/jiegaosophia/status/2119908170177106305), 8 likes, 1 reply, 2 bookmarks, 223 views). The public [project site](https://actonomy-agents.github.io/) and [repository](https://github.com/gaojie058/iab-actonomy) describe 10 actions, 46 subactions, and 120 leaf categories, with labels tied to quoted evidence in agent traces. The site links the [NeurIPS 2026 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13625) and downloadable codebook, datasets, and analysis tools.

![Act-onomy taxonomy diagram linking agent actions and subactions to evidence in a trace](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HWttHXNXYAAX9tW.jpg)

### Benchmark claims make cost and task selection visible

[@ArtificialAnlys](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys) reported Muse Ember at an Intelligence Index score of 36 and $0.14 per task for one setting ([post](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2119854348587123007), 67 likes, 14 replies, 8 bookmarks, 5,001 views). Its thread reports mixed task results, including a higher Terminal-Bench score than Claude Haiku 5.5 but a lower FrontierCode score. These are publisher-reported benchmarks, not an independently reproduced result, and the task split matters more than a single headline score.

## 7. Where the Opportunities Are

**[+++] Verifiable evaluation and recovery for long-running agents** — The Anthropic harness research describes structured handoffs and a separate evaluator; the ML-team build uses an independent test split and skeptic review; and [Act-onomy](https://github.com/gaojie058/iab-actonomy) links behavior labels to trace evidence. A reported quality regression during model switching shows why teams also need task-level regression checks ([\@JoeyGilkey](https://x.com/JoeyGilkey), [post](https://x.com/JoeyGilkey/status/2119906934799126889), 38 likes, 4 replies, 6 bookmarks, 1,249 views). This is strong because evaluation, trace inspection, and recovery recur across research, a build, and a user-reported failure.

**[++] Reusable permission boundaries and accountability** — [@berman66](https://x.com/berman66) describes per-user access scoping as a rollout bottleneck ([post](https://x.com/berman66/status/2119876427944251874), 26 likes, 8 replies, 8 bookmarks, 1,218 views), while the [Frontier report](https://www.thisisfrontier.ai/research/your-ai-policy-cant-stop-an-agent-ai-governance-platforms) distinguishes policy inventories from controls that constrain actions. The MCP thread adds practical concerns about permission scope and changed tool descriptions ([\@MaryamMiradi](https://x.com/MaryamMiradi), [post](https://x.com/MaryamMiradi/status/2119890223609806965), 14 likes, 5 replies, 14 bookmarks, 420 views). This is a moderate opportunity grounded in both enterprise rollout and tool-use discussions.

**[+] Job-level budgets and profitability checks** — [@2mrpc](https://x.com/2mrpc) and its replies outline budget checks, reserved costs, retries, and unpaid work in a $10 bounty example ([post](https://x.com/2mrpc/status/2119892436042523018), 21 likes, 13 replies, 6 bookmarks, 281 views). The need is specific and easy to measure, but evidence today is concentrated in one discussion rather than repeated across many independent posts.

([\@berman66](https://x.com/berman66), [post](https://x.com/berman66/status/2119876427944251874), 26 likes, 8 replies, 8 bookmarks, 1,218 views; [Frontier report](https://www.thisisfrontier.ai/research/your-ai-policy-cant-stop-an-agent-ai-governance-platforms))
