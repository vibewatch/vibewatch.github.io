## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 Local inference is being tested as a substitute for frontier services 🡕

[@log4j](https://x.com/log4j) [started](https://x.com/log4j/status/2115104490583925005) (577 likes, 53 replies, 13,139 views, 104 bookmarks) an eight-month experiment comparing a self-hosted compute cluster with frontier models for day-to-day work. The plan includes publishing configurations and rolling evaluations; the post is a proposed test, not a result.

### 1.2 Enterprise adoption is moving beyond pilots 🡕

[@businessbarista](https://x.com/businessbarista) [summarized](https://x.com/businessbarista/status/2114864181937586359) (50 likes, 15 replies, 9,659 views, 70 bookmarks) an interview with Experian's Chief AI Officer. The interview notes report 85% employee use of company AI tools, 88% developer use of coding assistants, and more than 6,000 employee-built GPTs by the first half of fiscal 2026. These are company interview figures; the account also describes a control plane and risk framework.

### 1.3 Agents are framed as supervised workflow components 🡕

[@garrytan](https://x.com/garrytan) [described](https://x.com/garrytan/status/2115141652344078597) (78 likes, 24 replies, 8,251 views, 45 bookmarks) a support workflow that drafts replies, call recaps, cancellation notes, CRM updates, and bug reports for a founder to review and send. Replies distinguish drafting from allowing an agent to send customer messages without approval.

**Comparison to prior day:** October 26 focused on scheduled developer chores, enterprise adoption barriers, and a production billing rebuild. Today adds a local-compute experiment and examples of company-wide use and human-reviewed support tasks.

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Trusting agents with customer-facing actions

The support workflow keeps a person in the final review-and-send step, and a reply explicitly distinguishes drafting from trusting an agent to send unaudited messages. This is a clear control preference, although the thread does not measure how often autonomous sending fails. [@garrytan](https://x.com/garrytan) [described](https://x.com/garrytan/status/2115141652344078597) (78 likes, 24 replies, 8,251 views, 45 bookmarks) the workflow.

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### Bounded agents with visible checks and a human handoff

The support example keeps people responsible for sending, while the local-compute experiment proposes publishing rolling evaluations against frontier models. Together they point to a practical need for agents that can do useful work while exposing enough evidence for a person to review the result. **Opportunity: direct.** [Support workflow](https://x.com/garrytan/status/2115141652344078597) (78 likes, 24 replies, 8,251 views); [local-compute experiment](https://x.com/log4j/status/2115104490583925005) (577 likes, 53 replies, 13,139 views).
