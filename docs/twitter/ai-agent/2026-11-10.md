# Twitter AI Agent - 2026-11-10

## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 Agent evaluation is expanding into visual professional work (🡕)

Artificial Analysis and Google DeepMind introduced a benchmark aimed at multi-step work over visually dense files, rather than text-only answers. It uses 200 private tasks across 10 industries, with roughly 70 weighted checks per task and deliverables such as slide decks, spreadsheets, reports, and marked-up images. Google says tasks were designed with domain experts across 83 occupations and represent work estimated to take a professional three to eight hours.

[@ArtificialAnlys](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys) reported Climb WSR scores of 57% for Claude Fable 5.2 (max), 54% for Gemini 4 Carbon (high), and 53% for Claude Opus 5.5 (max); no model leads across all verticals ([post](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2120220567038984212); 402 likes, 17 reposts, 42 replies, 29,329 views; [benchmark](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/mka); [Google DeepMind launch](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/multimodal-knowledge-agent-benchmark/)). Artificial Analysis reports large cost and turn-count differences as well as score differences; in one example task, Fable scored higher than Carbon but used more turns and cost more.

![MKA chart showing Climb WSR scores and cost per task across evaluated models](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HWyI__MWIAA0orK.jpg)

**Discussion insight:** The result highlights vertical-specific performance and expense rather than one general-purpose winner. The benchmark publishers say their automated grader agreed with domain-expert judgments 96% of the time on reviewed outputs; this is a publisher-reported validation.

**Comparison to prior day:** Nov. 9 focused on harness design, independent evaluation, and human checkpoints. The MKA launch makes evaluation more concrete by scoring agent-produced professional artifacts across multiple industries.

### 1.2 Agent orchestration is being framed as a runtime and cost choice (🡕)

Two posts describe agents as coordinated workers or on-demand jobs rather than one model handling every step. Their trade-offs are different: one vendor demo delegates implementation to a local model, while a practitioner argues that cloud agents should exist only while a task is running.

[@atomicagent_io](https://x.com/atomicagent_io) says its Fusion mode used Fable 5.2 as planner and local Qwen 3.8 27B as builders to make a pop-up book in 11 minutes for $2.98, versus 21 minutes and $15.77 with Fable alone ([post](https://x.com/atomicagent_io/status/2119918110752846094); 797 likes, 40 reposts, 19 replies, 58,797 views). The author clarified that $2.98 counts Fable tokens only and excludes local Qwen inference; commenters questioned the quality comparison and called for independent verification.

[@dabit3](https://x.com/dabit3) argues for a cloud agent harness that starts an agent and its computer for a job, then scales them down afterward ([post](https://x.com/dabit3/status/2119943488980877423); 130 likes, 8 reposts, 27 replies, 15,312 views). Replies note that rebuilding environments can consume time and that learning or memory may need to persist between jobs.

**Discussion insight:** Specialization can reduce paid model use in a single demo, but reported cost depends on whether local compute is counted. Ephemeral execution also raises environment setup and state-retention questions.

**Comparison to prior day:** Nov. 9 discussed long-running harnesses and job budgets. Nov. 10 adds a concrete planner/worker cost claim and a serverless-style proposal, with replies exposing what each leaves out.

### 1.3 Enterprise deployment is a process, not just an agent capability (🡕)

One high-engagement practitioner account describes the work between an agent pilot and production: sponsorship, workflow audits, data readiness, access control, harness engineering, benchmarks, change management, legal/security review, and ongoing permissioning. The post's 10-plus-year diffusion estimate is a forecast, while the named process bottlenecks are its concrete evidence.

[@businessbarista](https://x.com/businessbarista) outlines an 11-step “Corporate SDLC” and says procurement, data hygiene, access gateways, stakeholder turnover, and production readiness can slow deployment ([post](https://x.com/businessbarista/status/2119863491141996946); 232 likes, 18 reposts, 80 replies, 24,243 views). Replies compare the transition to cloud adoption and cite procurement time; others disagree with the timeline.

**Comparison to prior day:** The Nov. 9 report described per-user access scoping and accountability. Today's post broadens that control problem into the organizational work around pilots, evaluation, and production.

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Agent cost claims can omit local compute

**Severity: Medium.** Atomic Agent's demo reports a $2.98 Fable-token cost for a hybrid run, but its author clarifies that Qwen ran locally and its compute cost is not included ([post](https://x.com/atomicagent_io/status/2119918110752846094); 797 likes, 19 replies, 58,797 views). The post therefore does not establish a fivefold end-to-end cost reduction. MKA's benchmark results also show that quality, turns, and cost vary by model and task ([benchmark](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/mka)). This is a measurement gap, not evidence of a universal pricing failure. **Worth building for: High**, particularly where teams compare local and hosted execution.

### Enterprise rollouts have many dependencies beyond model quality

**Severity: Medium, based on one detailed practitioner account.** The enterprise diffusion post lists data readiness, procurement, access control, change management, evaluation, security review, and production readiness as blockers between sponsorship and deployment ([post](https://x.com/businessbarista/status/2119863491141996946); 232 likes, 80 replies, 24,243 views). Replies mention procurement delays and compare the rollout with cloud adoption, while others dispute the author's 10-plus-year forecast. **Worth building for: High** where agents need to move from pilot to governed production, though the post does not quantify prevalence.

### Stateless jobs may spend effort rebuilding context

**Severity: Low-confidence.** The proposed Lambda-like agent model drew replies that environment setup can consume the benefit and that useful memory may need to survive across jobs ([post](https://x.com/dabit3/status/2119943488980877423); 130 likes, 27 replies, 15,312 views). These comments identify design trade-offs rather than measured failure rates. **Worth building for: Medium** for workloads where setup or prior-task knowledge is material.

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### Cost accounting that includes the full agent run

**Need:** Compare agent configurations on wall time and complete cost, including local compute, retries, and output quality. Atomic Agent's own clarification says its quoted price excludes local Qwen inference, while MKA reports costs alongside task scores. **Urgency: Medium. Opportunity: Direct**, especially for teams mixing hosted planners with local workers. ([Atomic Agent post](https://x.com/atomicagent_io/status/2119918110752846094); [MKA benchmark](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/mka))

### A repeatable path from pilot to governed enterprise deployment

**Need:** Integrate data, permissions, evaluations, change management, and production monitoring without rebuilding each part for every initiative. The businessbarista post enumerates those steps and cites access gateways and data hygiene as blockers; the Nov. 9 discussion likewise described per-user permission setup as difficult to scale. This is a practical need, but the timeline attached to it is a forecast, not an independently measured industry estimate. **Urgency: Medium. Opportunity: Direct.** ([businessbarista post](https://x.com/businessbarista/status/2119863491141996946))

### Agent jobs that can reuse safe state without paying for idle workers

**Need:** Preserve useful environment and learning state across jobs while scaling compute down between tasks. Dabit3 proposes ephemeral cloud agents, and replies raise the cost of rebuilding the environment and losing memory. **Urgency: Low-confidence. Opportunity: Competitive**, because the thread proposes an architecture rather than documenting an existing deployment failure. ([dabit3 post](https://x.com/dabit3/status/2119943488980877423))

## 6. New and Notable

### MKA publishes a small public set alongside its private evaluation

Google and Artificial Analysis say the scored 200-task MKA set is private, but release 10 public examples for people to inspect and run themselves ([Google DeepMind launch](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/multimodal-knowledge-agent-benchmark/); [public dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/multimodal-knowledge-agent)). The launch describes tasks with expert rubrics weighted toward factual accuracy and visual design, and reports that domain experts agreed with the automated judge on 96% of reviewed outputs. The public examples make the task format inspectable without exposing the evaluation set.

## 7. Where the Opportunities Are

**[+++] Evaluation of real agent deliverables** — MKA scores outputs such as slides, spreadsheets, and reports across 10 verticals, while publishing a public sample and common harness. This extends evaluation beyond chat quality and gives teams concrete artifacts to inspect. ([MKA benchmark](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/mka); [public tasks](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/multimodal-knowledge-agent))

**[+++] Full-run cost and performance accounting** — Atomic Agent's demo excludes local inference from its advertised cost, while MKA reports substantial variation in turns and cost between model/task combinations. A ledger that includes hosted tokens, local compute, time, retries, and output quality is supported by both examples. ([Atomic Agent post](https://x.com/atomicagent_io/status/2119918110752846094); [MKA benchmark](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/mka))

**[++] Enterprise deployment controls and readiness** — The enterprise workflow post lists data access, policy, security, evaluation, and change-management steps before production; these overlap with the previous day's access-scoping discussion. The opportunity is moderate because today's evidence is one detailed practitioner's account, not a measured market prevalence. ([businessbarista post](https://x.com/businessbarista/status/2119863491141996946))

## 8. Takeaways

1. **Agent evaluation is moving toward finished work products.** MKA scores outputs from visual files across 10 verticals, with both a private evaluation set and public examples. ([MKA benchmark](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/mka))
2. **Agent cost claims need a complete accounting boundary.** Atomic Agent's advertised hybrid-run price excludes local Qwen inference, while benchmark results show model/task costs can differ materially. ([Atomic Agent post](https://x.com/atomicagent_io/status/2119918110752846094); [MKA benchmark](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/mka))
3. **Enterprise deployment involves data, permissions, evaluation, and organizational change.** The 11-step process is one practitioner's account and its 10-year timeline remains a forecast, but the listed implementation gates are concrete. ([post](https://x.com/businessbarista/status/2119863491141996946))
