# Twitter AI Agent - 2026-10-26

## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 Scheduled agents are being applied to routine developer work 🡕

PostHog says it has automated six developer chores with scheduled agents and offers copyable prompts. [@posthog](https://x.com/posthog) [announced](https://x.com/posthog/status/2114795132126106093) (50 likes, 5 replies, 4,850 views, 99 bookmarks) the examples, but the linked article was unavailable for inspecting the individual tasks or outcomes.

### 1.2 Enterprise adoption hinges on data access, ownership, and cost 🡕

Notes from one interview with a real-estate company's Head of AI identify data permissions, organizational incentives, time with subject experts, and inference cost as blockers. [@businessbarista](https://x.com/businessbarista) [summarized](https://x.com/businessbarista/status/2114508933075280232) (104 likes, 21 replies, 12,436 views, 217 bookmarks) the discussion, which also names Glean as a secure knowledge-work entry point. This is one interview, not a market-wide study.

### 1.3 A coding-agent team reports a production billing rebuild 🡕

An agency case study says a two-engineer team rebuilt a billing core, merged 122 pull requests in its first 90 days, and reached production by month six. [@mardehaym](https://x.com/mardehaym) [described](https://x.com/mardehaym/status/2114748008780685603) (45 likes, 15 replies, 6,287 views, 33 bookmarks) knowledge mapping before coding, written feature specifications, and human verification and review. It is a single reported project, not a general productivity benchmark.

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Access, incentives, and incomplete context

Interview notes identify data access, politics, staff time, and unclear ownership of AI costs as adoption barriers. In the billing case, many business rules were undocumented and had to be inferred from production data before implementation. These examples point to organizational and discovery work, not only model capability.
