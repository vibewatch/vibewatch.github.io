# Twitter AI Coding - 2026-11-02

## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 Specifications and independent checks before coding 🡒

Three workflow artifacts make intent and verification explicit: a specification is drafted before implementation, reviewed with fresh context, or checked against what actually ran. GitHub’s Octoverse supplies adoption context, but its platform-wide PR figures do not measure code quality.

[@JDSalbego](https://x.com/JDSalbego) [summarized](https://x.com/JDSalbego/status/2117150134735626254) (4 likes, 4 replies, 110 views) GitHub’s report that more than 40% of September pull requests started with an agent’s first draft, up from 17% in March. The [Octoverse report](https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-commits-pushes-reach-new-highs-in-2026-as-developers-build-with-agents/) also says agent-started PRs more often changed multiple files than PRs without detected agent involvement, 87% versus 79%.

[@dextune](https://x.com/dextune) [described](https://x.com/dextune/status/2117119514454172050) (10 likes, 4 replies, 781 views) Elephant-Goldfish Model: an Elephant drafts a specification with full project context, then fresh Goldfish reviewers inspect it without the drafting chat history. The [Google repository](https://github.com/google/elephant-goldfish-model) documents brainstorming, design review, and drift checks across Antigravity, Claude Code, and Codex.

![Elephant-Goldfish workflow showing context-rich specification drafting, fresh-context review, core skills, and supported coding agents](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HWF0SJRaYAAxgYj.jpg)

[@aiwithjainam](https://x.com/aiwithjainam) [outlined](https://x.com/aiwithjainam/status/2117243550098424270) (4 likes, 17 replies, 354 views) GitHub Spec Kit’s sequence from constitution and specification through clarification, plan, tasks, implementation, and convergence; its post cautions that this process does not guarantee correct code. The [project documentation](https://github.com/github/spec-kit) describes reusable workflows for existing projects as well as new builds.

[@Sakshar2303](https://x.com/Sakshar2303) [introduced](https://x.com/Sakshar2303/status/2117249739041501303) (5 likes, 7 replies, 143 views) Receipts, which compares an agent’s claims with session evidence and the git diff, marking claims verified, contradicted, stale, unverified, or needing human review. The author’s test on 39 agent replies is self-reported. Replies raised false negatives and incomplete evidence; the author says missing evidence should fail closed.

**Discussion insight:** The Octoverse figures describe adoption, while the Receipts thread focuses on whether a particular run’s claims are still true after its last edit. These are related but distinct signals: more agent-started work does not itself establish that review or verification is effective.

**Comparison to prior day:** The Nov. 1 report already covered EGM and Spec Kit as pre-coding workflows. [@stretchcloud](https://x.com/stretchcloud) [outlined](https://x.com/stretchcloud/status/2116625003277881615) (111 likes, 5 replies, 10,416 views) EGM’s design-and-review split, while [@KeyoAPI](https://x.com/KeyoAPI) [described](https://x.com/KeyoAPI/status/2116750506723086619) (7 likes, 10 replies, 271 views) Spec Kit’s staged flow. Today’s Receipts example adds post-run evidence checking to that same design-and-review discussion.

### 1.2 Enterprise access and self-hosted economics 🡕

The day’s enterprise examples show different choices around who may use coding agents, how they are governed, and how they are paid for. They are individual company decisions, not evidence of a universal deployment pattern.

[@BoringBiz_](https://x.com/BoringBiz_) [summarized](https://x.com/BoringBiz_/status/2117324473946743085) (288 likes, 66 replies, 37,130 views) Bloomberg reporting that Goldman Sachs updated internal guidance to limit Claude Code and GitHub coding tools mainly to engineers after reviewing productivity and risk; “strats” are exempt. [Quartz’s account of the report](https://qz.com/goldman-sachs-restricts-ai-coding-tools-traders-bankers-110226) says some traders were frustrated and quotes the bank describing a period of experimentation before deciding who is best placed to use the tools.

[@guilleflorvs](https://x.com/guilleflorvs) [reported](https://x.com/guilleflorvs/status/2117379073785696395) (4 likes, 7 replies, 273 views) Revolut AI operations lead David Abitbol’s account of serving about 2,000 engineers with self-hosted GLM on 56 Nvidia B200 GPUs. He cited a blended cost near $0.30 per million tokens and a 70% improvement in engineering lead time; these are company-reported figures, not a controlled comparison. The [AI Engineer talk transcript](https://ai.engineer/nyc/2026/talks/revolut) adds deployment and inference-tuning context.

[@GitHubEnt](https://x.com/GitHubEnt) [announced](https://x.com/GitHubEnt/status/2117343495912374391) (20 likes, 1 reply, 6,939 views) that eligible enterprise customers can include Copilot access for licensed GitHub Enterprise users. The [changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-10-28-github-enterprise-now-includes-access-to-github-copilot/) says this usage draws on the enterprise budget and that existing subscriptions are unaffected.

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Quota use and shared allowances are difficult to forecast

**Severity: Medium.** Two individual posts describe unexpectedly fast use: [@bebuu_ai](https://x.com/bebuu_ai) [said](https://x.com/bebuu_ai/status/2117141301821051225) (4 likes, 2 replies, 277 views) 73% of a Gemini 4 Argon weekly allowance was used in a 20-minute session, and [@qubitium](https://x.com/qubitium) [reported](https://x.com/qubitium/status/2117131185948397620) (2 likes, 1 reply, 107 views) that Copilot Max ran out in under 48 hours of code review. These are isolated user reports, not typical-use measurements. Separately, OpenAI’s shared ChatGPT/Codex pool may make resets more frequent for some users depending on model and usage, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/02/openai-is-finally-merging-chatgpts-chat-and-work-tabs/) and [@TechCrunch](https://x.com/TechCrunch)’s [post](https://x.com/TechCrunch/status/2117255174523638204) (15 likes, 12 replies, 8,347 views). The posts suggest a need for per-model and per-task usage visibility; they do not establish how many users face the problem.

**Worth building for:** Yes. A visible allowance balance, reset forecast, and attribution by model or task directly address the uncertainty described in these reports.

### An agent can report success after its last valid check

**Severity: Medium.** [@Sakshar2303](https://x.com/Sakshar2303) [described](https://x.com/Sakshar2303/status/2117249739041501303) (5 likes, 7 replies, 143 views) an agent that ran tests, then edited a test file without rerunning them, but still said the tests passed. The post’s claim that Receipts caught no false claims across 39 agent replies is the author’s own test, not an independent evaluation. The failure is specific, but it illustrates why a completion message or earlier test result is not proof of the final code state.

**Worth building for:** Yes, especially in workflows where test results or agent claims trigger review or merge decisions. Version-bound checks and evidence summaries address the demonstrated failure without treating one anecdote as prevalence data.

### Role-based restrictions can remove useful workflows

**Severity: Low to Medium.** Bloomberg reporting, summarized by [@BoringBiz_](https://x.com/BoringBiz_) [here](https://x.com/BoringBiz_/status/2117324473946743085) (288 likes, 66 replies, 37,130 views), says Goldman limited Claude Code and GitHub coding tools mainly to engineers after a productivity-and-risk review; Quartz reports that some traders who had used the tools were frustrated. The article says “strats” are exempt and does not establish how many workers lost access or what alternative tools they use.

**Worth building for:** Conditional. The signal supports clearer, task-specific access and review policies, but does not show that the restriction is a general user need outside this company.

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### Usage budgets visible before and during a run

The practical need is not merely a larger allowance, but a way to see what is consuming it. Replies to [@TechCrunch](https://x.com/TechCrunch)’s [shared-pool post](https://x.com/TechCrunch/status/2117255174523638204) (15 likes, 12 replies, 8,347 views) asked for more visible usage attribution, while the [article](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/02/openai-is-finally-merging-chatgpts-chat-and-work-tabs/) says some users may wait longer for resets depending on model and use. The reported change does not give plan-specific token totals. **Practical need; opportunity: Direct.** Per-model and per-task usage, remaining balance, and reset estimates would make the shared budget easier to plan.

### Evidence that stays tied to the final change

The [Receipts author](https://x.com/Sakshar2303) [described](https://x.com/Sakshar2303/status/2117249739041501303) (5 likes, 7 replies, 143 views) an agent that edited a test after running it, then claimed the tests passed without rerunning them. The thread’s discussion asks how missing evidence and false negatives are handled; the author says missing evidence should fail closed. The [Receipts repository](https://github.com/sakshar2303/receipts) checks agent claims against session output and the git diff, so this need has an early practical solution. **Practical need; opportunity: Competitive.** A version-bound verification record and explicit “not verified” state could make such checks easier to review across tools.

### One place to coordinate parallel agent work

[@DanKornas](https://x.com/DanKornas) [presented](https://x.com/DanKornas/status/2117382748692197498) (4 likes, 4 replies, 382 views) Emdash as a way to run agents on separate worktrees and review diffs and CI results without juggling terminals. Its [public repository](https://github.com/generalaction/emdash) also describes local and SSH-connected projects. This is a practical coordination need rather than a speculative request; the project is already a partial answer. **Practical need; opportunity: Competitive.** Differentiation would need to come from reliable task intake, review, and merging across the tools teams already use.

## 7. Where the Opportunities Are

**[+++] Evidence-bound verification for agent changes** — GitHub’s report says more than 40% of September pull requests began with an agent’s first draft, while Receipts addresses a concrete failure where tests were not rerun after an edit. EGM and Spec Kit also make specifications and review explicit. A version-bound record of changed files, current test results, and human approval would connect these signals without assuming that more agent-written code is inherently worse. ([Octoverse](https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-commits-pushes-reach-new-highs-in-2026-as-developers-build-with-agents/); [@Sakshar2303](https://x.com/Sakshar2303) [described](https://x.com/Sakshar2303/status/2117249739041501303) (5 likes, 7 replies, 143 views) the stale-test case; [@dextune](https://x.com/dextune) [described](https://x.com/dextune/status/2117119514454172050) (10 likes, 4 replies, 781 views) EGM.)

**[++] Usage attribution and predictable limits** — The shared ChatGPT/Codex pool, the Antigravity quota screenshot, and the Copilot Max report all point to difficulty forecasting usage, while TokenEconomizer and Revolut describe cost-control approaches. A dashboard that estimates per-task and per-model consumption and shows reset timing could complement these tools. The user reports are isolated, so demand prevalence remains unknown. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/02/openai-is-finally-merging-chatgpts-chat-and-work-tabs/); [@bebuu_ai](https://x.com/bebuu_ai) [shared](https://x.com/bebuu_ai/status/2117141301821051225) (4 likes, 2 replies, 277 views); [@qubitium](https://x.com/qubitium) [reported](https://x.com/qubitium/status/2117131185948397620) (2 likes, 1 reply, 107 views); [TokenEconomizer](https://github.com/microsoft/TokenEconomizer); [AI Engineer talk](https://ai.engineer/nyc/2026/talks/revolut).)

**[+] Governed parallel-agent operations** — Emdash and Copilot Superagent show demand for parallel task intake and worktree isolation; OpenShell focuses on runtime boundaries for files, network, and credentials. A combined workflow that lets teams parallelize work while retaining scoped permissions and reviewable diffs is an emerging opportunity, supported by several projects but not by evidence of broad adoption. ## 8. Takeaways

1. **Agent adoption makes verification more salient, not optional.** GitHub reports that more than 40% of September pull requests began with an agent’s first draft, while Receipts documents a case where tests were stale after the final edit. ([Octoverse](https://github.blog/news-insights/octoverse/octoverse-commits-pushes-reach-new-highs-in-2026-as-developers-build-with-agents/); [@Sakshar2303](https://x.com/Sakshar2303) [described](https://x.com/Sakshar2303/status/2117249739041501303) (5 likes, 7 replies, 143 views) the case.)
2. **Usage predictability is an active product issue.** OpenAI’s shared ChatGPT/Codex pool, an Antigravity quota screenshot, and a Copilot Max exhaustion report all raise questions about attribution and resets, though only the policy change is broader than an individual account.
