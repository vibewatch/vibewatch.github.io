# Twitter AI Coding - 2026-11-20

## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 Harnesses, not just models, shape agent outcomes 🡕

Several posts treated coding-agent results as a product of the full execution loop: context, tools, permissions, state, and verification. The evidence ranges from a harness overview to an OpenAI production workflow and two model comparisons; these are author-reported results, not a shared controlled benchmark.

[@MaryamMiradi](https://x.com/MaryamMiradi) [outlined](https://x.com/MaryamMiradi/status/2123875734305345575) (15 likes, 16 replies, 470 views, 12 bookmarks) five harness layers, from the agent loop and tool access to context, memory, and evaluation. She summarized LangChain research as moving from 52.8% to 66.5% on Terminal-Bench 2.0 without changing the model, and highlighted checks for early stopping and repeated edits.

[@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai) [summarized](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2123577231389934075) (13 likes, 14 replies, 2,015 views, 8 bookmarks) OpenAI's Performance Factory workflow: Codex agents inspect performance alerts, a second agent tries to disprove the diagnosis, and an engineer reviews proposed fixes. The post reports about 17 investigations per day with 70% of diagnoses judged accurate, while only about 15 fixes from more than 600 diagnoses shipped unchanged.

![OpenAI Performance Factory article describes Codex agents investigating performance regressions and proposing fixes for review](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HXh16g-bAAAElAK.jpg)

[@abbyxuu](https://x.com/abbyxuu) [compared](https://x.com/abbyxuu/status/2123696585028800896) (22 likes, 4 replies, 3,139 views, 5 bookmarks) Gemini 4 Neon results of 12.8% with mini-SWE and 39.0% in Google Antigravity. A reply asked whether both runs had the same retry allowance, leaving a key comparison condition unresolved.

[@Taddeus_B90](https://x.com/Taddeus_B90) [reported](https://x.com/Taddeus_B90/status/2123871916088082497) (3 likes, 3 replies, 114 views, 2 quotes) a self-run, seven-day, 36-step rollout across four coding-agent clients, with charts of capability, efficiency, model timelines, and work breadth. ### 1.3 Cost, reliability, and scope remain constraints 🡒

Alongside integration launches, individual users described long-session failures, startup crashes, process cleanup problems, and confusing quota boundaries. These reports do not establish how common the issues are, but they show that reliability and operational controls remain part of the day-to-day AI coding experience. The specific examples and workarounds are detailed in sections 2 and 3.

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Long-session and startup reliability

**Severity: High for affected users; prevalence uncertain.** [@LVpath](https://x.com/LVpath) [reported](https://x.com/LVpath/status/2123653249861300292) (4 likes, 2 replies, 149 views) that long Codex sessions had become unusable for them and recommended Claude Code with Higgsfield despite the higher cost. The author connects the change to a recent model release, but the post does not establish a cause. Separately, [@alexgetmancom](https://x.com/alexgetmancom) [reported](https://x.com/alexgetmancom/status/2123841699243794719) (9 likes, 1 reply, 298 views, 2 quotes) that Codex stopped launching after an update; [issue 62683](https://github.com/openai/codex/issues/62683) describes a macOS startup crash and duplicate reports.

![Desktop crash dialog attached to a report that Codex stopped launching after an update](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HXlmpMPXQAA8LLc.png)

The reports identify session continuity and startup recovery as high-impact problems for the affected users, but they do not measure how many users are affected. A practical opportunity is crash-safe resumption and clearer recovery diagnostics rather than assuming a specific model is at fault.

### Process cleanup and permission boundaries

**Severity: High in the reported failure; prevalence uncertain.** [@PatchOnChain](https://x.com/PatchOnChain) [described](https://x.com/PatchOnChain/status/2123905243155193974) (2 likes, 5 replies, 98 views) one agent test run that left 14 Node processes, used 18 GB of memory, and changed a port in configuration. [@buhlaustin](https://x.com/buhlaustin) replied with workarounds using process groups, hard timeouts, port checks, and read-only configuration.

One low-reach post by [@binancechiller](https://x.com/binancechiller) [shared](https://x.com/binancechiller/status/2123591160744788239) (2 replies, 14 views) an Antigravity permission guide. Its screenshots show a repeated read-access prompt and recommend checking the exact path, granting only required access, verifying the read, and avoiding broad always-allow choices. The author explicitly labels it practical troubleshooting, not a universally confirmed Google fix.

![Antigravity prompt asking whether to allow read access to a specific path, with one-time, conversation, always, and deny options](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HXiC49hWMAAEIpw.jpg)
![User-authored guide recommending narrow file access and checking that the requested read actually succeeded](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HXiC49cWkAAwmLm.jpg)

These are isolated examples, but process leakage and overly broad permissions can have costly consequences. Process lifecycle controls and least-privilege access are worth building into agent runtimes; the thread does not establish frequency.

### Quotas, billing, and plan terms

**Severity: Medium; evidence is limited to individual reports.** [@LincLogic](https://x.com/LincLogic) [mapped](https://x.com/LincLogic/status/2123605708180697550) (1 like, 2 replies, 27 views) at least seven Google AI Pro quota categories, cross-product credits, and separate Gemini API billing. The author asked other users to correct the diagram, so it is evidence of perceived complexity, not an authoritative quota specification.

![User-created diagram separating Google AI product quota pools, shared credits, and separate API billing](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HXiQIEkaUAAFVOr.jpg)

[@wunderacle](https://x.com/wunderacle) [reported](https://x.com/wunderacle/status/2123897790829072589) (4 likes, 2 replies, 86 views) a mismatch between an OpenAI open-source offer described as ChatGPT Pro at $100 and an account showing a $200 Pro tier with a temporary full discount. This is a single unconfirmed account report; it is not evidence of a general billing problem. Clearer quota and plan-entitlement views could reduce confusion, but the data does not show how widespread this need is.

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### Supported APIs for resuming active chats

This is a practical need with direct evidence. [@marcomarandiz](https://x.com/marcomarandiz) [asked](https://x.com/marcomarandiz/status/2123646615814545809) (2 likes, 2 replies, 233 views) for a supported API to wake an existing Codex desktop chat, preserving context when a user answers a card. The author says their Say Less prototype also needs documented handling for busy chats and duplicate events. **Opportunity: Direct.** The post describes a prototype workaround, not a supported interface.

### Searchable history across agent tools

This is a practical, moderate-urgency need that already has an early partial solution. [@v3frankie](https://x.com/v3frankie) [shared](https://x.com/v3frankie/status/2123891766613221604) (1 like, 3 replies, 89 views) Scrollback, which summarizes and searches sessions from multiple coding agents. [@not_sacke](https://x.com/not_sacke) replied that finding an old Claude Code chat was where they usually gave up, then asked how the index grows over time. **Opportunity: Competitive.** Scrollback addresses discovery, but its public repository is early and the reply raises a scaling question.

### Clearer usage limits and runtime-control behavior

The need is practical, but the evidence is small and mixed. [@LincLogic](https://x.com/LincLogic) [asked](https://x.com/LincLogic/status/2123605708180697550) (1 like, 2 replies, 27 views) other Google AI Pro users to correct a diagram of separate product quotas and shared credits. [@wunderacle](https://x.com/wunderacle) [reported](https://x.com/wunderacle/status/2123897790829072589) (4 likes, 2 replies, 86 views) one mismatch between an open-source offer and the account tier shown. In a separate enterprise-control thread, [@NashBriggsy](https://x.com/NashBriggsy) asked whether Defender's Block option stops a whole agent session or only a tool call; the author recommended testing it first. [@buckleyplanet](https://x.com/buckleyplanet) [described](https://x.com/buckleyplanet/status/2123733455036952734) (11 likes, 5 replies, 940 views, 15 bookmarks) the Audit and Block choices.

Budget controls in [OpenCode Console](https://opencode.ai/docs/console/harnesses/) and isolation products such as [Eclipse Enclave](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4251051/ai-coding-agents-need-a-safe-place-to-work-eclipse-is-building-the-sandbox-to-give-it-to-them.html) partially address these needs, but the cited posts do not show a single view that explains entitlements, quota usage, and runtime block outcomes together. **Opportunity: Competitive.** The immediate need is clearer state and policy feedback; prevalence is not established.

## 8. Takeaways

1. **Harness design is part of the result.** The same Gemini model was reported at 12.8% with mini-SWE and 39.0% in Antigravity, while a reply questioned retry parity. ([@abbyxuu](https://x.com/abbyxuu) [compared](https://x.com/abbyxuu/status/2123696585028800896) (22 likes, 4 replies, 3,139 views))
2. **Verification can improve diagnosis without guaranteeing accepted fixes.** The Performance Factory summary reports about 70% diagnostic accuracy but only about 15 unchanged fixes from more than 600 diagnoses. ([@rohanpaul_ai](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai) [summarized](https://x.com/rohanpaul_ai/status/2123577231389934075) (13 likes, 14 replies, 2,015 views))
3. **Agent ecosystems are adding integrations quickly, with deployment constraints.** New database and test connections sit alongside GitHub's public-preview and administrator requirements.
