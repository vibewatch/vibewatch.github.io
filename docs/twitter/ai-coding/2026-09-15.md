# Twitter AI Coding - 2026-09-15

## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 Antigravity is turning from a single Google surface into a multi-model, multi-extension runtime (🡕)

Antigravity stayed central, but the conversation broadened. `antigravity` mentions held near yesterday's peak at 37 today versus 38 on September 14, while `gemini` climbed to 31, well above the prior-week average of 17.9. The difference was not a clean new-model announcement. It was a cluster of posts treating Antigravity as the runtime where Google can mix models, expose APIs, and attract outside builders.

[@wallstengine](https://x.com/wallstengine) [reported](https://x.com/wallstengine/status/2099630451912802331) (244 likes, 10 replies, 32,058 views) that Google is now letting engineers use Claude company-wide for internal coding work through Antigravity under quotas, and the linked [Business Insider article](https://www.businessinsider.com/google-finally-lets-all-engineers-use-anthropics-claude-2026-9) says Gemini remains the primary model while third-party models cover specialized use cases. [@thtbee_](https://x.com/thtbee_) [read through the docs and summarized](https://x.com/thtbee_/status/2099566404546769397) (34 likes, 6 replies, 3,367 views) Antigravity as a persistent Ubuntu 24.04 sandbox with code, file, and web tools, custom functions, remote MCP support, and resumable multi-turn work, which matches Google's public [Antigravity agent docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/antigravity-agent). [@itsPaulAi](https://x.com/itsPaulAi) [shared](https://x.com/itsPaulAi/status/2099960192125325488) (70 likes, 5 replies, 103 bookmarks, 6,096 views) Google's open-source [ARTEMIS repo](https://github.com/google/artemis) as a way for Antigravity, Codex, and Claude Code to drive real Android devices, and the repo adds concrete scope: 6,001 stars, native MCP integration, captured logs/screenshots, and a claimed 99%+ AndroidWorld benchmark. [@yashjitpal](https://x.com/yashjitpal) [built](https://x.com/yashjitpal/status/2099846452369674591) (67 likes, 4 replies, 66 bookmarks, 4,345 views) BetterGravity, an external Antigravity mod layer with plugins, themes, in-app browser support, and bring-your-own-key routes via the public [repo](https://github.com/YashjitPal/BetterGravity).

[@Its_lakshya_ai](https://x.com/Its_lakshya_ai) [posted](https://x.com/Its_lakshya_ai/status/2099808193048199313) (64 likes, 5 replies, 4,655 views) an api-watch screenshot showing `models/antigravity-preview-09-2026`. The surrounding Gemini 4 speculation is unverified and should be treated as rumor, but the screenshot itself matters because it makes the Antigravity slug publicly legible enough to power documentation threads, reverse-engineering, and ecosystem work.

![api-watch screenshot listing models/antigravity-preview-09-2026 as a newly surfaced model entry](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HSOE6ZGagAA891J.png)

**Discussion insight:** The useful replies were pragmatic rather than tribal. A reply under the Google/Claude post called Opus inside Antigravity a productivity hedge, not a platform shift, while ARTEMIS replies clarified that the privacy-friendly on-device VLM is still roadmap, not shipped.

**Comparison to prior day:** September 14 established Antigravity as an execution surface. September 15 extended that story by showing Google widening the surface to rival models and third-party extensions.

### 1.2 Reliability, quota, and context management remain the gap between impressive demos and daily use (🡕)

Quota and reliability complaints sharpened materially. `quota` mentions rose to 31, up from 21 on September 14 and roughly double the prior-week average of 15.3. The loudest complaints were no longer vague frustration about cost. They were specific reports about capacity failures, forced compaction, and support channels that do not explain what is happening.

[@MmuzTW](https://x.com/MmuzTW) [said](https://x.com/MmuzTW/status/2099779773736370357) (154 likes, 27 replies, 13,063 views) they were canceling a Pro 20x subscription after repeated "model is at capacity" and "overloaded" failures, opaque risk control, and silent support follow-up. [@rodydavis](https://x.com/rodydavis) [acknowledged](https://x.com/rodydavis/status/2099930512303555007) (131 likes, 20 replies, 4,921 views) a live Gemini 3.8 Flash issue inside Antigravity and said rate limits would be reset after the fix. [@Soso_fun_yt](https://x.com/Soso_fun_yt) [published](https://x.com/Soso_fun_yt/status/2099921193474342973) (61 likes, 11 replies, 6,783 views) a highly detailed critique of Antigravity's harness-level context policy, arguing that a 256k active limit and 140k compaction threshold squander Gemini's larger native window and trigger lossy "forgetting loops" mid-session. Even the workaround layer became a talking point: [@MrTacticalX](https://x.com/MrTacticalX) [shared](https://x.com/MrTacticalX/status/2099987516392624371) (5 likes, 34 views) that Claude Code's `/low-priority` lane can keep work moving after the five-hour limit, albeit more slowly.

![Repeated 'Context automatically compacted' entries in a coding-agent session, each failing with 'Selected model is at capacity'](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HSPox_OawAASiVq.png)

**Discussion insight:** Replies were full of mitigations rather than denial. People compared tiers, suggested DeepSeek as a fallback, defended tighter context clamps as a stability tradeoff, or shared spare-capacity routes like `/low-priority`. That is a sign of a mature pain point: users are already building coping strategies because they do not expect the product surface to be fully truthful.

**Comparison to prior day:** September 14 quantified instruction and routing overhead. September 15 translated that into sharper anger about capacity errors, forced compaction, and support opacity during real work.

### 1.3 Copilot is moving up the stack into work tracking, live steering, and PR review (🡕)

Copilot discussion was less about raw model quality and more about orchestration surfaces above the prompt box. `copilot` mentions rose to 28 from 23 on September 14, and the noteworthy updates centered on keeping tasks, questions, and reviews inside the same working surface.

[@github](https://x.com/github) [announced](https://x.com/github/status/2099931978498007147) (48 likes, 4 replies, 27,153 views) the new My work pane in the Copilot app, and the linked [GitHub blog post](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/github-copilot/github-copilot-app-for-beginners-managing-your-work/) adds the concrete pieces: built-in views for active/review/done work, repo auto-scoping based on repos touched in the app, and custom GitHub-style filters. [@burkeholland](https://x.com/burkeholland) [showed](https://x.com/burkeholland/status/2099604401312694576) (46 likes, 6 replies, 2,413 views) that `/ask` can question a Copilot agent while it is still working, and a direct reply clarified that the feature works across all models. [@pierceboggan](https://x.com/pierceboggan) [introduced](https://x.com/pierceboggan/status/2099572243240276466) (29 likes, 3 replies, 3,171 views) Jira canvas in the Copilot app, while replies immediately flagged discoverability problems and Jira data failures. [@VisualStudio](https://x.com/VisualStudio) [promoted](https://x.com/VisualStudio/status/2099875891899768906) (10 likes, 3,482 views) PR review without leaving the IDE, and the linked [Visual Studio post](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/visualstudio/today-i-will-review-github-prs/) says developers can review diffs, add inline comments, approve/merge, then pull PR context into Copilot with `#pullrequest`.

**Discussion insight:** The replies were not rejecting these features. They were asking for cleaner entry points: better canvas discoverability, selected-text questioning, lighter app performance, and fewer hidden steps before a surface becomes useful.

**Comparison to prior day:** September 14 emphasized routing and provider choice. September 15 moved the competitive surface up a level, toward what is active, interruptible, reviewable, and waiting for a human.

### 1.4 The new competition layer is control: routing knobs, compression tricks, and safer execution boundaries (🡕)

Several of the day's most product-like updates sat above the model itself. They were about who controls spend, how much context reaches the model, and what boundaries remain when an agent acts on untrusted input.

[@markfenner](https://x.com/markfenner) [explained](https://x.com/markfenner/status/2099848905105997949) (10 likes, 3 replies, 473 views) that Copilot Auto can now be biased toward Efficiency, Balance, or Intelligence, and the linked [GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-09-14-configure-cost-and-quality-in-copilot-auto-model-selection) confirms the same model pool sits underneath each option while billing follows the actual model selected. [@AileLabs](https://x.com/AileLabs) [pitched](https://x.com/AileLabs/status/2099755678948556851) (22 likes, 3 replies, 2,123 views) [AILE](https://aile.sh) as a lower-price access market for premium models, while replies immediately questioned inference parity and revocation risk. [@0xBoomz](https://x.com/0xBoomz) [claimed](https://x.com/0xBoomz/status/2099922584967971254) (2 likes, 1 reply, 173 views) that SOMA's compressor can now cut a Copilot plus DeepSeek V4 Pro session by roughly 15%, up from about 10% at launch.

![Graphic comparing a 10 million token Copilot session without compression to an 8.5 million token session with SOMA](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HSRr0sHWkAAM81W.jpg)

The same "control layer" framing also dominated security discussion. [@_orcaman](https://x.com/_orcaman) [published](https://x.com/_orcaman/status/2099856720176546300) (33 likes, 10 replies, 1,333 views) a public [Accomplish writeup](https://www.accomplish.ai/blog/escaping-the-openai-codex-sandbox-twice/) describing two Codex sandbox escapes, while [@gossy_84](https://x.com/gossy_84) [summarized](https://x.com/gossy_84/status/2099876923530760430) (2 replies, 77 views) RoguePilot's passive prompt-injection path through GitHub issues and PRs. **Discussion insight:** Positive sentiment clustered around explicit levers: visible routing choices, visible token savings, visible sandbox mechanics. Negative sentiment clustered around opaque quotas and any workflow that still assumes users will trust hidden agent behavior.

**Comparison to prior day:** September 14 already showed routing becoming a product surface. September 15 widened that idea into compression middleware, secondary access markets, and concrete sandbox design.

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Opaque quotas and silent compaction make premium plans feel untrustworthy

The strongest frustration was not that premium coding agents cost money. It was that users do not trust the limit surface once work is underway. [@MmuzTW](https://x.com/MmuzTW) [described](https://x.com/MmuzTW/status/2099779773736370357) (154 likes, 27 replies, 13,063 views) a Pro 20x account that felt degraded, capacity-gated, and impossible to explain through official channels. [@Soso_fun_yt](https://x.com/Soso_fun_yt) [added](https://x.com/Soso_fun_yt/status/2099921193474342973) (61 likes, 11 replies, 6,783 views) the more technical version of the same complaint: the model may advertise a large window, but the harness can still erase that advantage with an aggressive compaction policy. [@rodydavis](https://x.com/rodydavis) [confirmed](https://x.com/rodydavis/status/2099930512303555007) (131 likes, 20 replies, 4,921 views) a live Gemini 3.8 Flash issue inside Antigravity, which made the pain feel systemic rather than anecdotal.

![Direct-message thread showing a feedback upload with a thread ID and a later follow-up that received no response](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HSPonsobgAAUhtL.png)

The severity is High because the complaint is about operational trust, not just pricing. Users feel they can pay for access and still be stranded mid-run with no honest explanation of why. Worth building for: High.

### Useful workflow surfaces still arrive with too much friction

The second frustration was that many of the right features are shipping, but they still require too much spelunking or too much surrounding machinery. [@pierceboggan](https://x.com/pierceboggan) [showed](https://x.com/pierceboggan/status/2099572243240276466) (29 likes, 3 replies, 3,171 views) Jira canvas in Copilot, but replies immediately cited invalid Jira payloads and the awkwardness of having an agent open the surface in the first place. [@burkeholland](https://x.com/burkeholland) [showed](https://x.com/burkeholland/status/2099604401312694576) (46 likes, 6 replies, 2,413 views) that `/ask` can interrupt a run usefully, yet replies still wanted selected-text questioning and complained about app heaviness. [@VisualStudio](https://x.com/VisualStudio) [framed](https://x.com/VisualStudio/status/2099875891899768906) (10 likes, 3,482 views) PR review plus Copilot handoff as one flow, which is directionally strong, but still tied to a specific host surface. [@paolino](https://x.com/paolino) [argued](https://x.com/paolino/status/2099865252238131629) (7 likes, 363 views) the counterpoint directly: simple issue triage should not require 2,000 lines of agentic YAML and expensive models when a small Ruby action and GPT-5.6-Luna can do the job.

This is Medium-High severity because the demand is clearly real, but the current answer often feels overbuilt or under-discoverable. Worth building for: High.

### Prompt injection and sandbox escape moved from theory to concrete diagrams and patch-level fixes

Security discussion was unusually concrete. [@_orcaman](https://x.com/_orcaman) [shared](https://x.com/_orcaman/status/2099856720176546300) (33 likes, 10 replies, 1,333 views) two named Codex escape techniques, Overpatch and Heapjack, plus fixed version thresholds. [@DataScienceDojo](https://x.com/DataScienceDojo) [argued](https://x.com/DataScienceDojo/status/2099927360459985170) (2 likes, 3 replies, 490 views) that "skip permissions" is effectively a speed hack that enlarges the blast radius unless a stronger sandbox exists underneath it.

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### Honest budget and routing controls across plans, models, and overflow paths

Users clearly want the kind of routing knob Copilot just shipped, but paired with truthful quota state and sanctioned overflow lanes. [@MmuzTW](https://x.com/MmuzTW) [wanted](https://x.com/MmuzTW/status/2099779773736370357) (154 likes, 27 replies, 13,063 views) a premium plan that does not silently degrade, [@markfenner](https://x.com/markfenner) [highlighted](https://x.com/markfenner/status/2099848905105997949) (10 likes, 3 replies, 473 views) the value of explicit routing bias, [@MrTacticalX](https://x.com/MrTacticalX) [shared](https://x.com/MrTacticalX/status/2099987516392624371) (5 likes, 34 views) a spare-capacity workaround in Claude Code, and [@AileLabs](https://x.com/AileLabs) [pitched](https://x.com/AileLabs/status/2099755678948556851) (22 likes, 3 replies, 2,123 views) a market route around premium-model pricing. This is practical and urgent. Opportunity: direct.

### Long-context agents that show their compaction policy and let users choose the tradeoff

The dataset repeatedly implies that developers do not just want a bigger theoretical context window. They want to know when the harness will compact, what it discards, and whether they can trade speed for continuity. [@Soso_fun_yt](https://x.com/Soso_fun_yt) [made](https://x.com/Soso_fun_yt/status/2099921193474342973) (61 likes, 11 replies, 6,783 views) that need explicit with a 256k-versus-1M argument, while [@thtbee_](https://x.com/thtbee_) [showed](https://x.com/thtbee_/status/2099566404546769397) (34 likes, 6 replies, 3,367 views) that Antigravity already exposes enough runtime structure for this to be surfaced more honestly. [@0xBoomz](https://x.com/0xBoomz) [offered](https://x.com/0xBoomz/status/2099922584967971254) (2 likes, 1 reply, 173 views) a compression-layer answer, but the stronger unmet need is native transparency in the harness itself. Opportunity: direct.

### Workspaces that are easy to interrupt, inspect, and carry across tasks

The Copilot app updates point toward a real need: users want to see active work, ask questions mid-run, attach PR or issue context, and re-enter the same task later without starting over. [@github](https://x.com/github) [introduced](https://x.com/github/status/2099931978498007147) (48 likes, 4 replies, 27,153 views) My work, [@burkeholland](https://x.com/burkeholland) [demonstrated](https://x.com/burkeholland/status/2099604401312694576) (46 likes, 6 replies, 2,413 views) `/ask`, and [@VisualStudio](https://x.com/VisualStudio) [showed](https://x.com/VisualStudio/status/2099875891899768906) (10 likes, 3,482 views) PR review plus `#pullrequest` handoff. Outside that stack, [@xunio98](https://x.com/xunio98) [argued](https://x.com/xunio98/status/2099677078257799627) (2 replies, 31 views) that agentic coding needs a runtime, not another chat pane, pointing to [herdr](https://github.com/herdrdev/herdr) as a persistence layer. This is practical and already partially solved, which makes the opportunity competitive rather than hypothetical. Opportunity: competitive.

### Safe-by-default execution boundaries instead of trust-based skip-permissions

People do not want to micro-manage approvals forever, but they also do not want safety to depend on careful prompting. [@gossy_84](https://x.com/gossy_84) [showed](https://x.com/gossy_84/status/2099876923530760430) (2 replies, 77 views) how little user-visible behavior a passive prompt injection requires, [@_orcaman](https://x.com/_orcaman) [showed](https://x.com/_orcaman/status/2099856720176546300) (33 likes, 10 replies, 1,333 views) that even mature coding-agent surfaces can ship real escapes, and [@DataScienceDojo](https://x.com/DataScienceDojo) [argued](https://x.com/DataScienceDojo/status/2099927360459985170) (2 likes, 3 replies, 490 views) for a harder container-plus-microVM boundary. This is a practical and urgent need. Opportunity: direct.

### Local model and harness matching that measures tool-calling durability, not just VRAM

The local-model story is still too easy to oversimplify. [@akshay_pachaar](https://x.com/akshay_pachaar) [presented](https://x.com/akshay_pachaar/status/2099857657872122108) (18 likes, 8 replies, 3,799 views) Magnitude as a way to profile hardware and recommend local models for major harnesses, and the public [Magnitude repo](https://github.com/magnitudedev/magnitude) turns that into a concrete product direction. But the replies were telling: people wanted assurance about long-run tool calling, context behavior, and harness stability, not just what fits in memory. That makes this a practical but more competitive niche. Opportunity: competitive.

## 6. New and Notable

### GPT-5.5 now has a hard retirement date inside Codex

[@CodexReleases](https://x.com/CodexReleases) [flagged](https://x.com/CodexReleases/status/2099961845171769472) (56 likes, 6 replies, 5,768 views) that GPT-5.5 retires from ChatGPT, ChatGPT Work, and Codex on October 14. The linked official [OpenAI changelog entry](https://developers.openai.com/codex/changelog#codex-2026-09-14-gpt-55-retirement) makes the operational consequence clear: for Codex with ChatGPT sign-in, teams need to move workspace defaults, custom agents, scheduled tasks, and scripts from `gpt-5.5` to `gpt-5.6-sol` before that date.

### ARTEMIS is one of the clearest bridges yet from coding agents to real-device execution

[@itsPaulAi](https://x.com/itsPaulAi) [surfaced](https://x.com/itsPaulAi/status/2099960192125325488) (70 likes, 5 replies, 103 bookmarks, 6,096 views) ARTEMIS as a Google open-source project, but the repo matters more than the hype: 6,001 stars, native MCP integration, captured logs and screenshots, and a concrete focus on real Android devices. That makes it more than another agent demo. It is one of the day's strongest examples of agent tooling escaping the editor and touching external systems in a structured way.

### Codex security discourse leveled up from vague caution to named exploits and exact patch levels

[@_orcaman](https://x.com/_orcaman) [documented](https://x.com/_orcaman/status/2099856720176546300) (33 likes, 10 replies, 1,333 views) Overpatch and Heapjack, plus the fixed versions for Codex CLI and Codex Desktop. Pair that with [@gossy_84](https://x.com/gossy_84) [making](https://x.com/gossy_84/status/2099876923530760430) (2 replies, 77 views) RoguePilot visually legible and [@DataScienceDojo](https://x.com/DataScienceDojo) [teaching](https://x.com/DataScienceDojo/status/2099927360459985170) (2 likes, 3 replies, 490 views) microVM-style containment, and the result is a broader public shift: security is now part of mainstream coding-agent product evaluation.
