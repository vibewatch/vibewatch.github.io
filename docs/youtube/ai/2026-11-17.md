### 1.2 Agent engineering focuses on memory, context, and governed actions (↑)

Five videos address coding agents, multimodal agents, or enterprise agent setup. Their practical advice converges on controlling context and actions, not just improving prompts.

[Google Cloud Tech](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCJS9pqu9BzkAMNTmzNMNhvg) demonstrates Scribant, a voice-to-action agent, with persistent Memory Bank, a 40-word rolling window, and rolling-summary compaction. The video reports 24 distilled memory tokens versus roughly 1,400 raw retrieval tokens and an 86% reduction in classifier tokens in its demo; these are provider-presented results, not an independent benchmark. The linked [Scribant repository](https://github.com/milesLV/Scribant-Agent) describes a local sandbox and requires user approval before Google Workspace actions. ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RvJ6UpR2oj8))

## 2. What Frustrates People

These are problems described in videos and product demonstrations, not measured audience complaints. The available engagement counters do not establish how many viewers experience them.

### Agents lose useful context or act unreliably

**Severity: Medium in the examples shown; prevalence unknown.** [Tech With Tim](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DfAOBID70Q0) lists complaints that agents forget instructions, claim work is finished when it does not work, or make unrelated changes. [Google Cloud Tech](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RvJ6UpR2oj8) describes a voice agent that repeatedly sent full transcripts, increasing token use and latency while losing state when the app closed. These sources identify failure modes but do not measure their rate across users.

### Creative generation requires correction and repeated attempts

**Severity: Medium in the tutorials and creator test; broader prevalence unknown.** [Jack Vs. AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_X7KQVwsn9U) demonstrates references, draft renders, anchor images, and editing to maintain character consistency and avoid expensive full rerenders. In [AI Samson's comparison](https://ltx.aifilmmaker.academy/gpt-image-2-6-sunburst/), the creator reports remaining errors in exact counts and reflected text. These are workflow demonstrations and a creator-run test suite, not a population-wide estimate.

### Local media tools have hardware and licensing limits

**Severity: Medium for users without supported hardware or suitable model rights; prevalence unknown.** The [Imagination Studio README](https://github.com/Keys-Wasserman/Imagination-Studio-by-keys-Wasserman) specifies machine-dependent model support and separate licences for models and bundled tools. Its local workflow is tangible, but the repository does not support the video's broad "zero limits" framing.

### Agent data access needs governance

**Severity: Unquantified.** A [Google Cloud demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QsGQQYj1vEY) explicitly contrasts a raw-SQL agent with one using governed business logic, data lineage, and field-level PII redaction. This signals that privacy and data context are design constraints in the vendor's example; it is not evidence of a documented deployment failure.

## 3. What People Wish Existed

The videos and their descriptions provide little direct evidence of viewer requests or willingness to pay for a product. The clearest explicit asks are policy positions; other needs below are workflow problems demonstrated by creators or vendors, not confirmed audience demand.

### A pause and rules for frontier-AI development

PauseAI's campaign calls for a temporary global pause on frontier-AI development and UK legislation. Its [event page](https://pauseai.uk/demonstrations/march-against-ai-extinction-nov-26) confirms that these are the organizers' stated requests. **Opportunity type: direct policy advocacy**, not a product opportunity; the video does not establish how broadly those positions are shared.

### Agents that retain context and make controlled changes

Google Cloud's memory demonstration and the coding-agent course both foreground persistent context, verification, and permissions ([memory demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RvJ6UpR2oj8), [course](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ajfQWhGQGLE)). Those are practical requirements represented in the workflows, but the videos do not document a direct request from users. **Opportunity type: competitive**; the cited demos already present provider-specific implementations.

### More predictable, controllable generative-media workflows

The animation tutorials use reference sheets, draft modes, selective correction, and editing, while the Images 2.6 comparison reports mixed results on precision tasks ([animation workflow](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_X7KQVwsn9U), [creator-run comparison](https://ltx.aifilmmaker.academy/gpt-image-2-6-sunburst/)). This is a plausible practical need shown by creators, not measured customer demand. **Opportunity type: aspirational** until supported by direct user requests or purchasing evidence.

No separate, strongly evidenced consumer feature request emerges from this snapshot.
