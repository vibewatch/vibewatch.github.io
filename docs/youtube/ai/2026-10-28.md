## 2. What Frustrates People

### Code generation can trade speed for understanding

**Severity: Medium; evidence of prevalence: Low.** Dev Tools Made Simple argues that generated code can leave developers with weaker codebase understanding and more difficult debugging or maintenance ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=97BwdxAeFHU)). This is one creator's commentary, not a measured frequency estimate. The suggested coping approach is to use AI as a pair programmer while retaining the ability to reason about and maintain the result; the snapshot does not establish willingness to pay for a solution.

### Agent actions require clear boundaries

**Potential impact: High; evidence of prevalence: Low.** The Home Assistant videos frame autonomous control as a trust question, and the Smart Life demo says it requests approval before creating automations ([Smart Home Solver](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IXx-mVG53Jc), [Smart Life](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LV9rnSw5VzU)). Separately, Binghamton's research describes a defensive approach to evaluating robot task plans ([research summary](https://www.binghamton.edu/news/story/6544/secret-trigger-words-could-make-ai-powered-robots-dangerous-and-these-researchers-developed-a-defense)). The examples support interest in bounded actions and review, but do not show how often deployed systems fail.

### Health-data sharing raises questions, not a measured complaint

The interview asks whether a patient should share years of lab results and genetic data with an AI tool, and when a clinician should replace a chatbot. The stakes can be high, but the video reports no adverse event or prevalence data; the evidence supports caution and escalation questions rather than a quantified user pain point ([interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iJfF_VC10tI)).

## 7. Where the Opportunities Are

**[+++] Permissioned, reviewable agent actions** - Home-control demos show why an agent may need approval before changing automations, while robot-planning research examines how to assess actions in context. Together these support an opportunity for clear scopes, approvals, and review logs around agents with real-world tools. This is a product hypothesis, not evidence of proven buyer demand.

**[++] AI coding workflows that preserve understanding** - One video describes risks to code comprehension and maintenance when developers delegate too much; another promotes no-code task automation. Tools that pair generation with explanation, tests, and review could address that tension, though the snapshot contains no direct request for such a product.

**[++] Transparent video-model comparisons and access terms** - One creator uses the same prompt and settings across eight models, while another notes that free credits, limits, and watermarks vary. Publishing reproducible comparisons alongside current access limits would address the information gap shown in the videos; no direct purchase intent appears.

**[+] Clinical AI data boundaries** - A physician interview raises questions about sharing medical and genetic data with AI, chatbot agreement, and when to seek a clinician. The stakes are meaningful, but this single interview does not establish a common unmet need.
