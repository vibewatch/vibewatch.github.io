## 2. What Frustrates People

### Control and accountability

High severity in the sources' framing, but not a measure of audience-wide sentiment: interview and campaign descriptions repeatedly point to concern about advanced systems outpacing oversight. The Diary of a CEO description connects agent-risk claims with corporate secrecy and regulation, while Team Human calls for a slowdown and international safeguards; CNN and Fox Business also frame control and government guardrails as open questions ([interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qDzg-xvkeXw), [campaign](https://www.teamhuman.org/), [CNN](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a-_TTEdieaI), [Fox Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S2qo07Am9BI)). The response shown is advocacy and policy discussion, not a concrete product. Safety evaluation and incident transparency are worth exploring, although this evidence does not establish buyer demand.

### Agent workload and hardware waste

Medium severity in one detailed practitioner tutorial: Nate B. Jones says agents can leave development servers, browsers, and build files running, while concurrent agents and local models compete for memory and storage. The suggested coping steps are to audit the existing computer, clean up background work, set stopping conditions, and schedule simultaneous workloads before buying more hardware ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xUl-RD4l4i4)). This is a specific productivity problem with practical workarounds, but prevalence is not established by one video.

### Uncertain limits of "free" video generation

Low-confidence friction: Malva AI's tutorial warns that free-video access can depend on quotas, refresh schedules, eligibility, region, and licensing; Vidu Q4's description also flags pricing as a topic ([Malva AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ApMdKQ5iXFo), [Vidu Q4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=asPQreUjtm4)). These are creator-stated caveats rather than reported user failures. Clear quota and rights comparisons could be useful, but the evidence for a widespread pain point is weak.

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### A credible path to human oversight

Team Human asks viewers to sign a statement calling for a global slowdown and international safeguards, and says signatures will be delivered to lawmakers. The site proposes policy measures including oversight of advanced chips and common safety standards, but these are advocacy goals rather than implemented controls ([Team Human](https://www.teamhuman.org/), [statement on superintelligence](https://superintelligence-statement.org/)). This is a practical political need with explicit urgency. Existing partial responses are petitions and public statements; the opportunity is aspirational because the requested outcome depends on coordinated public and government action.

### Agent workloads that fit the computer people already own

Nate B. Jones's video implies a practical need for visibility into agent memory, storage, and lingering processes before a user purchases new hardware. Its proposed workarounds are a diagnostic audit, cleanup, stop conditions, and scheduling concurrent tasks; the description does not identify a dedicated product ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xUl-RD4l4i4)). The need is practical, and the opportunity is direct for tools that make these checks and limits easier to apply.

### Predictable video-generation costs and rights

The video-generation tutorials make credits, quotas, eligibility, output limits, reference consistency, and licensing relevant to choosing a workflow ([Malva AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ApMdKQ5iXFo), [Vidu Q4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=asPQreUjtm4)). This is an implied creator need, not a quoted request. Existing tutorials and guides partially address tool selection, so the opportunity is competitive and the evidence remains early.
