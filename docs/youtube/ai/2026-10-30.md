## 2. What Frustrates People

The snapshot contains no comment text or clear first-person user failure reports. The concerns below come from video descriptions and demonstrations, so their prevalence is unknown.

### Agent permissions and influence

**Potential impact: High; evidence of prevalence: Low.** The risk-focused videos raise questions about persuasion, companionship, and human control, while the Smart Life demo says Claude asks before creating automations. These sources identify control as a design concern but do not document a rate of harmful actions or show how common the concern is among users. Permission and review tooling may be worth validating, but the dataset gives no willingness-to-pay signal. ([Neural Nutshell](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YAr0ckS_Uas), [Smart Life](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LV9rnSw5VzU))

### Free-video access terms are difficult to compare

**Potential impact: Medium; evidence of prevalence: Low.** The tutorials qualify free or unlimited claims with credits, eligibility, paid modes, sponsored segments, and changing access. One video does not name its tools in the description, leaving its claims difficult to reproduce. The available coping strategy is to compare specific tools and settings; this is creator-reported friction, not direct user complaint data. ([Malva AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CoFeT_SJXvg), [Malva AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJHsGX2Era0), [Becky the Ai Girl](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dqqmn5zew54))

### Local AI trades cloud dependence for hardware and software constraints

The hardware comparison describes memory capacity, speed, software compatibility, and cost as competing factors. It says local models can keep sensitive work on-device, but provides no linked test data or evidence of user dissatisfaction. The snapshot does not establish how often these tradeoffs block adoption. ([Tomorrow Engineered](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2OuSjPSPDmE))
