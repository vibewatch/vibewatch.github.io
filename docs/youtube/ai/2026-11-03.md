### 1.2 AI-generated mathematics brings verification and credit into focus (🡕)

## 2. What Frustrates People

The dataset contains creator descriptions and engagement counts, but no viewer comments or first-person reports. The issues below are implementation and evidence limits visible in the videos and linked sources; their prevalence among users cannot be estimated from this snapshot.

### Agent behavior is difficult to predict and verify

**Potential impact: Medium; prevalence: unknown.** IBM says coding agents need repository, dependency, test, and intent context, while the graph-engineering presentation proposes explicit decision structures to improve predictability. OpenAI’s public math repository also marks verification as uneven across its manuscripts. These sources identify control and validation work, but do not report a measured rate of agent failure. ([IBM Technology](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EkdIZXDnRK8), [AI Engineer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g_NgCLTibiM), [OpenAI math repository](https://github.com/openai/math))

### Generative-video production consumes credits and still has quality gaps

**Potential impact: Medium for creators; user prevalence: low evidence.** JOEY reports spending just under $450 on credits for a short film, including unsuccessful generations, and says voices remain difficult. Malva’s comparisons distinguish free credits and daily allowances from unlimited use in specific modes and warn that terms change. This supports cost and access as workflow constraints, not evidence that creators broadly share these experiences. ([JOEY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8g3Z1geC8BU), [Malva AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aG5OgGFYYuw))

### Smart-home agents require deliberate access choices

**Potential impact: Medium; prevalence: unknown.** HA-MCP documentation describes administrator credentials and separate local-network or remote-webhook connection options. These are configuration and permission decisions for an agent connected to household systems; the video does not provide a quantified failure rate or user complaints. ([HA-MCP documentation](https://github.com/homeassistant-ai/ha-mcp), [Smart Home Solver](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IXx-mVG53Jc))

## 7. Where the Opportunities Are

These are opportunity hypotheses suggested by creator coverage and public artifacts, not validated buyer demand.

**[+++] Verifiable evaluation and attribution for AI-generated research** — OpenAI’s public collection spans 721 manuscripts and 373 families, but reports uneven verification and formalization; researchers’ reactions also raise review, credit, and comprehension concerns. A clear way to trace claims to proofs, revisions, and independent checks would address a documented evidence gap. ([OpenAI math repository](https://github.com/openai/math), [researcher reactions](https://proofsandprompts.com/2026/10/08/100-reactions-to-100-solutions/))

**[++] Context and permission controls for action-taking agents** — IBM emphasizes repository-aware context, AI Engineer demonstrates graph-encoded decision structure, and Home Assistant examples require choices about credentials and local versus remote access. Making task context, permissions, and human override visible could serve several distinct workflows; this snapshot does not measure demand or reliability improvements. ([IBM Technology](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EkdIZXDnRK8), [AI Engineer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g_NgCLTibiM), [HA-MCP](https://github.com/homeassistant-ai/ha-mcp))

**[++] Cost-aware and reproducible generative-video production** — Malva’s two comparisons show that free access varies by mode and changes over time, while JOEY reports a short-film credit spend of nearly $450 and continuing audio limitations. Reusable production workflows and transparent per-output cost tracking are suggested by these examples, but no audience willingness to pay is established. ([Malva AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aG5OgGFYYuw), [JOEY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8g3Z1geC8BU))

## 8. Takeaways

1. **Agent design is being framed as a context and control problem, not only a prompt problem.** The videos cover repository context, graph-shaped skills, no-code task workflows, and local or remote smart-home access. ([IBM Technology](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EkdIZXDnRK8), [AI Engineer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g_NgCLTibiM))
2. **High-reach AI coverage joins capability claims to questions about human oversight.** InsideAI recorded 2.63 million views, while CNN covered black-box behavior and election chatbot responses; their view and comment counts show reach, not audience agreement. ([InsideAI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QRVz697rEx0), [CNN election report](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VjKREc7YWxo))
3. **AI-generated mathematical output is public at scale, but verification remains uneven.** OpenAI’s repository says roughly 42% of top-line results are formalized in Lean and does not present every manuscript as polished research. ([OpenAI math repository](https://github.com/openai/math))
4. **Generative-video workflows expose both practical craft and cost.** Malva compares changing free-use limits; JOEY describes a multi-stage workflow, nearly $450 in credits, and unresolved voice quality. ([Malva AI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aG5OgGFYYuw), [JOEY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8g3Z1geC8BU))
5. **The physical-AI evidence spans very different maturity levels.** Fetch is a maker demonstration with a public build page, Figure capabilities are channel-reported, and NVIDIA’s 6G examples are a vendor presentation of pilots and plans. ([Fetch build](https://projecthub.arduino.cc/sector_07/build-fetch-an-open-source-ai-robot-powered-by-arduino-uno-q-8092d1), [Figure coverage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JH8o8BV8Vds), [NVIDIA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yLMjEpMio2w))
