## 2. What Frustrates People

The videos are mostly tutorials and commentary, not first-person support reports. The issues below are creator-described; their prevalence among viewers is unknown.

### Keeping coding agents aligned with changing requirements

**Severity: Medium for teams using AI coding agents; prevalence: unknown.** IBM's [spec-driven development video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GpZ5T51jE4U) argues that stale or incomplete specifications leave agents with less context and can lead to repeated prompting. IBM's linked [explainer](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/spec-driven-development) also describes context drift and fragmented code as risks of AI-generated changes. The proposed coping method is to keep specifications iterative and version-controlled alongside code. The videos do not quantify rework or show measured outcomes, so demand for a separate tool is not established.

### Uncertain access, credits, and rights for generated video

**Severity: Medium for creators comparing tools; prevalence: unknown.** Malva AI's [four-tool comparison](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VIZlSgcknxA) and [nine-tool ranking](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aG5OgGFYYuw) qualify free or unlimited access by mode, credits, watermarks, region, licensing, and changing provider terms. Comparing tools and using draft generations are creator-suggested workarounds; comment counts do not measure how many viewers experience these problems.

## 6. New and Notable

### Controlled studies examine group-level agent behavior

The EXAM preprint reports that small groups of persistent extreme agents produced measurable, scenario- and model-dependent shifts across four simulated settings. A second linked paper studies collective risks such as conformity and collusion-like coordination. These findings concern controlled LLM simulations, not measured behavior in deployed agent populations. ([EXAM preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19863v1), [multi-agent risk preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27771v1))

### KV-cache research tests a memory tier between accelerator and host memory

An IBM research poster describes a vLLM emulator for a three-tier hierarchy of high-bandwidth memory, secondary fast memory, and host DRAM. Its purpose is to let researchers test KV-cache placement policies before the secondary-memory hardware is broadly available. ([poster](https://research.ibm.com/publications/offloading-kv-cache-to-secondary-memory-a-three-tier-hierarchy-emulator-using-vllm))

### Robotics and agent prototypes are reported with uneven evaluation detail

AI News attributes a 36% zero-shot result across 60 robot-manipulation tasks to LiberAI, while its description says Xiaomi did not share the full-shift success rate for the reported eight-hour humanoid trial. The same video calls DeepSeek's dsh-superagent research code. These are company-attributed developments, not independently validated comparisons in this sample. ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vu-_JAIpqGE))
