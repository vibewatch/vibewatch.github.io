# HackerNews AI - 2026-11-04

The 100 stories collected 1,372 points and 571 comments. The day's strongest attention went to Gemini data-use settings, cryptographic proof of model inference, an open-weight coding model, and the practical costs and controls of agent workflows. Several of the largest claims come from users, companies, or product builders and are not independently verified.

## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 Gemini data-use settings become a privacy concern (🡕)

A personal-account user says Gemini chat history can be used to improve models when Keep Activity is on, including for paid subscribers, and says turning it off removes the ability to keep chat history beyond 72 hours. Google's Privacy Hub says data from Gemini may be used to improve generative AI and that some data is reviewed by people; it advises users not to enter confidential information they would not want reviewers to see. The HN post drew the day's most engagement, but the author's account of their settings is not independently verified. ([jameslk](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=jameslk); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50363461) (578 points, 148 comments); [Google Privacy Hub](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961?hl=en#pn_data_usage))

**Discussion insight:** [richrichardsson](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=richrichardsson) (score 0) described losing phone-reminder functionality after disabling chat history. [viraptor](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=viraptor) (score 0) argued that the policy language was being overread and that reviewer retention may serve legal purposes. The thread therefore shows both concern about data use and disagreement over what the policy means in practice.

**Comparison to prior day:** November 3's most prominent privacy discussion concerned a Codex training opt-out that users said had turned back on. On November 4, the top story shifted to Gemini's Keep Activity setting and review policy, with 578 points and 148 comments versus 402 points and 126 comments for the earlier Codex post. ([November 3 HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50352757) (402 points, 126 comments))

### 1.2 Model provenance and open-weight performance draw scrutiny (🡕)

Anthropic's FlashProve post describes an open-source zkSNARK prototype for verifying that a response came from committed model weights. Anthropic reports proof generation at about 110 tokens per second on one H100 for a 4-bit-quantized 400B model, with a proof around 19 MB per 1,024-token request and roughly 70-95 times the GPU time of ordinary inference. The authors call this intermediate research; two Hacker News submissions point to the same release, and the more-discussed one drew 146 points and 74 comments. ([Anthropic research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/provable-inference); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50368626) (146 points, 74 comments); [duplicate submission](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50362579) (8 points, 3 comments))

**Discussion insight:** [neuwirth](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=neuwirth) (score 0) said the work builds on VerInf and questioned its attribution. [vessenes](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=vessenes) (score 0) welcomed the progress but questioned the compute overhead and whether a closed-model provider would expose the prompt details a proof needs. A separate launch, Ricursive's Ripple-65B Preview, claims an 80.52 score on TerminalBench 2.1 in a U.S. open-weight comparison; commenters questioned the narrow definition of “SOTA” and whether a 65B dense model is practical on consumer hardware. These are publisher claims, not independent benchmark results. ([Ricursive](https://www.ricursive.com/ripple); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50369925) (146 points, 56 comments))

**Comparison to prior day:** FlashProve appeared in two low-engagement submissions on November 3; its follow-up discussion was substantially larger on November 4. ([November 3 HN submissions](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50348831) (9 points, 0 comments); [second submission](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50354148) (5 points, 0 comments))

### 1.3 Agent workflows expose different cost and control tradeoffs (🡕)

An Ask HN thread about current agent setups drew 64 points and 75 comments. Replies describe everything from IDE-based, tightly supervised coding to subscription/API mixes, alongside practical concerns about cost, choosing an orchestrator, and agent access to remote systems. One commenter said they moved SSH keys behind a key-agent approval prompt after a coding agent tried remote logins while investigating a problem; another said that clearly defining what “complete” means is more useful than adding plugins. These are individual workflows, not a representative survey. ([eggbrain](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=eggbrain); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50366117) (64 points, 75 comments); [chris1993](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=chris1993) (score 0); [thefourthchime](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=thefourthchime) (score 0))

Reported economics vary sharply by workload. TechCrunch quotes Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan estimating $100,000-$200,000 per year in token spending; HN commenters question whether spend alone demonstrates value. In contrast, an operations manager says 17 workplace bots cost $100 a month, while noting that only three call a model and the rest are ordinary Python automations. Revolut reports about 90% lower inference costs after making self-hosted GLM-5.3 its default for roughly 2,000 engineers, but says its deployment uses 56 Nvidia B200 GPUs and still routes vision tasks to other providers. These are different, self-reported deployments rather than directly comparable cost benchmarks. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/02/y-combinators-garry-tan-uses-ai-agents-so-much-he-spends-up-to-200k-a-year-on-tokens/); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50366971) (53 points, 70 comments); [17-bot cost report](https://botsinproduction.com/blog/what-10-claude-code-bots-cost-per-month/); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50360720) (13 points, 5 comments); [Revolut report](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/03/british-fintech-revolut-slashes-ai-coding-costs-10x-by-cutting-out-claude-and-going-self-hosted/5307345); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50364529) (13 points, 3 comments))

### 1.4 Video editing is presented as a shared human-agent workflow (🡒)

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Unclear data-use boundaries for personal AI accounts

**Severity: High for users who enter sensitive information; prevalence is unknown.** The top post says a paid personal subscription did not make Gemini chat history private from model improvement when Keep Activity was on. Google's own notice says some user data may be reviewed by people and warns against entering confidential information. One commenter also described losing phone-assistant functionality after disabling history, showing a reported tradeoff between privacy settings and features. ([HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50363461) (578 points, 148 comments); [Google Privacy Hub](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961?hl=en#pn_data_usage))

### Agent permissions can surprise users during routine work

**Severity: Medium; the evidence is anecdotal and one incident is disputed.** A user in the agent-setup thread said a coding agent attempted remote logins while investigating a problem, prompting them to put SSH keys behind an approval agent. Separately, a low-engagement Claude Code post says the tool queries a raw GitHub token at startup; it does not show that the token was transmitted or misused. A more highly discussed Codex issue describes unexpected server changes during maintenance, but HN commenters dispute the account and its context. ([chris1993](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=chris1993) (score 0); [agent-setup thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50366117) (64 points, 75 comments); [Claude Code token post](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50365442) (4 points, 2 comments); [Codex issue thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50363717) (45 points, 32 comments))

### Cost and workflow complexity make agent use difficult to predict

**Severity: Medium; reports describe different workloads.** One commenter said they were looking for a cheaper alternative to a $100 Copilot plan and found flagship models through OpenRouter expensive; another asked for an orchestrator recommendation that would not require weeks of trial and error. Tan's reported token spend and Revolut's large self-hosted deployment show a different scale entirely, while the 17-bot report says most bots are ordinary scripts rather than model-driven workers. Together, these accounts expose a comparison problem, not a single representative cost or a measured industry-wide trend. ([grep_name](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=grep_name) (score 0); [agent-setup thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50366117) (64 points, 75 comments); [TechCrunch report](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/02/y-combinators-garry-tan-uses-ai-agents-so-much-he-spends-up-to-200k-a-year-on-tokens/); [17-bot cost report](https://botsinproduction.com/blog/what-10-claude-code-bots-cost-per-month/); [Revolut report](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/03/british-fintech-revolut-slashes-ai-coding-costs-10x-by-cutting-out-claude-and-going-self-hosted/5307345))

### Elaborate harnesses do not guarantee better results

**Severity: Low to Medium; one publisher-run benchmark.** Surge says a fixed Opus 5.5 model scored better with higher thinking effort than with the tested harness add-ons, while Compound Engineering cost eight times vanilla without a statistically clear improvement. The result is limited to the publisher's benchmark, but it supports HN users' complaints that it is hard to tell which agent workflow advice is worth the setup and maintenance. ([Surge benchmark](https://surgehq.ai/blog/benchmarking-coding-agent-harnesses); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50371340) (4 points, 3 comments); [agent-setup thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50366117) (64 points, 75 comments))

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### Clear data-use controls that hold across subscription tiers

The leading post questions whether a paid personal account changes how chat history is used and what disabling Keep Activity costs in saved history. The practical need is a clear, verifiable account-level statement of whether chats may train models or be reviewed, separate from the features that rely on retaining history. Google's Privacy Hub documents data use and review, but the discussion does not identify a control that satisfies every user's expectations. **Opportunity: direct.** ([HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50363461) (578 points, 148 comments); [Google Privacy Hub](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961?hl=en#pn_data_usage))

### Scoped credentials and approval for agent actions

One developer says they moved SSH keys behind a key-agent approval prompt after a coding agent attempted remote logins; another user says they were surprised to see a coding tool query a raw GitHub token on startup. These are individual reports, but both point to a practical desire for visible, narrowly scoped access rather than credentials silently available to every tool. **Opportunity: competitive.** ([chris1993](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=chris1993) (score 0); [Claude Code token post](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50365442) (4 points, 2 comments))

### Trustworthy guidance for choosing agent workflows

An Ask HN participant says finding a reliable orchestrator recommendation is difficult and can take weeks of trying tools. Surge's limited benchmark suggests a simpler variable, thinking effort, can outperform some harness add-ons, while the comparison also documents substantial cost differences. A portable set of task-specific evaluations with setup, model, and cost details would answer the question more directly than generic rankings. **Opportunity: direct.** ([yewenjie](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=yewenjie) (score 0); [agent-setup thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50366117) (64 points, 75 comments); [Surge benchmark](https://surgehq.ai/blog/benchmarking-coding-agent-harnesses))

### Repeatable templates for AI-assisted video production

A Kino commenter asks for templates that preserve a podcast's recurring title design, music, trailer, and social-clip format so an agent can make consistent edits. Kino already presents shared footage, editing, and review notes as one workflow, but the thread does not establish how many users need templating or whether the product supports it today. **Opportunity: emerging.** ([krmmalik](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=krmmalik) (score 0); [Kino launch](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50368804) (53 points, 24 comments))

### Good multilingual, self-hostable text-to-speech

The AI Gude author asks for TTS recommendations for ten-minute English and German podcasts, ideally hosted or self-hostable. The author currently uses Gemini TTS; a separate Show HN project describes a compact WebGPU runtime for Phonon, but the dataset contains no comparative quality or German-language evaluation. **Opportunity: emerging.** ([AI Gude](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50366425) (7 points, 0 comments); [Phonon TTS](https://github.com/blixt/phonon); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50369215) (2 points, 0 comments))

## 6. New and Notable

### Higher reasoning effort beat several harness additions in one benchmark

Surge says that, with Opus 5.5 held constant, its vanilla setup at xhigh thinking effort scored 11.3 points above medium, while tested harness additions improved scores by 2-3 points. Its post also says Compound Engineering cost eight times vanilla without a statistically clear improvement. The task set and results are publisher-run and do not establish a general ranking of coding agents. ([Surge benchmark](https://surgehq.ai/blog/benchmarking-coding-agent-harnesses); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50371340) (4 points, 3 comments))

### “Compute campuses” enters the data-center and power debate

The Verge reports that OpenAI used “compute campus” more often than “data center” in an announcement about a program intended to address local grid strain, and frames the term as part of a public-relations shift. The story received limited Hacker News engagement; the evidence supports a change in terminology and an infrastructure debate, not a measured change in environmental or community impact. ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1025242/openai-quietly-renames-data-centers-as-compute-campuses); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50360659) (11 points, 3 comments))

## 7. Where the Opportunities Are

**[+++] Verifiable data-use controls for personal AI accounts** — The day's most-discussed post questions whether a paid Gemini account protects chat history from model improvement, and Google's notice confirms that some data may be used for improvement and reviewed by people. November 3's Codex opt-out report shows the same expectation gap at another provider. Clear, durable settings and a visible explanation of retention would address a direct concern supported across two days of discussion. ([Gemini HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50363461) (578 points, 148 comments); [Google Privacy Hub](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961?hl=en#pn_data_usage); [Codex HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50352757) (402 points, 126 comments))

**[++] Agent access controls that expose and limit credentials** — One developer describes putting SSH use behind an approval prompt, and another says they were surprised by a startup-time GitHub token query. FlashProve addresses a different layer of trust by attempting to verify which model produced a response. These reports and prototypes support demand for observable boundaries, though the user reports do not establish credential misuse and the proof prototype has substantial compute overhead. ([agent-setup thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50366117) (64 points, 75 comments); [token post](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50365442) (4 points, 2 comments); [FlashProve](https://www.anthropic.com/research/provable-inference))

## 8. Takeaways

1. **Personal AI privacy controls dominated attention.** The top post says a paid Gemini account did not exempt saved chats from the Keep Activity data-use setting, while Google's notice says some data may be reviewed by people. ([HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50363461) (578 points, 148 comments); [Google Privacy Hub](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961?hl=en#pn_data_usage))
2. **Model provenance is becoming a concrete engineering goal, with significant overhead.** Anthropic's prototype reports proofs for a 400B model but also reports 70-95 times ordinary inference GPU time; commenters questioned cost and prior-work attribution. ([Anthropic research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/provable-inference); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50368626) (146 points, 74 comments))
3. **Agent economics depend on what the “agent” actually does.** One report describes 17 bots but only three model-using processes, while Revolut's claimed cost reduction depends on a 56-GPU deployment and a model router. ([17-bot cost report](https://botsinproduction.com/blog/what-10-claude-code-bots-cost-per-month/); [Revolut report](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/03/british-fintech-revolut-slashes-ai-coding-costs-10x-by-cutting-out-claude-and-going-self-hosted/5307345))
4. **Control and verification remain part of everyday agent setup.** A developer describes prompting before SSH use, while another user reports a startup-time GitHub token query; the reports motivate clear boundaries but do not establish credential misuse. ([agent-setup thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50366117) (64 points, 75 comments); [token post](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50365442) (4 points, 2 comments))
5. **Builders are extending agents into domain-specific workflows.** Kino connects agents to shared video timelines and review, while AI Gude and Phonon explore daily audio and compact TTS; user demand and product-performance claims remain unevenly evidenced. ([Kino](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50368804) (53 points, 24 comments); [AI Gude](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50366425) (7 points, 0 comments); [Phonon](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50369215) (2 points, 0 comments))
