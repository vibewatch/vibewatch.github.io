# HackerNews AI - 2026-11-20

## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 AI agents are moving from writing code toward hardware experiments (↑)

The day's strongest story follows an engineer using coding agents to implement and optimize LLM inference on an AWS FPGA, a domain the author says they had not worked in before. The author reports raising bit-exact throughput from 6 to 213 tokens per second over 166 prompts, a 33x improvement, while spending about $10,000 on model tokens. These are results from one author-run experiment, not an independent benchmark. ([Vibecoding an inference chip](https://ankitg.me/blog/vibecoding-inference-chip/); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50579297)) (78 points, 24 comments)

![Chart of 166 prompts and measured FPGA inference throughput rising from 6 to 213 tokens per second](https://ankitg.me/assets/fpga-prompts.png)

**Discussion insight:** [jluxenberg](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=jluxenberg) (score 0) called the reported token spend excessive for an experiment, while [datadrivenangel](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=datadrivenangel) (score 0) noted that the article did not specify the served model's type or size. [cpldcpu](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=cpldcpu) (score 0) said implementing transformers is becoming a common AI-assisted coding exercise.

**Comparison to prior day:** November 19's Onlook and Mastra Factory stories applied coding agents to visual design and issue-to-PR work. ([Onlook discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50562320)) (69 points, 61 comments); [Mastra Factory discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50548660) (69 points, 21 comments)

### 1.2 Cloud coding agents compete on responsiveness and deployment control (↑)

OpenComputer launched an open-source cloud coding agent that can run in a team's AWS, GCP, or Azure account. Its authors describe durable VMs, Git worktree-isolated sessions, and session backups that allow work to resume elsewhere. In a vendor-run benchmark, the authors report a 24-second median from Slack prompt to first push and 56 seconds to four green PRs; their chart shows 5 of 6 OpenComputer runs completed, compared with 5 of 5 for Devin. The HN post also claims that a continuously running 4-vCPU VM can be cheaper than per-session sandboxes above roughly 640 session-hours per month; that estimate is not independently verified. ([OpenComputer repository](https://github.com/diggerhq/opencomputer); [benchmark and methodology](https://opencomputer.dev/blog/cloud-agents-as-fast-as-local/); [Show HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50581403)) (28 points, 10 comments)

![Vendor benchmark chart showing median time from Slack prompt to first GitHub push, with OpenComputer at 24 seconds and Devin at 72 seconds](https://opencomputer.dev/blog-visuals/cloud-agents-as-fast-as-local/first-push-chart.png)

**Discussion insight:** [andyjtyler](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=andyjtyler) (score 0) asked whether the whole product can be self-hosted, and [sleepytimetea](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=sleepytimetea) (score 0) asked about Discord support. The questions focus on deployment scope and workflow access; they do not establish adoption.

**Comparison to prior day:** November 19's cloud-agent discussion centered on agents turning repository issues into pull requests. OpenComputer's pitch puts more emphasis on shortening interactive feedback loops and keeping the execution environment under the team's control. ([Mastra Factory discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50548660)) (69 points, 21 comments)

### 1.3 OS-level agents create new privacy boundaries for apps (→)

Apple's since-removed engineering job listing described agents that could carry out multi-step tasks across apps, browsers, command lines, code, and APIs. It is a hiring signal, not evidence that such a product has shipped. ([The Verge report](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1038227/apple-ai-agent-engineer-job-listing); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50573166)) (16 points, 11 comments)

Signal says OS-integrated agents that read screen contents can undermine an app's privacy protections. It is restricting screenshots and screen capture by Siri AI on iOS 27 and some Android assistants, but describes the mitigation as incomplete and asks Apple, Google, and Microsoft for documented app-level controls. Two HN submissions covered the same announcement: ([Signal announcement](https://signal.org/blog/protecting-your-privacy/); [The Verge report](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1038390/signal-blocking-ai-agent-screenshots-mobile-app); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50573902)) (5 points, 1 comment); ([Reuters report](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/signal-restricts-screenshots-block-ai-agents-reading-messages-2026-11-20/); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50576061)) (3 points, 2 comments)

![Signal iOS setting enabled to block Siri AI from scanning Signal content, also disabling in-app screenshots and App Switcher previews](https://signal.org/blog/images/protecting-your-privacy-1.png)

![Signal Android Screen security setting enabled to block screenshots and some AI assistants from scanning Signal content](https://signal.org/blog/images/protecting-your-privacy-2.png)

**Discussion insight:** [bemtevi](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=bemtevi) (score 0) objected to AI being compulsory in the operating system and described considering a switch to Linux. Signal's post frames a separate developer concern: apps currently lack simple controls and documentation to keep OS agents from accessing sensitive content.

**Comparison to prior day:** November 19 included Canva's new controls for disabling most generative AI features. Signal's announcement shifts the privacy question from opting out of an app's own AI to controlling what an operating-system agent can read inside another app. ([Canva coverage](https://www.engadget.com/2321318/canva-will-let-users-switch-off-most-generative-ai/); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50559947)) (5 points, 0 comments)

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Keeping OS agents out of private app data

**Severity: High for sensitive conversations; prevalence is not established.** Signal says screen-reading agents can expose message content outside the app's privacy boundary, and that screenshot blocking is incomplete. It asks operating-system vendors for documented controls that let apps opt out of AI access without losing core functionality. Two HN submissions covered the same announcement, with modest engagement. ([Signal announcement](https://signal.org/blog/protecting-your-privacy/); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50573902)) (5 points, 1 comment); [second submission](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50576061) (3 points, 2 comments)

### Safety and bystander privacy on AI glasses

**Severity: High in the reported safety-critical tests; prevalence is unknown.** The Dutch data protection authority tested six camera-glasses models. The Register reports that some produced incorrect medication guidance or claimed an emergency call had been placed when it had not; the regulator also raised concerns about recording visibility and access to footage. These are regulator-reported test findings, not evidence that every model behaves this way. ([The Register report](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/20/dutch-data-watchdog-highlights-safety-issues-with-ai-glasses/5311345); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50580461)) (4 points, 0 comments)

### Cost and reproducibility of agent-led experiments

**Severity: Medium for high-compute experiments; based on one author's account.** The FPGA experiment's author reports spending about $10,000 on Claude and OpenAI tokens. A commenter, [jluxenberg](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=jluxenberg) (score 0), said that was beyond their budget for a curiosity. The article provides a detailed measured speed sequence, but the result is still a single author's experiment rather than a repeatable comparison across projects. ([Article](https://ankitg.me/blog/vibecoding-inference-chip/); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50579297)) (78 points, 24 comments)

### Slow or opaque loading in agent-built experiences

**Severity: Low; limited to one project thread.** Users of a Claude-assisted 3D village-driving app reported a long load, a page stuck near completion, and no progress bar while waiting. [sfpk](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=sfpk) (score 0) said the pause made the app seem broken; [thelbartworks](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=thelbartworks) (score 0) said loading stalled near 90 percent. ([Show HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50574412)) (7 points, 11 comments)

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### App-level controls and documentation for OS agents

Signal asks Apple, Google, and Microsoft for well-documented ways for apps to block AI access to sensitive content across screens, files, notifications, and APIs without sacrificing core features. Its screenshot restrictions are a partial workaround, and the company says they do not cover every access path. This is a practical, urgent need for privacy-sensitive apps, but the evidence here is one app developer's request rather than broad demand. **Opportunity: direct, with limited evidence of market size.** ([Signal announcement](https://signal.org/blog/protecting-your-privacy/); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50573902)) (5 points, 1 comment); [second submission](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50576061) (3 points, 2 comments)

### Model routing that can adapt to task difficulty

An Ask HN participant has scored several models on a labeled set of about 100 tasks and wants to route each task to the least expensive or fastest model that meets a quality threshold. They say simple probes and nearest-neighbor matching did not work because task complexity can become apparent only after work starts. This is a concrete engineering question from one user, with no comments in the captured thread; existing prompt-complexity classification is mentioned but has not solved their case. **Opportunity: aspirational and low-confidence.** ([Ask HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50575992)) (3 points, 0 comments)

## 6. New and Notable

### Voice-agent setup adds simulation and continuous evaluation

ThunderPhone's Zeus configures voice-agent systems using multiple transcription and language models, then tests them on simulated calls and internal evaluations for accuracy, speed, cost, and reliability. The founder reports a 99.4% Big Bench Audio result, but the product is new and the claim is not independently verified. The Show HN post received 3 points and no comments, so this is a builder signal rather than evidence of broad adoption. ([Product announcement](https://thunderphone.com/blog/introducing-zeus); [Show HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50568763)) (3 points, 0 comments)

### AI glasses face a regulator's safety warning

The Dutch Data Protection Authority advised against buying AI camera glasses after testing six models. Reported issues included incorrect advice in safety-relevant situations as well as recording and access-control concerns; the regulator's findings, as reported by The Register, do not establish that all devices fail in the same ways. The HN submission received 4 points and no comments. ([The Register report](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/20/dutch-data-watchdog-highlights-safety-issues-with-ai-glasses/5311345); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50580461)) (4 points, 0 comments)

## 8. Takeaways

1. **Agent-assisted coding is reaching hardware work.** One experiment reports bit-exact FPGA inference improving from 6 to 213 tokens per second, but at a reported token cost of about $10,000 and without independent replication. ([Vibecoding an inference chip](https://ankitg.me/blog/vibecoding-inference-chip/); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50579297)) (78 points, 24 comments)
2. **Cloud-agent competition is about workflow design as well as model capability.** OpenComputer highlights latency, self-hosting, session continuity, and cost; its benchmark is vendor-run and only 5 of 6 reported runs completed. ([Benchmark](https://opencomputer.dev/blog/cloud-agents-as-fast-as-local/); [Show HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50581403)) (28 points, 10 comments)
3. **OS-level agent access is becoming an application privacy issue.** Signal's screenshot controls are a partial defense, and the company is asking for app-level controls and clearer platform documentation. ([Signal announcement](https://signal.org/blog/protecting-your-privacy/); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50573902)) (5 points, 1 comment)
4. **Agent trust depends on evaluation and limits, not just autonomy.** Zeus describes simulated-call evaluation, while the Dutch regulator's tests found safety concerns in AI glasses; both are early signals, with Zeus's claims self-reported and the glasses findings tied to six tested models. ([Zeus announcement](https://thunderphone.com/blog/introducing-zeus); [Dutch regulator coverage](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/20/dutch-data-watchdog-highlights-safety-issues-with-ai-glasses/5311345); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50580461)) (4 points, 0 comments)
5. **Local AI is one privacy-preserving product path, with hardware trade-offs.** LarpLess says it processes screen and camera data on-device and offers a lightweight classifier mode, while its VLM mode needs more memory and GPU resources. ([LarpLess](https://larpless.app/); [Show HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50577115)) (3 points, 2 comments)
