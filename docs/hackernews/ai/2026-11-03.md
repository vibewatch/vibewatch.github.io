# HackerNews AI - 2026-11-03

The 103 stories collected 950 points and 260 comments. The day's strongest attention centered on a report that Codex's model-training setting had been re-enabled and GitBook's disclosure of an incident involving AI agents. Other signals covered self-hosted inference economics, coding-agent harnesses, model verification, and how platforms surface AI-generated work.

## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 Opt-out settings become a trust issue (🡕)

An HN user said coworkers found Codex's "Improve the model for everyone" setting enabled again after they had disabled data sharing; the author said none of them knowingly opted back in and asked whether it was isolated. The report drew the day's most engagement, but the post and comments do not independently establish why the setting changed or how often this happens. ([user052919](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=user052919); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50352757) (402 points, 126 comments))

**Discussion insight:** [chriskanan](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=chriskanan) (score 0) reported that the setting had returned after two earlier requests to disable training. Other commenters described uncertainty around Google Gemini activity and Anthropic feedback controls, and suggested self-hosting, Bedrock, or an enterprise zero-data-retention contract as alternatives. [edot](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=edot) (score 0) suggested advanced account security as a way to lock sharing off. These are individual reports and suggestions, not independent confirmation of the original setting change.

**Comparison to prior day:** November 2's most visible AI discussions covered silent speech input and agentic QA benchmarks. On November 3, the largest discussion shifted from new interfaces and testing to control over prompts and training data. ([Subvocal](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50340088) (414 points, 239 comments); [Momentic QA](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50339913) (97 points, 66 comments))

### 1.2 An incident report links AI agents to exposed credentials (🡕)

GitBook's incident update says an attacker used AI agents and accessed some infrastructure credentials on November 2. GitBook said integrations tokens, Git Sync secrets, API tokens, and questions to GitBook Assistant may have been accessed; it invalidated customer API tokens and said it had found no evidence that content was modified. The company also said it took services offline while responding. ([GitBook incident update](https://www.gitbook.com/status/security-incident-2026-11-03); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50354336) (128 points, 29 comments))

**Discussion insight:** GitBook CTO [SamyPesse](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=SamyPesse) (score 0) said services were being restored while the team monitored the situation. Commenters welcomed the token rotation but asked for more detail about how access was gained and which infrastructure was affected. The public update instructs customers to rotate connected secrets and re-authenticate the GitBook MCP server.

**Comparison to prior day:** November 2's notable supply-chain story involved a tampered coding-assistant extension and attempted persistence. The GitBook disclosure broadened the day's security discussion to credentials connected to documentation, integrations, and agent services. ([extension analysis](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/10/30/inside-malicious-claude-code-branded-cursor-extension-persistent-ssh-backdoor/); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50332497) (8 points, 1 comment))

### 1.3 Coding-agent economics and harness quality draw scrutiny (🡕)

Octomind's open-source Rust harness drew 50 points and 34 comments. Its README describes a model-flexible agent that can run interactively, in CI, as a daemon, or through editor protocols, with policy guardrails in TOML. Its published benchmark reports 24 solved tasks out of 25, but is dated July 30, 2026; commenters asked for current model and harness versions and additional comparisons. The results are the project's own benchmark, not an independent ranking. ([Octomind repository](https://github.com/Muvon/octomind); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50352898) (50 points, 34 comments))

Revolut's infrastructure lead told The Register that self-hosted GLM models cut the company's AI inference costs by about 90% after becoming the default for roughly 2,000 engineers. The account describes 56 Nvidia B200 GPUs for GLM-5.3 and a model router that still sends vision work to other providers; this is a company-reported deployment, not a general cost benchmark. Two HN submissions covered the same switch. ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/03/british-fintech-revolut-slashes-ai-coding-costs-10x-by-cutting-out-claude-and-going-self-hosted/5307345); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50358388) (6 points, 1 comment); [second submission](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50358078) (4 points, 1 comment))

The Register also quoted Gartner analysts forecasting that open-weight models could handle 90% of routine enterprise agent steps. They estimate open models at a quarter of the cost of the cheapest closed model at the same score, or as little as one-twentieth the cost of using a frontier model for every step; they temper the latter with expected retries and verbosity, putting task-level savings nearer five or six times. This is an analyst projection, not an observed adoption rate. ([The Register on Gartner](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/03/open-models-can-do-90-of-agentic-work-at-a-twentieth-of-the-cost-of-a-frontier-llm-gartner/5307132); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50353385) (3 points, 0 comments))

**Discussion insight:** A separate harness guide recommends repo documentation, just-in-time context, validation loops, and turning recurring mistakes into guardrails. Together with the Octomind thread, the day's builder evidence emphasizes controllable workflows and current, reproducible evaluation rather than model choice alone. ([deepsense.ai harness guide](https://deepsense.ai/blog/harness-engineering-for-coding-agents-9-patterns-for-more-reliable-ai-assisted-development/); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50350673) (12 points, 1 comment))

One benchmark provides a counterpoint to broad claims for additional code context: a 12-question test across three repositories and 360 trials found no clear accuracy or cost gain from graph facts. Its author reports that a revised hook reduced tokens by about 13% at similar accuracy in a smaller follow-up. The author calls the main test small and the follow-up directional. ([Graphify benchmark](https://www.srijans.space/graphify-benchmark); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50353269) (2 points, 1 comment))

**Comparison to prior day:** The previous day's QA discussion compared how many bugs agents found and at what cost. On November 3, the discussion moved toward harness portability, benchmark freshness, and the economics of hosting models. ([Momentic QA](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50339913) (97 points, 66 comments))

### 1.4 AI disclosure is a discovery and quality-control tradeoff (🡒)

Itch.io is adding generative-AI disclosure filtering to more parts of its site. The discussion did not settle on a simple solution: one commenter argued that low-quality and scammy submissions predate generative AI, but that lower production barriers can skew the volume; they also warned that self-labeling could group legitimate AI-assisted games with low-quality work. Another commenter framed disclosure as inviting negative attention. ([HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50350730) (18 points, 19 comments))

### 1.5 Grounding and model verification target different trust gaps (🡕)

An Apify article reports that, in its test of 15 product prices, GPT-6 Luna and Claude Opus 5.5 each gave only two exact prices from memory. Built-in web search improved exact results but still produced wrong prices in every reported run. The article argues that agents need live data, checks for seller and product variant, and explicit error states; these are results from the publisher's test, not an independent evaluation. ([Apify test](https://blog.apify.com/llm-grounding-with-live-data/); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50350302) (15 points, 0 comments))

Separately, Anthropic published FlashProve, an open-source zkSNARK prototype intended to prove that a response came from a specific committed model without revealing its weights. The post reports about 110 tokens per second on one H100 for a quantized 400-billion-parameter model, a roughly 19 MB proof per 1,024-token request, and approximately 70-95 times the GPU time of ordinary inference. The two HN submissions point to the same research, which the authors describe as intermediate progress. ([Anthropic research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/provable-inference); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50348831) (9 points, 0 comments); [second submission](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50354148) (5 points, 0 comments))

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Unclear or unstable data-use controls

**Severity: High for affected users; prevalence is unknown.** The top HN post describes a setting that reportedly turned back on after being disabled, while a commenter described the same outcome after two disable requests. Other commenters discussed confusion around retention, training, and feedback controls at multiple providers. These reports do not establish that the behavior is widespread, but they show why users who send proprietary code want controls whose state and effect are easy to verify. ([HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50352757) (402 points, 126 comments))

### Agent-connected credentials increase incident impact

**Severity: High in the reported incident.** GitBook said infrastructure credentials were accessed and that integration tokens, Git Sync secrets, API tokens, and visitor questions to its assistant may have been exposed. It invalidated customer API tokens and advised rotating connected secrets. The HN discussion also asked for more detail about the access path; the incident update says a fuller postmortem was still forthcoming. ([GitBook incident update](https://www.gitbook.com/status/security-incident-2026-11-03); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50354336) (128 points, 29 comments))

### Agent benchmarks can age faster than their claims

**Severity: Medium; one substantive critique.** Octomind's benchmark reports results on 25 tasks, but a commenter noted that it used July model versions and requested current model, harness, and reasoning-effort details. Without refreshed comparisons, a benchmark may not show whether an advantage comes from the harness, model version, or configuration. ([Octomind benchmark](https://github.com/Muvon/octomind#benchmarks); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50352898) (50 points, 34 comments))

### AI labels can reduce discoverability for legitimate work

**Severity: Medium; the thread shows disagreement, not prevalence.** Itch.io's new disclosure filters address a discoverability problem, but commenters worried that a label could make legitimate AI-assisted projects indistinguishable from low-quality spam. The data shows a platform tradeoff being debated, not evidence that the filter has already produced either outcome. ([HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50350730) (18 points, 19 comments))

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### Data-use controls users can verify

The Codex post and follow-up comments show a practical need for clear, persistent settings and a way to confirm whether prompts can be used for training. Commenters pointed to self-hosting, Bedrock, and zero-data-retention contracts as alternatives, but the thread does not show which option most users prefer. **Opportunity: direct.** ([HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50352757) (402 points, 126 comments))

### Temporary, least-privilege access for agents

Privato's author proposes giving personal agents access only to the data needed for a request, for a limited time, rather than granting ongoing access to all email, messages, calendar entries, and files. GitBook's report of potentially exposed integration secrets makes this a relevant design direction, but Privato received little engagement and the submission provides no independent evaluation. **Opportunity: competitive.** ([Privato](https://github.com/roe-ai/privato); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50348836) (1 point, 0 comments); [GitBook incident update](https://www.gitbook.com/status/security-incident-2026-11-03))

### Reproducible, current evaluations of agent harnesses

Octomind commenters asked for refreshed results with current models, reasoning settings, and competing harnesses. The benchmark post makes cost and task completion visible, while the comments show that version detail is necessary to interpret them. **Opportunity: direct.** ([Octomind repository](https://github.com/Muvon/octomind); [HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50352898) (50 points, 34 comments))

### Discovery controls that distinguish disclosure from quality

The Itch.io discussion suggests a need to let users filter for AI disclosure without treating disclosure alone as a proxy for quality. The comments offer competing views and do not establish whether the new filter meets that need. **Opportunity: aspirational.** ([HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50350730) (18 points, 19 comments))

## 7. Where the Opportunities Are

**[+++] Verifiable data-use controls for AI services** — A 402-point discussion describes an unexpected settings change, and commenters report related confusion around data use at other providers. Clear, persistent controls and auditable state would address a direct concern, although the thread does not establish how widespread the reported behavior is. ([HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50352757) (402 points, 126 comments))

**[+++] Credential governance for agent-connected services** — GitBook's incident update lists tokens and secrets that may have been accessed and advises rotation, while the incident response invalidated customer API tokens. Scoped access, short-lived credentials, and clear incident communications are supported by the incident and by Privato's proposed access model. ([GitBook incident update](https://www.gitbook.com/status/security-incident-2026-11-03); [Privato](https://github.com/roe-ai/privato))

**[++] Reproducible agent evaluations and portable harnesses** — Octomind's benchmark drew substantial discussion, including requests for current model versions and fair comparisons; harness guides separately emphasize tests, context, and guardrails. The opportunity is to make quality, cost, configuration, and model versions visible together. ([Octomind](https://github.com/Muvon/octomind); [deepsense.ai harness guide](https://deepsense.ai/blog/harness-engineering-for-coding-agents-9-patterns-for-more-reliable-ai-assisted-development/))

**[++] Cost-controlled inference with explicit routing tradeoffs** — Revolut reports a 90% cost reduction after self-hosting GLM, but retains other providers for vision tasks and uses a large GPU fleet. The evidence supports a routing and deployment opportunity, not a universal case for self-hosting. ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/03/british-fintech-revolut-slashes-ai-coding-costs-10x-by-cutting-out-claude-and-going-self-hosted/5307345))

**[+] Discovery filters that distinguish AI disclosure from quality** — Itch.io's changes and the debate about honest AI-assisted work versus spam point to a platform design problem. Interest is visible, but the thread does not show which filtering approach works. ([HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50350730) (18 points, 19 comments))
