**Comparison to prior day:** October 25's Hurston-1 addressed a specialized model gap. October 26's Hob focused on the broader local assistant experience and device fit.

**Comparison to prior day:** October 25 focused on access to platforms and communities. October 26 added a concrete job-search product that uses aggregation and agent support to reduce application friction.

### 1.3 Agent collaboration makes context provenance a product requirement (🡕)

[qhoxie](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=qhoxie) shared [Swarmatory, a shared chatroom and feed for a team's coding agents](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50249449) (21 points, 0 comments). A research paper, [Compaction Laundering: Provenance Destruction in Context-Compacting Coding Agents](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50246778) (9 points, 2 comments), examined how context-compaction designs preserve message provenance. Its [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29869) reports a large difference between flattened and role-typed harnesses and points to preserving provenance in serialization as a mitigation.

**Discussion insight:** Shared agent workspaces need more than a common feed; they also need to preserve where instructions and context came from when conversation history is compacted.

**Comparison to prior day:** October 25's projects explored replay and procedural memory. October 26 sharpened the issue to the authority and provenance of remembered context.

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Local AI can be hard to match to a user's hardware

Hob promises to select a suitable open-weight model, but a commenter still asked for examples of what hardware supports which model. The number of similar desktop assistants also makes product differentiation harder. Severity: Medium. Worth building for: yes, if hardware compatibility is explained before setup. ([Hob](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50246238) (58 points, 16 comments))

### Context compression can obscure the origin of instructions

The compaction paper reports that some harness designs lose message provenance during summarization, while role-typed designs in its evaluation did not show the same result. This points to a design-level trust issue for long-running agents. Severity: High in workflows that act on sensitive files or systems. Worth building for: yes, through provenance-preserving serialization and review. ([paper discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50246778) (9 points, 2 comments))

### Discovery tools need clear first-use flows

Freehire's commenter liked the filters but found sign-in confusing. Broad data coverage does not automatically make a product easy to use. ([Freehire](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50237813) (27 points, 7 comments))

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### Private assistants that work on existing hardware

Hob represents demand for offline, on-device alternatives, while the comments ask for clear model-to-hardware guidance. Opportunity: direct.

### Job search across fragmented career sites

Freehire's job aggregation and filters respond to a practical discovery need; its CLI and agent-assisted application features extend the workflow beyond browsing. Opportunity: direct.

### Context and collaboration that preserve provenance

Swarmatory and the compaction paper point to team-level feeds and persistent context, but the paper shows that context must retain message origins and roles. Opportunity: direct for safety-sensitive agent work.

## 6. New and Notable

### Context provenance is a measurable design difference

The compaction paper compares flattened and role-typed agent harnesses and reports markedly different outcomes in its evaluation. Its implication is architectural: preserving the origin and authority of messages matters when long conversations are summarized. The report does not reproduce the paper's attack procedure. ([HN story](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50246778) (9 points, 2 comments))
