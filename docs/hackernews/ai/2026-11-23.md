# HackerNews AI - 2026-11-23

## 1. What People Are Talking About

### 1.1 AI job-loss discussion centers on agency, not only replacement (↑)

A Verge report, drawing on a Wall Street Journal profile, says Dario Amodei suggested in 2015 that immersive video games might address political strife caused by AI-related joblessness. The story was submitted twice: the larger HN thread drew 72 points and 81 comments, and the duplicate drew 16 points and 10 comments. The discussion treated the anecdote as a question about income, power, and agency, not as a current employment policy. ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1039745/dario-amodei-video-games-ai-joblessness); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50610830) (72 points, 81 comments); [duplicate HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50606447) (16 points, 10 comments))

**Discussion insight:** [snowwrestler](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=snowwrestler) (score 0) argued that people cannot simply be pacified, while [beloch](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=beloch) (score 0) questioned how income support could coexist with work that remains unpleasant. Others in the thread disputed how much AI will displace jobs. These are competing commenter views, not evidence of a settled forecast.

**Comparison to prior day:** November 22's prominent discussion focused on AI's cyber risks and the difficulty of verifying generated code. November 23 shifts the strongest conversation toward the social consequences and governance of job displacement. ([Bridgewater research](https://www.bridgewater.com/research-and-insights/current-efforts-arent-enough-to-slow-the-ai-cyber-virus); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50595940) (29 points, 8 comments); [evaluation retrospective](https://dblc.fyi/blog/a-retrospective-on-ai-evaluations/); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50598455) (5 points, 3 comments))

### 1.2 Agent capability is being assessed through safeguards and verification (→)

A hands-on comparison of Nono, Docker Sandboxes, Microsandbox, and OpenShell reports scores between 4 and 4.5 out of 5, but says none is perfect or at v1.0; the authors also identify gaps in fine-grained file and system-call controls. Its HN submission received 12 points and no comments. Separately, HAProxy's lead developer says many AI-generated security reports arrive without a reproducer or patch, while validated model findings can be useful when paired with expert review. ([Sandbox comparison](https://kudelskisecurity.com/research/sandboxing-coding-agents); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50611099) (12 points, 0 comments); [HAProxy account](https://www.haproxy.com/blog/on-the-use-of-ai-based-tools-to-find-bugs-in-open-source-software); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50608448) (4 points, 0 comments))

### 1.3 Builders package agents around concrete workflows (→)

Several low-engagement Show HN posts focus on the interface around an agent rather than a new model: Hermit CRM stores solo-business records as local Markdown files that agents can read and update; CadYam lets a person and an agent iterate on creative projects through a local MCP server; and Blobby surfaces agent status, questions, test results, and file conflicts. These are individual product pitches, not evidence of broad adoption. ([Hermit CRM](https://hermitcrm.io/); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50609708) (9 points, 2 comments); [CadYam](https://codeyam.com/apps/cadyam); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50614327) (5 points, 3 comments); [Blobby](https://blobbyapp.com); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50612033) (3 points, 2 comments))

## 2. What Frustrates People

### Agent work can spend more time being checked than produced

**Severity: Medium; prevalence is uncertain.** One developer timed an overnight run of 11 features across 14.6 hours and estimated the model was actively working 26% of the time; UI tests and simulator checks took about two thirds. The author says process changes doubled the feature count in a later run, but this is a single self-reported workflow, not a general benchmark. ([Author's timing report](https://jurajb.dev/coding-agents-mostly-waiting/); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50614258) (4 points, 0 comments))

### Generated security findings are not always actionable

**Severity: Medium for maintainers receiving such reports; prevalence is not measured.** The HAProxy lead developer describes a stream of AI-assisted reports without reproducible cases or patches, while saying that validated model findings can uncover complex bugs. The distinction in the account is between useful analysis and reports that do not give maintainers enough evidence to act. ([HAProxy account](https://www.haproxy.com/blog/on-the-use-of-ai-based-tools-to-find-bugs-in-open-source-software); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50608448) (4 points, 0 comments))

### Agent sandboxes remain immature

**Severity: Medium; evidence comes from one tool comparison.** The comparison authors found trade-offs across isolation, credential handling, usability, and maturity; they say none of the four tools had reached v1.0 and identify missing fine-grained controls. The article does not establish how frequently those gaps cause incidents in practice. ([Sandbox comparison](https://kudelskisecurity.com/research/sandboxing-coding-agents); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50611099) (12 points, 0 comments))

## 3. What People Wish Existed

### A clearer way to supervise several coding agents

The waiting-time report and Blobby address related workflow gaps: noticing when an agent needs input, seeing whether work is complete, checking tests, and spotting concurrent edits. These are practical needs described by individual builders, and Blobby is one partial product response; neither thread establishes broad demand. **Opportunity: direct, low confidence.** ([Author's timing report](https://jurajb.dev/coding-agents-mostly-waiting/); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50614258) (4 points, 0 comments); [Blobby](https://blobbyapp.com); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50612033) (3 points, 2 comments))

### Verifiable boundaries for agent actions

The sandbox comparison describes missing controls, while the HAProxy maintainer emphasizes that findings need reproducible evidence before they are useful to maintainers. An Ask HN post frames a related choice between developers checking agent-produced artifacts and more automated “feature factories,” but received only 2 points and 1 comment. Better permission boundaries and verifiers are practical needs for teams delegating work; this day's evidence does not establish a preferred design. **Opportunity: competitive, moderate evidence of a problem but not of demand.** ([Sandbox comparison](https://kudelskisecurity.com/research/sandboxing-coding-agents); [HAProxy account](https://www.haproxy.com/blog/on-the-use-of-ai-based-tools-to-find-bugs-in-open-source-software); [Ask HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50618223) (2 points, 1 comment))

### Income and agency in a changing labor market

The most discussed thread raises questions about job displacement, income support, and whether leisure is an adequate response. Commenters offer competing views rather than a concrete request or tested solution. This is a salient social concern, but the day's evidence supports an aspirational issue area, not a defined product opportunity. **Opportunity: aspirational.** ([HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50610830) (72 points, 81 comments); [snowwrestler](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=snowwrestler) (score 0); [beloch](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=beloch) (score 0))

## 6. New and Notable

### A weapons startup makes the optimization target explicit

WIRED reports that Polemos Industries, founded by 16-year-old Henrijs Dandzbergs, is developing guided missiles and describes the company's ambition as lowering “cost per kill.” The story puts an ethical question about the objectives of AI-enabled products in unusually direct terms; it does not establish that the systems are deployed or autonomous. The HN submission received 9 points and 4 comments, including an objection to YC funding the company. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/henrijs-dandzbergs-polemos-missiles-ai-warfare/); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50606849) (9 points, 4 comments); [dabinat](https://news.ycombinator.com/user?id=dabinat) (score 0))

### Cognitive offloading is discussed as a concern, not a settled finding

A founder's essay argues that over-reliance on AI could weaken critical thinking, but also notes that reduced brain connectivity in one small writing study does not directly show lower intelligence and that long-term effects remain understudied. The HN post received 2 points and 1 comment, so this is a low-engagement signal rather than evidence of a broad consensus. ([Essay](https://inklum.com/christiansafka/the-cognitive-debt-of-the-ai-generation-4q6y); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50617566) (2 points, 1 comment))
